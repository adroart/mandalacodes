## Part 1. The system, for someone who knows nothing

Human Design says you were marked twice, at the moment of your birth and once
more about three months before it, and that the marks do not change. Drawn on
a body they make nine places where a kind of energy lives, joined by
thirty-six paths, and each path has a door at each end. There are sixty-four
doors, numbered the same as the sixty-four hexagrams. The marks light some of
your doors and leave the rest dark.

A gate is one of those doors. To a person it is a theme and a pressure: a way
energy keeps moving in you, whether or not you notice, and you recognise it
the way you recognise your handwriting. Gate 3 is the pressure to give a new
thing its order. A gate is not a talent and not a fault, and nothing about it
is to be fixed.

A centre is one of the nine places, and it is the body a gate lives in. Each
does a kind of work: the head presses you to make sense of things, the belly
makes the energy for work, the throat turns things into speech and action, the
base of the trunk presses you to move. A centre is either fixed in you or open.
Fixed means it works the same way every day and is yours. Open means it takes
in that kind of energy from whoever is near, makes it louder, and lets it go
when they leave. Open is not weaker. It is where you feel other people most
and where you become wise about that energy over a life, and it is where you
mistake other people's pressure for your own.

A channel is two gates at opposite ends of one path, joined. When both are lit
the path is open end to end, the two centres it joins are switched on for
good, and a third thing exists that neither gate is alone. That is what
"defined" means: a channel, and so a centre, that runs all the time. A gate lit
on its own with the other end dark is half a path, and the system says you
are drawn to the people who carry the other half.

How much of this can a stranger use with no chart? More than the system's own
teachers usually admit, and they admit it themselves: everyone experiences
every gate's theme at some point, through the people they are near and the
planets passing over, so the dark gates are not missing, only not permanent.
That is the whole licence for the card. The card can read a gate as a pressure
the reader is inside now, because they drew it, and describe it so they
recognise it. It can say where in the body that pressure is felt and what it
drives a person to do. It can name the other half of the circuit and what the
two make together. What only makes sense with a chart is whether the pressure
is yours all the time or only visiting, whether the centre is fixed or open in
you, whether you carry the partner gate, and everything the system builds on
top of that: the five types, their strategy for acting, their authority for
deciding, the profile read from two numbers, the lines under each gate, the
planets that placed them. The card does none of that, and the page shows a
logged-in reader the one fact it can (whether they carry the partner gate)
without the prose ever claiming it.

## Part 2. What the section is for, and why it sits beside the other two

All three traditions on the card run on the same sixty-four numbers, and each
answers a different question about one energy. The I Ching says what the
situation is and what it counsels: outside you, in time. The Gene Keys say
what the energy becomes at its lowest and its highest: inside you, in height.
Human Design says where it presses in you and what it drives you to do: in
the body, in direction. A reader who has felt the situation and seen the arc
still does not know where in themselves the thing is happening or what it
makes their hands do. That is the section's question, and it is the only
section that can answer it, because it is the only tradition of the three
that put the sixty-four on a body.

Each subsection gives a reader who knows nothing one thing.

The drive gives the gate as a pressure and a theme in a person: what it keeps
making you do, what it feels like from inside, and what it gets called from
outside by people who do not carry it. This is the misnaming beat the
guideline already assigns here (section 4). Gate 3's drive is to give a new
thing its order before it is ready to be seen, and it gets called
disorganised.

Where it lives gives the centre as a felt place and its function, and then
what that place does with this one drive: the pressure it puts on you and the
trap the same place sets. For gate 3 the place is low in the belly, the engine
that answers yes or no, and the drive to shape a new thing is fed and judged
by that answer. No organ, gland or chemical; that is BODY's, and the two
bodies rule in section 4 already holds.

What completes it gives the channel: the partner gate named, what the two
make together as one working circuit, and the fact that the reader may or may
not carry the other half. Written as internal relating, two pressures inside
one body that can argue, never as a partner card (that is RELATIONS). For
gate 3 the partner is the pressure at the base to work within limits and keep
only what holds, and together they make change that lasts, in pulses, with
long still stretches between.

What the section must not do. It must not teach types, strategy, authority or
profiles: those are the reader's chart, and a card written for a stranger has
no chart. It must not say the reader has a fixed or open centre, or carries or
lacks the partner gate. It must not use the system's words as terms of art in
prose: gate, centre, channel, defined, undefined, motor, aura, sacral,
generator, and the coined names (Evolution Center, sacred flaw, inner GPS,
quantum body) stay in the books. It must not name the tissue. It must not
re-teach the situation the I Ching owns or the arc the Gene Keys own. And it
must not claim the system is true or that the reader's body works this way;
it describes a pressure so the reader can check it against themselves, which
is the same contract the Gene Keys section has.

## Part 3. Preparing the sources for sixty-four cards

The per-gate sources are already per gate and already small. For gate 3:
the gate article, 1,665 words, scraped from one commercial site, generic in
the way of that site (a "journey" every fourth paragraph, six line
descriptions copied from the standard keynotes, and one usable idea, that the
gate sees the structure inside a mess and that the danger is rushing). The
Quantum Wellness essay, about 300 words, and the strongest thing in the pile:
challenge, expression when met, expression when conditioned, a page of
prompts, and for gate 3 the single sharpest sentence in any source, that this
genius is rooted in timing as much as in originality. The channel article,
1,066 words, same site as the gate article, usable for the tension between
innovating and conserving and for the "disruptor or traditionalist, both"
misreading. Beside those three, two more per-gate sources exist and are wired
to nothing: the Modern Guide's chapter 9 carries a short entry for every gate
and every channel (five to eight lines each, 11,152 words for all 100 entries),
and for gate 3 and its channel those lines say the two things every other
source misses, that the energy moves in pulses with still stretches between,
and that the dark stretches are part of the process and not a fault. And the
centre chapters themselves carry per-gate material: Winn gives each gate a
gift line and a grapple paragraph inside its centre's chapter, and the root
chapter has a paragraph on the 3-60 channel that the sacral chapter does not.

The centre chapters are the problem the brief names. Winn's nine run 7,950

sacral chapter for gate 3 reads 8,231 words to find perhaps 900 that the card

34, 42 and 59. Nine gates, nine reads of the same chapter, is the cost the
brief is asking to remove.

### 9. Human Design

Its question: where does this energy press in me, and what does it drive me
to do. Not the situation (the I Ching has it), not what it becomes (the Gene
Keys have it). One energy located in a body and pointed in a direction.

**Assume nobody knows Human Design.** The gate, the centre and the channel are
shown as labels in the three subsection headings, and the parser reads the
names off the headings' tails. The prose never explains the system and never
uses its words as terms of art: no gate, centre, channel, defined, undefined,
open, motor, aura, chart, type, strategy, authority, profile, line, transit,
and none of the coined names from any book. "Low in the belly" does the work
of the centre's name; "the other half of this" does the work of the channel's.
The page explains the system once, behind its own tap, and the principles
reference in the vault is where a writer learns what to lean on.

**The reader has no chart.** The section is written for someone who may never
have one. So it never says the reader has this pressure all the time, has the
place fixed or open in them, carries or lacks the partner gate, or is any
type. It describes the pressure so the reader can find it in themselves now,
because they drew this card, the way the Gene Keys Shadow is written as
recognition and never as a verdict. The page shows a logged-in reader whether
they carry the partner gate, from their own chart, beside the third
subsection; the prose therefore never claims it either way, and a sentence
that would be false for a reader who lacks the partner is rewritten.

**What the section owns.** The misnaming by others: what a person carrying
this drive gets called by people who do not carry it. This is DESIGN's beat
and KEYS' is the mishandling by self (section 4); neither uses the retired
stem. The section also owns the partner gate as internal relating: two
pressures in one body and what they make together. The partner card as a card
is RELATIONS'.

**Subsections and lengths.** The drive, 120 to 180 words: what this energy is
as a pressure and a theme in a person, from experience, opening on the body
or the situation, never on "Gate N is the gate of." It says what the pressure
keeps making you do, what it feels like from inside, and what it gets called
from outside. Where it lives, 120 to 180: the place as a felt region and its
function in one or two sentences a stranger could repeat, then what that place
does with this one drive: how it feeds it, the pressure it puts on you, and
the trap the same place sets. Not the centre in general; the centre reference
holds that, and the card says only what this drive needs from it. What
completes it, 120 to 180: the partner gate as the other pressure in the
circuit, what the two make together, and how it feels when the two are both
at work in you. For the four gates that sit in three channels (10, 20, 34,
57) the card treats the channel its frontmatter names and does not list the
others. Under 500 words for the section.

**Read with five questions, for the gate.** Before a sentence is written,
answer from the packet and nothing else: what this pressure is; what it does
in a life; what it presses you to do; what it feels like, from inside and as
named from outside; what it is not, which is usually the neighbouring gate in
the same centre or the thing it gets mistaken for. The section is done only
when it answers all five without the packet open. Then find the thread word:
read the gate essay's challenge, the gate's lines in the centre chapter, and
the channel's short entry, and see what they share. On gate 3 it is timing.
That word appears once per subsection in its plainest form and is not a
refrain.

**Voice.** The ten rules and the two-minute check. To you throughout,
including the channel paragraph ("the other half of this in you," never "the
two of you"). One picture per subsection, and the picture is drawn from the
body or the work, never from the scene that ICHING owns; a glimpse of the
scene is CODE's once and not this section's. The measured checks apply here
as everywhere: under 15 words a sentence, under three commas per hundred
words, no sentence with three commas. This section has been the deck's
longest-sentenced, and card 3's DESIGN measures 23.4 words a sentence with
six three-comma sentences today.

**How it hands to Body.** The centre paragraph names the region, the pressure
and the function, and stops. It never names an organ, gland, chemical or
tissue, and it never says what is happening in the tissue. When the region
and the organ coincide (belly and navel on 3, throat and thyroid on 23 and
33) DESIGN keeps the sensation and BODY keeps the mechanism, and the reader
who swipes from one to the other goes from feeling to fact. The last
sentence of the section may lean toward the body ("what that costs the body
is the next thing") but names nothing in it.

**What it must not say.** No type, no strategy for acting, no authority for
deciding, no profile, no lines under the gate, no planets, and no "seventy
percent of people." No claim that the system is true or that the reader's
body is built this way. No re-teaching of the situation or the arc. No
sentence that also stands in ICHING, KEYS or BODY on the same card, and no
sentence that would still be true under the ring sibling's name or the
partner gate's name (the one-minute test, section 4).

**Retired here.** "The misread is to call this" (40 cards). "The whole
teaching" (27). The Alone-then-Joined turn closing the channel paragraph
(52): "alone, this code is X; joined, it becomes Y." "Gate N is the Gate of."
"The mutation," "the channel," "the centre" and their siblings as the subject
of a sentence in prose. "This is where" as an announcement. "What ruins it
is" and the "what makes this X is" family.

**The sources.** One packet per gate, assembled by script from the gate
article, the gate essay, the channel article, the partner's essay, the
Modern Guide's entries, the centre chapter's lines on this gate, the centre
reference and the principles reference (`todo/plans/writing-guideline/human-design-guidance.md`,
Part 3). The gate essay and the centre chapter's gate lines outrank the
scraped articles when they disagree; the articles contribute the misnaming
and the innovate-versus-conserve tension and nothing that is not also in a
book. Assimilate the packet whole, then write with it closed.

**Names.** The gate keyword, the centre name and the channel name are kept
lineage, shown in the headings. They come from the card's frontmatter
(`human_design:`), never inferred.

## Part 5. Card 3 as the test

Does the current DESIGN section answer the five questions for gate 3 from the
sacral chapter, the gate article and the gate essay? Read against those three
and the channel article, the Modern Guide's entries and the root chapter's
paragraph, in the form of the Gene Keys audit.

What it carries. The drive carries the pressure to give a raw new thing its
own order rather than a tidy one (the article's structure-inside-chaos, made
concrete), the pull to be heard before it is time and that the pull is panic
(the essay's conditioned expression, nearly whole), the restlessness with
patience inside it (the essay's quantum expression), and the misnaming
(disorganised, flaky, a starter who never finishes; the article's
"misunderstood," the root chapter's "not designed to finish everything you
start"). Its child image is Winn's own ("it takes time before a child can be
productive in society"), carried close enough to read as hers. Where it lives
carries the yes and the no that rise before thought, form given from below
and not from the head, the excitement, jump-in, plateau cycle and the
question of finished-or-paused, and burnout from a start with no yes behind
it; all of that is the sacral chapter, and it is the right material. What
completes it carries the partner as the pressure from lower down that keeps
only what works, the tension between trying the next arrangement and
refusing to keep what does not serve, "disruptor to some and traditionalist
to others and both," and the timing close (the thing was rooting the whole
time). That is the channel article and the root chapter, faithfully.

What it has lost. The channel's one fact every source but the card agrees
on: this energy moves in pulses, long still stretches and then a leap, and
the still stretches, which can feel like a dark place, are part of the
process and not a fault. Nothing in the section says the drive is
intermittent, and a reader in the still stretch would read this section as
describing someone else. The essay's sharpest line, that the genius is in
the timing as much as in the originality, is present only as its shadow
(panic) and never as the gift. Winn's discouragement and self-doubt during
the waiting, which is her gate 3 grapple, is implied by "panic" and never
described as the low, slow thing it is. The partner gate's own theme,
working within limits and under pressure to bring something into form, is
narrowed to "keeps only what works," which is conservation without the
limit. And the open-centre reader is lost entirely: "Where it lives" is
written as if every reader has the engine fixed and the yes and the no
available, which the sacral chapter says is seven readers in ten. The
sentence "the question the engine puts to you is whether you are truly done
with this thing or only paused before the next level of it, which it cannot
tell you" also reverses the source: Winn's point is that the response can
tell you, if it is asked.

What it has added. "Half idea and half object," "the tidy one that would
look finished from outside," the framing of a building with no walls, "six
projects and no floor under any of them" (a list), "the hundredth
arrangement" against "ninety-nine of them were rubbish," and "the two of
you argue inside one body," none of which is in a source. Some of it is
good writing; the building and the hundredth arrangement are two pictures in
a section that should carry one. "The seed under the crust" is the I Ching's
sprout, a scene glimpse this section does not own (section 4 gives DESIGN
answer, not callback). "That plateau is the difficulty, exactly" reaches for
the hexagram's title. "This is where the seed under the crust is felt" and
"What ruins it is deciding from above" are two of the announcing shapes the
two-minute check strikes (its lines 5 and 7). "The argument is the mutation"
puts a system word in prose as the subject. "Both is correct" is a fragment
verdict. And the closer, "turns out to have been rooting the whole time," is
the same construction as the Gene Keys Shadow's "it had been holding you the
whole time," two sections apart on one card.

The five questions, scored. What it is: answered (the pressure to order a
new thing before it is ready). What it does: half answered (the making and
the being misnamed, but not the pulse and the still stretch). What it
presses you to do: answered (shape it, wait, meet it with the whole of your
energy). What it feels like: answered from inside and outside, but only for
a reader with the engine fixed. What it is not: not answered; the section
never separates this gate from its neighbour in the same place (gate 42,
finishing what was started, is the obvious foil, and the root chapter hands
it over in one line: look for someone with the gate of finishing). No thread
word is carried; "timing" is the one the sources share and it appears once,
in the drive.

The numbers, measured today with the punctuation script: DESIGN runs 23.4
words a sentence, 5.9 commas per hundred words, six sentences with three or
more commas, against the section 3 limits of 15, 3 and none. It is the
longest-sentenced section on the card after RELATIONS, and the guideline's
own rule says a section can pass the numbers and still read as chopped; this
one fails the numbers and reads as dense, which is the other failure.

Verdict, in the Gene Keys audit's terms: the section is faithful to the
sacral chapter and the channel article and has read them well. It has not
read the Modern Guide's two entries or the root chapter's channel paragraph,
because nothing wired them in, and the two facts those carry (the pulse, the
limit) are the two facts a stranger could most use. It claims the reader's
chart in its second subsection. It has two pictures where it needs one, and
its sentences are half again too long. It is not rewritten here.

## Part 6. How the section relates to the reader

Added 2026-09-12 at Adrian's request, before any writing: "I'm not quite
sure people are gonna know how to relate to the section. They're getting a
reading. The I Ching and Gene Keys are kind of a reading of the situation and
how to relate to it. Now this one is something quite a bit different." And
the two aims: to learn Human Design through this, and to help the reader
embody the wisdom of the card better. A second opinion, given independently
of the coordinator's framing (situation as the frame, instruction closing
each subsection), then set against it.

**What the section is to someone getting a reading.** The I Ching and the
Gene Keys read a moment: a situation outside you and an arc inside you, both
of them things that pass. Human Design is the one tradition of the three that
describes a permanent piece of a person, and that is the mismatch Adrian is
feeling, because a drawn card is about now and a gate is about always. The
honest bridge is the system's own claim that everyone meets every gate's
theme through the people near them and the passing year: with this card
drawn, this pressure is running in you the way it runs all the time in the
people who were born with it. So the section is that wiring on loan, for the
length of the reading, and what the reader gets is what the people built for
this pressure have had to learn about it: where it sits, what it makes the
hands do, what it needs to run and what starves it. That is why it is the
most embodiable panel on the card, and why it must never say the reader
carries it.

**Four ways the section could relate to the reader.**

Borrowed wiring. The reader is told this pressure is visiting them the way
it lives permanently in some people, and gets how those people have to handle
it. The risk is that it reads as being about someone else, and the reader
stops feeling for it in themselves.

Operating conditions. The reader is told what this energy needs to run (a
yes from below, a limit to work within, a still stretch it is allowed) and
what starves it, so they can give it those things today. The risk is a
rulebook, and strategy and authority coming in by the back door in plain
clothes.

The locator. The reader is told where in the body to put their attention and
what response to wait for there, so the wisdom of the other panels has a
place to land. The risk is a body-scan instruction that collides with BODY
and turns the panel into a wellness exercise.

The argument. The reader is given the gate and its partner as two pressures
in one body that disagree, and recognises the disagreement as their own. The
risk is narrowness: it carries the channel well and the drive and the centre
badly, and it re-teaches the Gene Keys' two natures in other clothes.

**Recommendation.** Frame the section as borrowed wiring, which is the
coordinator's option 4 said honestly: the drawn card puts this pressure in
you now, and the section describes it from inside a body that has it. Inside
that frame, spend the operating conditions once, in Where it lives, because
that is the paragraph that owns what the place does with the drive; let The
drive end on recognition and What completes it end on the tension,
unresolved. Two disagreements with the coordinator's framing. First, closing
every subsection with instruction gives three coaching endings on every
card, which is the retired "so the work is to" closer three times over and a
formula the two-minute check would strike, and it is where the reader gets
