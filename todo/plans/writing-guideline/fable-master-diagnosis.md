# What is going wrong with the writing, from Adrian's words

Written 2026-09-14 by Fable as template master, from the 147 messages in `adrian-said.md` read twice, the two briefs, card 1 as written this morning, the reading Adrian called much better, card 3, and the packet for card 1. Only his words are evidence here. The new brief at `oracle/WRITERS-BRIEF.md` is built from this document.

## What he wants the writing to be

Take his messages in order and one thing is said in every register he has: the writing gives a person something true about their life, in words chosen so well that the simplicity is the poetry. "Simple can be one of the most poetic if you pick your words correctly." "This should feel like a beautiful piece of literature that every culture can understand." "This is sending a message to people about their life, and it wants to have a moment where people really feel what is being shared with them." "Powerful is the way that it speaks to someone and speaks to their heart and really connects with something that is true, rather than trying to be big and powerful." He said what it is not, too: "It doesn't need to be boring. It just needs to be able to be read by all cultures and understandable. That has nothing to do with the interest level."

What that literature is made of, he has also said. It is the wisdom. "The reading is the wisdom." "There's a lot of nuggets of truth and wisdom in this. There's massive references, especially in the Gene Keys." Not only there: "not only Gene Keys, also the other sources." The traditions are treasures carried into the present, never taught as systems: "the treasures of the past being brought into assimilation of the present"; "assume nobody knows anything"; "the goal is for someone to understand it and assimilate it, not to read it as a system"; "I wanna make sure it doesn't read as a history book." Old phrases are kept and completed rather than replaced: "we don't have to change it majorly, just add connecting words and make the sentences more complete."

Its centre is the energy in balance. "The bigger focus is on how you hold the energy when it is in balance." "We're looking at what this energy is, not what it is when you're having a hard time with it." The card is real rather than positive: "The card doesn't have to be positive. We're looking for it to be real." The challenges are honoured and never made into a formula: "the steps to get there need to be a part and need to be honored"; "not formulaically saying this is your challenge, here's your solution"; "Some people may not be drawing the card in the challenged state"; "It's just a small note that should subtly guide." It never accuses: "avoid all the writings saying you failed, you this, you that."

Across the page, nuance. "Each one of them should have their strengths and reveal something a little bit deeper, instead of feeling like you're reading the same thing." "If everyone is just talking about the creative power, what about the other aspects that go to this? Is it only about the creative power?" "I worry if we only focus on one thing per paragraph. Everything's gonna talk about the most obvious thing and the nuances are gonna be left alone."

Then the sentence itself. Whole, in proper English, enjoyed by someone who loves the language: "the sentences do not read well for people who know English well. Even though they fit functionally as a sentence, I would say they're not proper sentences and definitely not literary inspiring." One thought a sentence, absorbed: "one sentence, one concept, to help it be absorbed"; never chopped: "you just added periods, ten sentences in the first paragraph is horrible." Entered, never joined in the middle: "There is no entry into the readings, kinda like when someone starts a conversation in the middle." Named, never hinted at: "It doesn't really tell me what this one's about. Rises on its own. Are you talking about making bread?" No riddles: "This becomes more of a riddle once again." No pronouncements, no filler: "Most of these words say nothing"; "mastering the art of saying a lot without saying actually anything." No examples that assign the reader a life: "Are you implying that some people have and some people haven't? And what do you mean anyone who's started a family?"; "Why does it say you mean it every time you start a piece of work, cook for people? It feels like a crappy sentence." No monotonous words, above all in an opening: "Instead of saying is up in you, something like is alive in you is much better. I do not want the monotonous, indescript words, especially for an opening sentence." The rule that arrived last, which overrides anything earlier: "One thing I don't want: to start sentences with and, or have two and's in a sentence."

His own sentences are the best evidence, because he wrote them after rejecting ours. "Creation itself moves through you, the power that makes something from nothing. This energy comes in waves, and between the waves there is nothing. No matter how hard you try, the wave will only return on its own." Look at what these do. The first names the thing in its opening words and says what it is before the full stop. The second gives one picture, whole, and uses it at once. The third carries a fact from the source (creativity cannot be controlled; when it is absent there is nothing to do but wait) with no source in sight, in a sentence a reader could test against their own life. Each sentence is one thought and each is finished. He had written a fourth, "And every time it returns it is the beginning of something new," and cut it. It began with And; it added nothing the first three had not already made true. That cut is the craft: the sentence was pleasant, so it went. When the writer then produced something else, he said: "you did not adjust to what I wrote you, you just had your own thing happen again. I want you to see what I wrote versus what you have and understand why I like what I wrote better." That comparison was never made properly. This document makes it.

## What is going wrong

The reading on card 1 written this morning, from the current brief by a fresh writer, has three faults. The earlier cards carry the same three in different clothes.

The first is that it is speech written down, not prose. "It is the push in you to make something that was not there before, and to make it your own way, and it keeps no schedule." Three thoughts roped together with and, a nondescript noun (push) where the energy needed its name, a colloquial phrase (keeps no schedule) standing where an image should be. From the same paragraph: "While it is in, you are all the way in: the days have colour, the work gets you out of bed, and stopping is not something you could do if you tried." That is how a person talks across a table. It is not how a person writes a sentence meant to be read twice. Adrian never asked for talk. He asked for literature.

The second is that the sentences do not carry the wisdom. "It comes when it comes." "Then one morning it is there again, and what it carries in is the start of something new." "It looks like nothing is happening, and something is." Each is a shape with nothing inside, while the sources beside them are full. Creativity cannot be controlled, so when it is absent there is nothing to do but wait. The low comes on suddenly; left alone, it passes as suddenly. The worst enemy in it is a mind asking what is wrong. Hunting the reason fixes the low in place. The numbness is the kindling. Nothing new is made without hundreds of mistakes. An idea moves one way, then another, and has to be let. You are the midwife, not the author. The writer had all of this in the packet. The card says "It comes when it comes." This is the fault Adrian named as "saying a lot without saying actually anything" and "wasting the reader's time without giving the nuggets of truth or the wisdom." It matters most, because everything else is finish.

The third is that the reading is not centred where he asked. Its first paragraph names the energy as "the energy that makes things," which is too small to be its name, then spends its remaining sentences on the wave going in and out. Its third paragraph is entirely the flat stretch. He had said, an hour before: "You're so focused on the wave coming in and out. You've missed the whole aspect. We're looking at what this energy is, not what it is when you're having a hard time with it." Inside that paragraph sit a picture that must be decoded ("an idea held too tightly goes out like a small fire under a closed hand") and a sentence that edges toward the verdict he forbade ("Asking what is wrong with you is what turns a flat week into a flat year"). The earlier cards did the same with other material: the first card 1 Siddhi ("Up in the sky with nothing under it this energy is beauty"), the aphorisms ("It is the oldest thing in you." "Held well, it is light."), the essence that named the energy as "the wanting," the reading that opened "What begins things in you rises on its own" with no thing named. The wave itself is not the error; it is his own picture. The error is treating the rhythm as the whole of what the energy is.

## Why the templates produced it

Two briefs have been used. Each was built against the other's faults.

The rulebook brief of 2026-09-12 answered his early complaints (too many adjectives, too many commas, run-ons, ten sentences in a paragraph) with measurement: fifteen words a sentence, three commas a hundred words, forty retired closers, an eighteen point check. A writer under it defends rather than writes. It produced chopped prose, then riddles, because a riddle is what remains when a writer has been told everything not to say and shown nothing to say. The aphorisms, the eleven-word Siddhi and the frozen fragments came from there. What it got right was structure: six voices with one question each, the scene governing the card, the sheet, the parser.

The brief of 2026-09-13, which I wrote, saw the chopping, saw the riddles, and corrected them with one device: say every sentence out loud to a tired person across a table, keep the connecting words in, keep the commas where you would breathe, do not tidy a spoken sentence. It also carried "And every time it returns" as a model after Adrian had cut it, defending the And as "the breath before the last sentence." That device is the direct cause of this morning's reading. Told to talk, the writer talked: "and… and… and," "it comes when it comes," "all the way in," "keeps no schedule." The brief mistook plainness for the whole of the register. Adrian's register is plain, yet it is written: composed, chosen, one finished thought per sentence, none opening the way he has forbidden. That brief also warned against aphorism and pronouncement, correctly, but offered chat as the only alternative, so the writer swung from riddle to filler. Two things in it worked and are kept. The list of true things from every source, allotted across the six sections, is why the reading he called much better existed at all; writing the reading from all the sources rather than from the essence alone is why it had nuggets in it. The rest was exhortation, which produced voice with nothing inside.

Both briefs share a deeper fault. Each told the writer what sentences must not do and what they should feel like. Neither showed a finished paragraph in the register with the reason it works, sentence by sentence; neither took a failed sentence and repaired it in view. Adrian asked for exactly that, twice: "tell me why I might not like it and what's going on"; "can you tell me why and we could figure out at the roots how not to do this in the future." An Opus writer changes what it writes when shown the move, not when told the rule. The new brief is built on showing.

One more cause, which is not the templates. The reading he liked was written with every source open. The reading he disliked was written from the brief, the packet and the essence in one pass by a fresh agent with no reader on the page. Card 3, the one he approved, was written section by section with him reading each pass. No template replaces the reader. A template can put the sources between the writer and the page, require the list of true things before a sentence is written, then show the register instead of describing it. That is the closest a writer alone can get to him.

## What follows

The new brief at `oracle/WRITERS-BRIEF.md` is shorter than either predecessor and is written to teach the register by being read. It carries Adrian's three sentences with his cut as the model, takes this morning's sentence apart to repair it in view, keeps the list of true things with the all-sources reading as the two things a writer does before writing, and puts the never-dos inside the craft rather than in a list at the end. Card 1's reading has been rewritten from the packet under it, so the template and its result can be read together.
