# Do the trigrams map to the body? What is real tradition and what is modern

Research question for the writing guideline: is "the elements of the trigrams inside your body" a real traditional Chinese idea, or a modern invention dressed as one. Short answer up front: both things are true at once, and they are different claims. The trigram-to-body-part mapping is genuinely ancient, textually attested, and about 2,300 years old. The trigram-to-internal-organ mapping is a real and traditional idea too, but it comes from a different, later layer of Chinese thought (Five Phase medicine, matured by the Song and Ming dynasties), and popular sources disagree with each other about the details because eight trigrams do not divide evenly into five phases. Anything wrapping this in Human Design, Gene Keys, or acupoint "essences" is modern, dating from the 1970s onward, and should never be called tradition.

## 1. The classical answer: Shuogua, the body parts

The Shuogua (說卦, "Discussion of the Trigrams"), one of the Ten Wings appended to the Yijing, is traditionally dated to around the third century BCE (Warring States period), reaching something close to its received form by the Han dynasty. It is the source of nearly every trigram correspondence used today: family roles, animals, directions, seasons, and body parts.

The body-part passage (commonly numbered section 9 in the Wilhelm/Baynes chapter division, section 10 in some others) reads, in the received Chinese text:

乾為首，坤為腹，震為足，巽為股，坎為耳，離為目，艮為手，兌為口。

A public-domain style translation (Wilhelm/Baynes lineage, found via the Ten Wings page at yijing.website) renders the passage:

"The Creative manifests itself in the head, the Receptive in the belly, the Arousing in the foot, the Gentle in the thighs, the Abysmal in the ear, the Clinging in the eye, Keeping Still in the hand, the Joyous in the mouth."

followed immediately by a gloss on why: "The head governs the entire body. The belly serves for storing up. The foot steps on the ground and moves; the hand holds fast. The thighs under their covering branch downward; the mouth in plain sight opens upward. The ear is hollow outside; the eye is hollow inside. All these are pairs of opposites corresponding with the trigrams."

An independent modern scholarly translation (cited from "The Yijing: A Guide," found via web search) gives the same eight assignments in different wording: "Zhen is the foot; Sun is the thigh. Kan is the ear; Li is the eye. Gen is the hand; Dui is the mouth." The two translations agree exactly on all eight assignments, which is good confirmation this is a stable, well-attested passage, not a translator's invention.

The eight assignments, as a table:

| Trigram | Chinese | Element (later) | Body part | Chinese |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qian (Heaven) | 乾 | Metal | head | 首 shǒu |
| Kun (Earth) | 坤 | Earth | belly | 腹 fù |
| Zhen (Thunder) | 震 | Wood | foot | 足 zú |
| Xun (Wind) | 巽 | Wood | thigh(s) | 股 gǔ |
| Kan (Water) | 坎 | Water | ear | 耳 ěr |
| Li (Fire) | 離 | Fire | eye | 目 mù |
| Gen (Mountain) | 艮 | Earth | hand | 手 shǒu |
| Dui (Lake) | 兌 | Metal | mouth | 口 kǒu |

Note the wordplay risk: head (首 shǒu) and hand (手 shǒu) are different characters with the same pronunciation. A writer transliterating loosely could accidentally swap them; always keep the hanzi next to the gloss.

A separate scholarly analysis found in the search (Academia.edu, "Reconstructing the Symbols of the Trigrams and Hexagrams") argues these eight assignments were not arbitrary: the position of the yin or yang line within the trigram maps structurally to where the body part sits. Gen and Dui, whose distinctive line sits on top, get upper-body parts (hand, mouth); Zhen and Xun, whose distinctive line sits on the bottom, get lower-body parts (foot, thigh). That is a real structural reading of the text, not folklore, and it is worth knowing that the Shuogua correspondences were seen even by ancient commentators as having an internal logic, not just an arbitrary list.

This is genuinely traditional. It is old, textually documented, translated consistently by multiple independent scholars, and it predates Chinese medicine's mature organ theory by centuries.

## 2. The medical answer: Five Phases, trigrams, and internal organs

This is a different and later idea: mapping trigrams to internal organs (zang-fu 臟腑) via the Five Phases (Wu Xing 五行: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water), which is the real engine of Chinese medicine's organ theory. This layer matured later than the Shuogua body-part passage, developed across the Han through Song and Ming dynasties as Five Phase cosmology fused with medicine, and reached one of its most explicit statements in Zhang Jiebin's (張介賓, 1563-1640) Leijing (類經, "Categories of the Classic," 1624), a massive Ming-dynasty reorganization of the Huangdi Neijing. Zhang Jiebin is one of the historical figures most associated with the phrase "yi yi tong yuan" (醫易同源), "medicine and the Yijing share one origin" - the explicit traditional claim that Yijing cosmology and medical theory are one system. Web search confirms Zhang Jiebin and the Leijing as real, well-documented historical medical texts, though I could not pull a specific hexagram-by-hexagram diagnostic passage from primary sources in this pass; treat the Leijing as the real historical anchor for "the Yijing and Chinese medicine are one tradition" without over-claiming a specific hexagram-diagnosis system from it.

The mechanism: the eight trigrams each carry a Five Phase element (this part is Shuogua-adjacent and old); the Five Phases each govern one yin organ and one paired yang organ (this part is core Chinese medicine, from the Huangdi Neijing's Five Phase correspondence system, well before Zhang Jiebin). Chain the two together and you get trigram-to-organ. Because there are eight trigrams and only five phases, two phases (Wood and Earth) and one phase (Metal) each have to be split or shared across more than one trigram, and this is exactly where popular sources disagree:

| Trigram | Element | Organ pair usually given | Where sources disagree |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qian (Heaven) | Metal | Lung / Large Intestine | agreement |
| Dui (Lake) | Metal | Lung / Large Intestine (some: just "joy/mouth", no organ) | Qian and Dui share Metal; some sources give Dui no organ at all |
| Li (Fire) | Fire | Heart / Small Intestine | agreement |
| Zhen (Thunder) | Wood | Liver / Gallbladder | shares Wood with Xun |
| Xun (Wind) | Wood | Liver / Gallbladder | shares Wood with Zhen; some sources differentiate Xun as "gallbladder" specifically vs Zhen as "liver" specifically |
| Kun (Earth) | Earth | Spleen / Stomach | shares Earth with Gen |
| Gen (Mountain) | Earth | Stomach / Spleen (reversed order from Kun in some sources) | shares Earth with Kun; some give Gen "stomach" as primary, Kun "spleen" as primary, others reverse it |
| Kan (Water) | Water | Kidney / Bladder | agreement |

This table (element and organ-pair columns) matches what a generalist acupuncture-clinic web source presented, and independently matches the meridian codes found in Adrian's own AcuPresence hexagram data (see section 3): hexagram 1, both trigrams Qian, meridian codes given as GV, LU, LI (Governor Vessel, Lung, Large Intestine) with element Metal - exactly the standard Qian-Metal-Lung/LargeIntestine chain. That internal consistency across an independent modern source and Adrian's own vault data is reassuring that this Five Phase chain is the standard version practitioners actually use, not a one-off invented mapping.

The vault's own back-matter.md (Benebell Wen's "I Ching, The Oracle" appendix) footnotes this literature directly, citing "Yang Li, Book of Changes and Traditional Chinese Medicine" (Beijing Science and Technology Press) three separate times, and citing 邹学熹/邹成永, 中國醫易學 ("Chinese Medicine-Yi Studies," Sichuan Science and Technology Press, 1989) as a source for the trigram/Wu Xing correspondence work. This confirms there is a real, named, published body of Chinese-language scholarship specifically on "Yi Jing and Chinese medicine" as its own subfield, which is worth knowing even though I did not read those two books directly.

Where this section's reliability genuinely drops: the redundant assignments (which of Zhen/Xun gets liver vs gallbladder specifically; which of Kun/Gen gets spleen vs stomach specifically) are not settled the same way in every source, because the Shuogua and the Five Phase system were never designed to run on the same eight-way division as the five phases; the joining is a later interpretive overlay, useful and traditional in spirit, but not a single canonical table handed down whole.

## 4. Hexagram-level (not just trigram-level) body mappings found

Two are genuinely classical and belong to the oldest layer of the text itself: Hexagram 31 (Xian) and Hexagram 52 (Gen), both using an ascending body-part sequence across their six lines (see section 3). These are the strongest, most defensible "the I Ching maps to your body" claims available, because they are in the core text, not a later commentary layer, and multiple independent translators (Legge, Cleary x2, Deng) preserve the same body-part sequence.

Beyond those two, the Adrian deck's own **AcuPresence** source material (found at `/Users/adrianrasmussen/Documents/Files/2 Areas/Laser/Oracle Cards/Adrian deck/Universal Langauge/Source Material/`) is a full 64-hexagram, hexagram-by-hexagram body mapping, but it is entirely modern. AcuPresence is a proprietary system by Alaya DeNoyelles (AcuPresence.com), explicitly self-described in its own handbook as "a synthesis of Classical Acupuncture, the I Ching, and the universal principle of vibration," combined further with Human Design and Gene Keys. The extracted data (`acupresence_data.json`, built from a 36MB PDF, "AcuPresence 64 Hexagrams Extended.pdf", via `extract_acupresence.py` using Claude Opus) gives each hexagram: its upper and lower trigram's element/nature/familial-role/meridian/quality (built correctly on the standard Five Phase chain described in section 2 - e.g. Hexagram 1, trigram Qian, meridian GV/LU/LI, element Metal, matches the standard Qian-Metal-Lung/LargeIntestine assignment), plus six invented "AcuPresence acupoints" (Source, Physiology, Core Stability, Vesica, Spirit, Window of Heaven), each with a specific real acupuncture point code (e.g. Hexagram 1's Physiology point is Liver 14, LV.14), an "invocation" ("At Merchant Yang, a strong boost of vitality inspires me...") and an "essence." The acupoint locations and codes appear to be real acupuncture points; the assignment of which point belongs to which hexagram, and the invocation language, is AcuPresence's own modern invention. This is usable and Adrian evidently already invested in extracting it, but it must be labeled as a modern proprietary synthesis (AcuPresence, 2010s-2020s) if used, never as ancient Chinese medicine.

One more modern hexagram-body mapping surfaced: the Schonberger scan on the Desktop (`/Users/adrianrasmussen/Desktop/I Ching/The I Ching & the Genetic Code - Schonberger, Martin/`, containing `DNAiChing.htm` and associated images) is Martin Schönberger's 1973 book (German original "Verborgener Schlüssel zum Leben," English "The Hidden Key to Life," 1979, ASI Publishers), which maps the 64 hexagrams to the 64 codons of the DNA genetic code - a numerological structural parallel (64 = 4^3 codons = 2^6 hexagram lines), not a body-part or organ correspondence. Table of contents chapters include "The Genetic Code," "The Code of the I Ching," and "Combination of the Genetic Code and the I Ching in a Single Table." This is a real, influential book (it is one of the sources that seeded the later Gene Keys / Human Design "hexagram equals biology" framing), but it is squarely a 1970s numerological synthesis, not a traditional Chinese claim, and it is about DNA codons, not organs.

## 5. Honest verdict

A writer can say, with a straight face and as real tradition: the Shuogua (an appendix to the Yijing roughly 2,300 years old) explicitly assigns each of the eight trigrams to a part of the body - Qian the head, Kun the belly, Zhen the foot, Xun the thigh, Kan the ear, Li the eye, Gen the hand, Dui the mouth - and two hexagrams in the oldest core of the text itself, Hexagram 31 and Hexagram 52, run an ascending body-part sequence line by line from toe to jaw. A writer can also say, as real tradition though from a later and less uniform layer, that Chinese medicine links the eight trigrams to the body's organs through the Five Phases, and that "the Yijing and medicine share one origin" (yi yi tong yuan) is an actual traditional claim associated with Ming-dynasty physicians such as Zhang Jiebin. What must be labeled modern synthesis, never tradition: any specific organ assigned to a specific trigram beyond the broad Five Phase families (because eight trigrams do not divide evenly into five organ-pairs and popular sources disagree on the details), Gene Keys' one-organ-per-hexagram "physiology" list, Human Design's body centers, AcuPresence's hexagram-by-hexagram acupoint invocations, and Schonberger's hexagram-to-DNA-codon correspondence - all are twentieth or twenty-first century inventions layered on top of real trigram language, useful as design material but false if presented as what the ancient Chinese believed. What to avoid outright: implying that any single, tidy "trigram equals one organ" table is what Chinese tradition itself hands down whole - it never did; the tidy single-organ tables in circulation today are modern compressions of a messier, contested older overlay.
