## Part 1. What the organ and the amino acid are, and where they come from

Every Gene Keys chapter opens with a header of five facts, and two of them
are the whole of this section's source: a physiology and an amino acid. For
key 3 they read Navel and Leucine. Nothing else in the chapter or the 64 Ways
essay says anything about the navel, and the only bodily sentence in either
is the essay's line that the fear of losing what we hold is felt as a deep
discomfort in the stomach, the belly, which is the Gene Keys' own place for
the Shadow and not a fact about an organ. So the section's source is two
words per card, and everything the current cards say beyond them is the
writer's own.

Where the two words come from is now settled from Rudd's own site. The
physiology is "an area of focus for contemplation," and the anatomy
associations were "inspired by the Human Design body graph placements,"
which were "intuited or given" to Ra Uru Hu. The 64-row table confirms it:
the organs are the nine Human Design centres spelled out (the sacral plexus
seven times, the solar plexus six, the throat and thyroid eight, the pineal
three, the adrenals three) with a few finer placements dropped in (navel,
sternum, diaphragm, colon, body hair, inner ear, gall bladder). Card 3 is one
of those finer placements, and it sits inside the sacral region that Human
Design already gave it. The organ is not a traditional-medicine fact and not a
biological one; it is a location intuited in 1987 and adopted into the Gene
Keys in 2009. The trigram research in this folder already reached the same
verdict for the organ tables generally: real tradition assigns body parts to
the eight trigrams, and nobody before the twentieth century assigned one
organ to one hexagram.

The amino acid comes from a different place and is half real. The genetic
code has 64 three-letter words and 20 amino acids plus a stop, so most amino
acids are spelled several ways: leucine, serine and arginine six ways each,
five others four ways, isoleucine three, stop three, nine of them twice, and
tryptophan and methionine once. Rudd's 22 codon rings are exactly that table.
Count the amino acids across his 64 keys and the numbers match the genetic
code's spelling-count amino acid for amino acid, which is a real fact about
biology that the deck can state without blushing. What is not biology is
which hexagram gets which codon. That is Martin Schonberger's 1973
transcription (the yin-yang pairs read as the four bases), a numerological
correspondence built on the coincidence that both systems have 64 members
because both are built from a four-way choice made three times. Rudd says as
much in his introduction: a "mathematical similarity," a "new holistic
language," and then the claim that the book "talks directly to your DNA."
His site is plainer: the amino acid "does not mean taking more of that
physical supplement, but rather to use it as a focus for your contemplation,"
and "Gene Keys Ltd does not provide medical advice."

So for someone who knows nothing: the organ is where a modern system places
this energy in the body, chosen by intuition and worth checking against your
own; the amino acid is a real building block of the body, one of twenty, and
the reason this card carries this one is a correspondence somebody drew fifty
years ago between the shape of the hexagram and the shape of a codon. The
first is a place to look. The second is a real thing to learn about, hung on
the card by a coincidence of counting.

## Part 2. What a reader receives from Body, and what discovering it deeper means

The other five sections describe the energy as it is lived. Body is the only
section that hands the reader a fact they can check outside the card. That is
what it gives and no other section can: a literal thing in their own body,
doing a literal job, that the rest of the card has been circling. The reader
who has felt the situation, seen the arc, and located the pressure gets, at
the end, something that is simply true whether or not any of the systems
are, and it lands because it is unlike everything before it. This is the
section's relation to the reader: not a further reading but a floor. The
Human Design plan called that panel wiring on loan; this one is the wall you
can put your hand on.

Discovering the organ deeper, in practice. The organ is not the region (Human
Design's "Where it lives" owns that, and on card 3 the new Design paragraph
already places the engine between the navel and the legs), so Body cannot say
where to feel. What it can say is what the tissue is doing while the energy
is felt, and that is the thing a reader can do something with: notice it
happening. On card 3 the navel is a closed scar where the first supply came
through and was cut, and the wall around it is the first muscle that
tightens under fear and the first to soften when the fear is felt through. A
reader can put a hand there and find that out for themselves inside a minute.
On the thyroid cards it is the pace of the whole body set at the base of the
neck and a held throat felt from across a room. On the adrenal cards it is
the surge and its cost. The practice the section supports is attention to a
mechanism, not a meditation on a region, and the honest support for it is
that the mechanisms are real: a scar, a gland, a muscle wall, a pulse.

Discovering the amino acid deeper, in practice. Here the reader can learn
something and act on it, within limits. Every amino acid has four plain facts
a stranger can hold: whether the body makes it or must eat it (nine are
essential, leucine among them), what it builds or signals (leucine is the
signal a cell reads as "enough has arrived, start building," the one that
sets muscle repair going), where it comes from in food (cheese, fish, beans,
pumpkin seeds, and every whole protein), and one thing that is true of it
alone (leucine is spelled six ways in the code, as many as any letter gets).
A reader can take the first three into a kitchen. The fourth is the one the
card is for, because it is the fact that rhymes with the card without being
made to. The relation Adrian is asking after, how the organ and the amino
acid play together, is real on card 3 (a beginning made under a cut supply,
and the letter that says begin building, spelled every way so a fault in one
copy cannot stop the start) and is not guaranteed on every card. Where it is
not there, the section does not manufacture it; it gives the organ's fact and
the amino acid's fact and lets them sit a paragraph apart.

What must not be claimed. That the energy is in the organ, or that the
organ is the card's "seat" as a fact about the body; it is a place the system
chose. That the amino acid is this card's chemistry, or that contemplating
the card does anything to it, or that eating more of it serves the card; the
section may say what the amino acid does and where it is found, as fact, and
never as advice. That the hexagram-to-codon correspondence is biology; it is
a modern correspondence and the card never explains it, so it never has to
claim it. That any of this is ancient: no "the old maps," "the old yogas,"
"the old teaching" (all three appear on cards 3, 23 and 42 today), because
the organ table is from 1987 and the codon table from 1973. And nothing about
illness, supplements or healing, which is where the Quantum Wellness book
goes ("potential illnesses: sexual diseases, exhaustion, breast cancer" for
the sacral) and where the card does not follow.

## Part 3. The one-time preparation for sixty-four cards

The amino acid reference is worth making once and is small. There are twenty
amino acids, plus the stop (three cards: 12, 33, 56) and methionine as the
start signal (card 41), so twenty-two entries of about 100 words each, one
file, in the card's voice rules with a `sources:` list. Each entry carries the
four facts above (made or eaten, what it builds or signals, where it comes
from in food, one thing true of it alone) and one line on how many ways the
code spells it, since that is the ring size. Drawn from a clinical
encyclopedia page per amino acid (the Rochester and Cleveland Clinic pages
that came up for leucine are the right register) and the standard codon
table for the spelling counts; nothing in the Gene Keys is needed for it,
and the codon ring pages on Rudd's site are not used, because their content
is the rings' "karmic" meaning, which the deck moved to Relations today as a
family fact. The ring rule stays: siblings share the entry, so the card
states the shared fact in one clause and writes its paragraph from its own
scene. Today cards 3, 23 and 42 all carry "the signal that tells a cell to
begin building" and "the body cannot make it," which is the one-minute test
failing three times on one amino acid; the reference does not fix that, the
rule does, but the reference makes it visible because every writer reads the
same 100 words and knows what the siblings have already spent.

The organ reference is worth making once and is medium. The 64 physiology
labels reduce to about 35 distinct organs, glands and regions, and most of
the repeats are Human Design centres restated (sacral plexus, solar plexus,
throat and thyroid, pineal, adrenals, pituitary, liver, lymph, immune system,
perineum). One file, one entry per organ of about 120 words: what it is, what
it does, one thing it does while a person feels something (the thyroid sets
the pace; the adrenals fire before thought; the diaphragm is the breath's
floor), and the plain anatomy a writer would otherwise invent. Drawn from a
standard anatomy reference, not from any of the three systems, and checked
by a second pass, because invented anatomy is the section's standing
failure: card 3's navel paragraph is right about the cut cord and the
ligaments but adds a lung fraction nobody measured and a sentence about cells
that is Rudd's ring passage dressed as biology. Where the label is a Human
Design centre by another name, the entry says so, so the writer knows the
region is already spent in Design and writes only the mechanism.

The per-card packet is then five things: the chapter's two words (already in
the card's frontmatter under `body:`), the organ entry, the amino acid entry,
the finished KEYS section of the same card (section 10 says Body is written
after KEYS, having read it), and the finished DESIGN section, so the writer
can see what region and what pressure are already on the card and not repeat
them. About 1,500 words. It is the smallest packet on the card and the one
that most needs the two neighbouring sections in it, because Body's fault is
never too little source; it is a second description of the belly.

Who does what. The amino acid file is a Sonnet job checked once against the
clinical pages, about twenty-two short entries and an afternoon; Adrian reads
it once for voice, not for chemistry. The organ file is a Sonnet draft and an
Opus check for anatomy, since the failure mode is confident invention.
Fable writes each card's section from its packet. One-time cost: two files,
perhaps three runs each with the check, six runs, and no Fable run until the
cards. The sixty-four writes are the deck pass and are not counted here.

### 10. Body

Its question: what is my body doing while I feel this. Not where it presses
(Human Design has it), not what it becomes (the Gene Keys have it). One
literal thing in the body and one literal building block, and what each is
doing.

**The frame.** Body is the floor. Every other section describes the energy
as it is lived; this one hands the reader a fact they can check outside the
card, in their own body, and it lands because it is true whether or not any
of the systems are. It is spoken as a layer of the oracle, never as a biology
explainer, and never as a healer. The deepest inward point of the card,
written after KEYS and after DESIGN, having read both.

**Assume nobody knows any of it.** The organ and the amino acid are named in
the two subsection headings and as labels. The prose never explains where
the assignments came from, never names a codon, a ring, a chart, a centre or
a system, and never says "this code is seated in." It says what the organ is
and what it is doing, and what the amino acid is and what it does, to you,
in words a stranger could repeat to a friend.

**The two subsections, each giving one thing.**

- Physiology, 120 to 180 words: the organ or gland, what it is in plain
  words, and what it is doing while this energy is felt. Open on the thing
  itself, never on the region ("put a hand below the navel" is Design's
  gesture now). One mechanism, told as something happening in you right
  now, that a reader could notice inside a minute. When the organ is a
  Human Design centre by another name (the sacral plexus, the solar plexus,
  the throat and thyroid) the region is already spent in Design, and this
  paragraph writes only the tissue and what it does.
- Amino acid, 100 to 150 words: the one amino acid, what it is (made by the
  body or eaten), what it builds or signals, where it comes from, and the one
  thing true of it alone. Facts a reader could take into a kitchen, stated as
  facts and never as advice. Then, when the card's scene and the fact rhyme
  on their own, one sentence that lets them; when they do not, the paragraph
  ends on the fact. Under 330 words for the section.

**Read with five questions, for the organ and again for the amino acid.**
Before a sentence is written, answer from the two reference entries and the
card's KEYS and DESIGN: what it is; what it does; what it is doing while
this energy is felt (for the amino acid: what it signals); what a reader
could notice or use; what it is not, which for the organ is the region and
for the amino acid is the ring's meaning. The section is done only when it
answers all five for both without the references open.

**The ring rule.** Ring siblings share the amino acid (leucine on 3, 20, 23,
24, 27, 42). The shared facts (made or eaten, what it signals) are stated in
one clause at most, and the paragraph is written from this card's scene; run
the one-minute test against the two nearest siblings before anything else.
Today three leucine cards carry the same two facts as their lead, which is
the fault this rule exists for.

**Voice.** The ten rules and the two-minute check. To you throughout ("put a
hand to your throat and swallow" on card 33 is the model register). One
picture per paragraph, and the picture is the mechanism itself, never a
second picture of the scene. The measured checks apply here as everywhere:
under 15 words a sentence, under three commas per hundred words, no sentence
with three commas, no list of nouns. Card 3's Body measures 21 words a
sentence with three list sentences today.

**What it must not do.** Describe the region a second time; name the centre;
say the energy is in the organ or that the organ is its seat; claim the
amino acid is the card's chemistry, or that contemplating the card affects
it, or that eating more of it serves the card; mention supplements, illness
or healing; call any of it old ("the old maps," "the old yogas," "the old
teaching" are retired here with the rest); explain or defend the
hexagram-to-codon correspondence; invent anatomy (every mechanism traces to
the organ entry or is cut); and end by stepping back to say what it meant.

**Retired here.** "Bodily floor" (32 cards). "There is a teaching in that"
(39). "The code runs all the way down to this" (45). "Deeper than the organ."
"The old teaching puts," "the old maps tie," "the old yogas watched." "The
shadow has a bodily floor here too." "The climb out is also bodily." "The
letter this code is written in" and "the letter under this code" as the
amino acid's introduction on every card. "One real fact about it" as a
required beat: a paragraph is not one fact plus a moral.

**The shape is optional.** The three beats (organ, the shadow's floor, the
climb out) and the two beats (fact, teaching) are retired as required
shapes. A section that runs organ-then-floor-then-climb on all 64 cards is a
formula with fresh words.

**The reference.** Two files in the vault, made once: the amino acid entries
(twenty-two, one per ring) and the organ entries (about thirty-five), each
with a `sources:` list, drawn from clinical and anatomical references and
not from any of the three systems. The packet per card is the two frontmatter
words, the two entries, and the card's finished KEYS and DESIGN.
`todo/plans/writing-guideline/body-guidance.md` holds the reasoning.

**Names.** The organ and the amino acid are the chapter's two words, kept as
labels from the card's frontmatter (`body:`), never inferred. The codon ring
is a family and lives in RELATIONS.

## Part 5. Card 3's Body, audited against the two words and the findings

The source is Navel and Leucine. Read against that, the 64 Ways essay's one
belly line, the ring passage in the chapter, the leucine facts, and the new
Design section beside it.

What it carries. Physiology carries the navel as a fact rather than a
metaphor: the cut cord, the vessels sealing on their own, the bands of tissue
that run up the inside of the belly wall, all of which is real anatomy (the
umbilical vein and arteries become ligaments). It carries the one thing a
reader can check, that the wall around the scar tightens first under fear and
softens when the fear is felt through, which is the 64 Ways essay's belly
line made mechanical. The amino acid paragraph carries the four facts almost
whole: leucine is the letter read as permission to build, it is spelled six
ways, that is as many as any letter gets, and five other codes share it. The
join between organ and amino acid is present and earned (a start made the
hard way; the instruction written every way so a fault cannot stop it), and
the closer, that the body never did wait to begin correctly, is this card's
sentence and would be false on 23 or 42.

What it has lost. The essential fact (the body cannot make leucine; it comes
from food) is absent, and it is the one a reader can act on; the two
siblings both carry it and this card does not. Where it is found is absent.
What it is doing while the energy is felt is answered for the organ and not
for the amino acid, which is described as a signal in general. The first
sentence opens on "a scar in the middle of you where a beginning was once
made the hard way," a picture, where the rule now asks for the thing first.

What it has added. "A person can run for years on a quarter of their lungs"
is not a measurement anyone made. "The old yogas watched this long before
anyone measured it" announces significance and calls a 1987 placement old;
it is the retired family. "A cell, likewise, only starts to cooperate with
what surrounds it once it stops bracing to survive" is the chapter's ring
passage (cells integrating into one body) dressed as a fact about cells, and
it is not one. "The mark of that sits exactly where this code lives" states
the seat as a body fact. The second paragraph is a description of the belly
wall, the ring of muscle at the base of the trunk, the breath climbing and
dropping, which is now, sentence for sentence, the territory Design's "Where
it lives" occupies (the engine low in the body, the lift and the flatness
felt there), so the reader who swipes from Design to Body meets the middle
of the body twice. The amino acid paragraph's "five other codes carry this
letter and hear it from their own place in the story" is a maker's-voice
aside about the deck.

The five questions, scored. Organ: what it is, answered; what it does,
answered; what it is doing while this is felt, answered and then repeated as
region; what a reader could notice, answered ("whether the wall around your
navel is soft this week is worth knowing"); what it is not, not answered,
and the paragraph drifts into the region. Amino acid: what it is, half
(signal, not essential or eaten); what it does, answered; what it signals
while this is felt, answered by the join; what a reader could use, not
answered; what it is not, not answered, and "hear it from their own place in
the story" reaches toward the ring's meaning that now belongs to Relations.

The numbers, measured today with the punctuation script: 21.0 words a
sentence, 6.0 commas per hundred words, three sentences with three or more
commas, three list sentences, against limits of 15, 3 and none.

Verdict, in the Gene Keys audit's terms: the section has the right two
facts and the right join, and it is the only Body on the three leucine cards
whose last sentence is its own. It fails on three counts the rules now name:
it describes the region a second time after Design, it adds anatomy nobody
measured and biology that is a metaphor, and it calls the placement old. It
drops the one amino acid fact a reader could act on. Its sentences are half
again too long. It is not rewritten here.
