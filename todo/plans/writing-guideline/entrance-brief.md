## The rule (from oracle/WRITERS-BRIEF.md, section "The entrance")

The entrance is the first prose a person reads, under the keynotes. It is a taste of the card, not the card: two sentences, three at most, that leave someone wanting the rest. Write it from the finished card alone, with the reading (the three paragraphs under `## CODE`) open beside it.

The reading's first paragraph names the energy and says what it is. The entrance therefore does not; the one job it may not do is define. It comes in from somewhere else on the card: a moment, a picture, a feeling in the body, the thing people say about you, the turn the card makes. It borrows no phrase from any section, no image the reading spends (if the reading tells the rainmaker, the entrance does not mention rain), and no opening word the reading opens with. Read the two together before you finish: if the entrance could be the reading's first sentence, or the reading's first sentence could be the entrance, start again.

It still says what this energy is, in the reader's life, so a stranger holding only the card name and these sentences would understand what the card is about. Speak to you. Plain, chosen words every culture uses. Never a sentence beginning with "and", never two "and"s in one sentence, never an em dash, at most two examples. No system word (hexagram, gate, centre, shadow, gift). The difficult side may be a clause, never the subject; the card is about what the energy is and how it is held.

## Method per card

1. Read the whole card once (every section, so the entrance can come from anywhere on it).
2. Read the current entrance beside the reading's first paragraph and name what they share.
3. Choose the angle the reading does not use. Write the entrance.
4. Check: no shared phrase of three words or more with any section; no shared image; not a definition; two or three sentences; the "and" rules; the reader in it.
5. Replace the line.
