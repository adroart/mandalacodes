# The Fable voice pass: how a card is written so a person feels it

Written 2026-09-13 after reading card 3 (the one Adrian approved on the page), cards 1 and 2 (written from the rulebook and failed on the page), the writer's brief and the guideline, the outside research, the Codex review, and the three Gene Key 1 sources. This answers Adrian's question from this morning: how do you write a card so that a person anywhere feels what is being shared, with words chosen, simple and poetic, powerful by being true and not by being big. It ends with three passages from card 1 rewritten, and each rewrite says what changed and why. The new writer's brief at `oracle/WRITERS-BRIEF.md` is this document turned into a template.

## What went wrong

The guideline grew to fifteen thousand words and the brief to five and a half thousand, and nearly all of it is prohibition and measurement. Under fifteen words a sentence. Under three commas per hundred words. Forty retired closers. An eighteen point check. A writer working against that does not write. They defend. Every sentence is built to pass the tests, and a sentence built to pass tests has nobody in it. The tell is in card 1's Siddhi: "Up in the sky with nothing under it this energy is beauty." The comma after "it" was dropped to keep the count down, so the sentence cannot be parsed at first hearing. "Beauty is what a life becomes when the making has gone all the way through" was a whole thought from the source (give yourself to what you make so completely that it changes you) compressed into eleven words to stay under the gate, and what is left is a riddle. The writer knew what they meant. The reader has to guess.

Card 3 is good where it is good for a different reason. Adrian read every pass on the page and said "that's hard to understand" and "too many adjectives, too many commas" and "you just added periods". The voice on that card is not the rules. It is the corrections of one reader who had to understand it. The rules were then written from the corrections, and the next writer applied the rules without the reader. That is the whole fault. A rule cannot stand in for the person the sentence is for.

## The craft

### Say it to one person before you write it

Every sentence on a card is something you would say out loud to one person sitting across from you. They are living this energy right now. They have never heard of the I Ching or the Gene Keys or Human Design and they are not going to. They are tired, and they are listening, and you have one chance to be understood. If you would not say the sentence to them, it is not a card sentence. If they would say "what?", it is not finished.

This one idea carries almost everything the rulebook tried to enforce. You speak in plain words because you are speaking. You keep the connecting words in, and, so, because, when, because speech has them. You put the commas where you would breathe. You do not say "this card" because you are not reading them a card. You do not say where it came from because you are not citing. You do not use a system word because they do not know the system. You give one picture and then say what it meant, because that is what you do when someone's face tells you they did not follow. None of that needs a rule once the person is in the room.

But you cannot say it until you understand it. Read the sources until you could shut the book and tell a friend what this energy is at its lowest and its highest, in your own words, without a single phrase from the page. If you cannot say it simply you do not have it yet, and a sentence you cannot put into other words means nothing. The riddle in card 1's Siddhi is what it looks like when a writer reaches the page before they have reached the understanding. Assimilate first. Then speak. Then write down what you said and choose the words.

### Who the person is

The person across the table is someone able to hold this energy, and that is who you speak to. Adrian, this morning: "I don't want to assume someone is negative. The goal is to allow them to discover the challenges through embodying the strengths, but it's not that formulaic. I just want to avoid all the writings saying you failed, you this, you that. The bigger focus is on how you hold the energy when it is in balance." So the centre of gravity of every section is what it is to carry this energy well, and the difficult face is found from inside that, in whatever way the card's own material makes natural. Sometimes the true thing is hard and the card says it. What the card never does is accuse. "You are numb" is a verdict. "Numbness is a feeling, though almost nobody knows they are in it" is something a person can recognise and still be standing when they have read it.

### How the sentence is made

The plain thing first. Say what it is in the words you would use across the table. "The days go flat." "You have more strength in you than the moment needs." Then, if it helps, one picture, and then let the next sentence say what the picture was for. The plain sentence governs and the picture serves it. When the picture is doing the meaning on its own, the reader is decoding, and a reader who is decoding is not feeling.

The subject of the sentence is something that can be seen or felt, and the verb is what that thing really does. Days go flat. Colour drains out of the work. A hand rests on a chest and finds not much there. When the subject is "this energy" or "beauty" or "the making" and the verb is "is" or "becomes", nothing appears in the reader's mind, however true the sentence is. Card 3's Siddhi puts a person in the first sentence, someone who has been through everything and opened again, and the reader sees them. Card 1's puts an abstraction in a sky, and the reader sees nothing.

Put the weight at the end. The last word of a sentence is the one that stays, so the sentence should land on the word that matters. "You have lost what you held more than once and are still delighted by what arrives" lands on arrives. Build the sentence so the voice rises to its last word and stops.

Let the sentence be as long as the thought and no longer. A whole thought sometimes needs twenty words and its commas. A fronted phrase takes a comma or the reader trips. A pause in the voice is a comma on the page. The comma gate was written against lists of nouns ("the work, the house, the people at your table, the body you wake in") and it was right about those. It was never meant to take the breath out of a sentence, and that is what it did.

### How the picture is chosen so every culture sees it

A picture works across cultures when the reader has touched the thing with their own hands. The body: a hand, a chest, breath, eyes, the back of the neck. Weather and light: morning, first light, a grey week, rain that has not fallen, the dark. Fire, water, ground. A child at play, an old person. Sleep and waking. A door, a road, a table, food. A seed under packed ground. These are the pictures the I Ching itself reaches for, and it has travelled three thousand years on them.

A picture fails across cultures when it is an idiom ("drag the strength back up by the collar"), a local object, or a system's furniture (the dragon, the trigram, the gate) used to carry meaning it has not earned in plain words on this page. The dragon belongs to the I Ching section, where it is given as itself. Everywhere else the card says rise, or comes up, or the light comes back, and a person in Lagos or Lima or Oslo sees the same thing.

One picture per height, at three temperatures, and it should be the picture the source actually uses. Gene Key 1's source is explicit: the Gift is rooted in light, the Siddhi is light become beauty, and the Shadow is the colour and the energy draining out. So the picture is colour and light. The colour goes out of the days. The light comes back all at once and something new comes out of you. Everything you look at is lit from inside. No sky needed.

### How the sentence lands in the body

A sentence lands in the body when it names a sensation the reader can check right now, or a state they have been in and never named. "Put a hand on your chest and notice how little is there." "You get to the end of a day tired and cannot say what the day was." Those two are already on card 1 and they are the best sentences on it. They land because they are specific about an ordinary thing. The reader stops for a second, because that is true of them and nobody had said it.

That stop is the moment Adrian is asking for. It cannot be manufactured by size. It comes from exactness about something small. Every section should have one sentence that does this, and the writer finds it by asking what this energy is actually like on a Tuesday, in a kitchen, in a body, and saying that.

### How you know a sentence is alive rather than functional

Three tests, and all three are done with the ear. Read the sentence aloud and ask what appeared. If something appeared, a face, a hand, a flat afternoon, the sentence is alive. If nothing appeared and you had to think about what it meant, it is functional: grammatically complete and empty. Then ask whether you can say the sentence in other words. If you cannot, it does not mean anything yet. Then ask whether the next sentence needs this one. A living sentence makes the next one necessary. A functional sentence can be removed and the paragraph does not notice.

Card 1's "Quiet holds it" fails all three. Nothing appears, it cannot be said another way because the writer does not know what "it" is, and the sentence after it does not need it. It was planted to satisfy the short sentence rule.

### What chosen words means in practice

A chosen word is the one plain word that carries the exact weight, picked from among the plain ones after the sentence has been said. Not happy, not glad, but delighted, because there is surprise in delighted and the source insists on surprise. Not new but fresh, because fresh has aliveness in it and new does not. Not rest but quiet, because the numbness needs quiet more than it needs sleep. The choosing happens on the second pass, never the first. On the first pass you say it. On the second you go word by word and ask whether this is the word or the first word that came.

Choosing is mostly cutting. The second adjective goes. The word that was put in to sound like an oracle goes. The sentence that says what the paragraph meant goes, because the paragraph already meant it. What is left is fewer words, each one doing work, and that is what "less words but more potent" looks like on the page. It is not compression. Compression makes riddles. It is subtraction, and what is subtracted is everything the person across the table did not need.

### How simplicity becomes poetry

Poetry is not in the ornament. It is in the recognition. A simple sentence becomes poetry when it says something true that the reader had felt and never put into words, and says it in words they could have used themselves. "Something in you has begun, and it does not yet have ground to stand on." There is nothing decorative in that sentence and it is the best line on card 3. The reader feels it because it is true of them, and because the rhythm carries it: a rise, a turn on "and", a landing on "stand on".

So the work is not to make sentences beautiful. It is to find the true thing and get out of its way. Then to say it in the order the mind takes it in, the plain thing before the picture, the feeling before the reason. Then to choose the words so each one is the right one. Then to read it aloud and let the ear cut what the ear does not want. What survives that is simple, and it is poetry, and it did not try to be.

## Three passages from card 1

### The CODE opening paragraph

Before:

"A force that begins things is up in you. You want to make and to begin and the wanting has no strain in it. People feel the freshness when you come into a room and come nearer without being asked. It moves the way the sky moves, all day and without effort, because nothing in it is being pushed."

After:

"There is a force in you that starts things. It rises on its own, and while it is up there is no strain in it. You want to make something, and the wanting is enough to carry you, and what you make is new. It is the oldest thing in you and it is fresh every time it comes."

What changed. "Is up in you" was an odd placement and the paragraph opened on it; now the force simply starts things and rises, which is the card's verb. "You want to make and to begin and the wanting" was an and-chain built to dodge a comma; now the thought has its commas. The sentence about people feeling the freshness when you walk into a room belonged to the Gift and is gone from here. "It moves the way the sky moves" had an "it" with two possible owners and contradicted the essence, which says this force surges and goes under; now there is no sky and the paragraph says only what is true in balance. The last sentence is the one the reader stops on, and it is made of six ordinary words: oldest, fresh, every time.

### The I Ching Reading's opening

Before:

"The force that begins things is up in you and it is not going to stop. Whatever you have been putting off wants doing now. The wanting is strong enough to carry you if you let it. This is the part of the cycle where everything begins. What you start here will have to be grown and gathered and tested before the year is out. All of that is in front of you at once."

After:

"You have more strength in you than the moment needs. It came up on its own and it is still coming. Nothing stands in your way, and that is the whole of the situation: a strength that could go all the way up, and no one to tell you when to stop. What you begin now can last a long time. The question is how to carry a force this size without burning through it."

What changed. The old opening repeated the CODE's first sentence almost word for word, and then repeated the Judgement's four stage sequence (grown, gathered, tested), which the Judgement bullets already carry two screens down. "Whatever you have been putting off wants doing now" is not in any source; it made the hexagram about procrastination. The new opening states the situation as a person lives it: more strength than the moment needs, nothing in the way, no one to say stop, and the danger that comes with that, which is Heaven's own risk of burning through. "A strength that could go all the way up" is the dragon in plain clothes, without the word. The last sentence names what the Reading will answer.

### The Siddhi

Before:

"Up in the sky with nothing under it this energy is beauty. Beauty is what a life becomes when the making has gone all the way through. Nothing stands between you and what is there. A face, a field at dawn, a low grey week: each one shines with being exactly itself and all of it comes from one place. Quiet holds it. It is so fine that one word said about it can crack it, so you say less and see more.

This is what the freshness becomes when even the one who was fresh is let go of. There is no maker now. Whatever is made comes through you and goes on out and it is the more beautiful for nobody claiming it. Even the flat season is beautiful from here. Nothing about you needs showing and the ordinary is the whole of it. It is morning, and the dew is still on everything."

After:

"At the height of this energy, everything you look at is beautiful. A face, a field at first light, a flat grey week: each one shines with being exactly what it is, and it is all one light. This is what happens when you give yourself to what you make so completely that it changes you. There is nothing left between you and what you see. It needs quiet, the same quiet you learned in the flat days, because a beauty this fine can be cracked by one word said about it. So you say less, and you see more.

This is what freshness becomes when you stop being the one it happens to. There is no maker now. Whatever is made comes through you and goes out, and it is the more beautiful for nobody claiming it. Even the flat days are beautiful from here. It cannot be taught, only lived. You are lit the way a morning is, and anyone who wants to can come and stand in it."

What changed. The first sentence now says the plain thing: everything you look at is beautiful. A person can feel that. Then the picture, the face and the field and the grey week, and this time the list is there because the source insists that the dull week counts as much as the dawn. "The making has gone all the way through" is now what it always meant: you give yourself to what you make so completely that it changes you. "Quiet holds it" is gone and the quiet is tied to the flat days of the Shadow, so the thread runs through all three heights. "The one who was fresh is let go of" became "you stop being the one it happens to", which is the same letting go said as a person would say it. The ending no longer fits card 2's Unity unchanged: it names something present and particular to this card, a person lit like a morning, and the source's one hard fact about this height, that it cannot be taught. The dew is gone because it was the source's own image and the sentence could close any Siddhi in the deck.

## What this means for the template

The new brief is short and it is written the way the cards should be written, so reading it teaches the register. It keeps everything a writer cannot do without and puts the numbers where they belong, in the checker, as information a second pass reads and not as a gate the first pass writes against. The one idea it turns on is the person across the table. Every other rule in the old brief is either a consequence of that idea or a thing a script can count afterwards.
