# mandalacodes.com — Article Cluster Plan

Last updated: 2026-05-28

The editorial backbone of mandalacodes.com. Articles are the primary mechanism for SEO authority, AI citation, and substantive value-add.

## Editorial Voice Rules

Every article on mandalacodes follows the same voice conventions:

- **Editorial, not promotional.** Articles are *about* mandalas, not selling mandalas.
- **First-person plural ("we" as the editorial voice) or third-person.** Adrian's first-person singular ("I") essays live on adrianrasmussen.com, not here.
- **When Adrian's perspective appears, it is named.** "As Adrian Rasmussen, the artist editor of this site, observed in his studio…" — perspective is contextualized, not hidden.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or "to" for ranges.
- **No "wall art" anywhere.**
- **Citations are real.** Every historical or scholarly claim cites a verifiable source. Footnote convention: inline `[1]`, references list at bottom.
- **Length target**: 1,500 to 2,500 words per article. Long enough to be substantive, short enough to be read.
- **Images**: minimum 3 per article, each with descriptive alt text. Cloudinary canonical URLs.

These are the foundation. Publish in this order — each one's authority compounds for the next.

### Article 1 — "What Is Mandala Art?"

**Route**: `/articles/what-is-mandala-art`

**Target keywords (primary)**: `mandala art` (49,500/mo), `what is mandala art`, `original mandala art`

**Target keywords (supporting)**: `mandala meaning`, `mandala symbolism`, `mandala definition`, `history of mandala art`

**Intent**: Definitional. The reader does not know what a mandala is, or knows the word vaguely and wants depth. This article must outclass the current SERP — Wikipedia, Invaluable.com, and World History Encyclopedia are the existing top results.

**Structure recommendation**:

1. **Opening**: 1-2 paragraphs defining mandala as a geometric symbol of wholeness, present in Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, and contemporary practices. State the article will cover history, symbolism, traditions, and contemporary practice.
2. **Etymology and origin**: Sanskrit etymology, earliest known examples (Indus Valley, Vedic period), evolution into Hindu and Buddhist iconography.
3. **The Hindu mandala and yantra**: Sri Yantra as canonical example. Tantric tradition.
4. **The Buddhist mandala**: Tibetan tradition specifically. Kalachakra mandala. Construction-and-dissolution sand mandalas as ritual practice.
5. **Western reception**: Jung and the Red Book. Mandala as psychological symbol. The mid-20th century "discovery."
6. **Contemporary mandala practice**: A short section naming working contemporary mandala artists across mediums. Link to `/artists` hub. Editorial framing: "The mandala remains a living symbolic form."
7. **What a mandala is for**: Meditation, contemplation, sacred space marking, art-as-practice.
8. **Closing**: One paragraph on the article's mission to be a resource — invite further reading via internal links to `/articles/sacred-geometry-mandala-art`, `/articles/tibetan-mandalas`, `/articles/jung-and-the-mandala`.

**Source folder**: `~/Documents/Obsidian Vault/5 Sources/mandalas/what-is-mandala-art/` — 23 sources. Read the curated 5 in `_top-sources-for-writing.md` first.

**Linking obligations**:
- Inbound: linked from `/`, `/about`, `/articles` hub, every other article in the cluster
- Outbound (internal): every supporting article, `/artists/adrian-rasmussen` when contemporary section names him
- Outbound (external): cite Wikipedia, World History Encyclopedia, Rubin Museum, the Met's 2025 Tibet show coverage

### Article 2 — "Sacred Geometry Mandala Art"

**Route**: `/articles/sacred-geometry-mandala-art`

**Target keywords (primary)**: `sacred geometry mandala art` (1,300/mo), `sacred geometry art` (18,100/mo)

**Target keywords (supporting)**: `flower of life`, `metatron's cube`, `vesica piscis`, `sacred geometry symbols`, `sacred geometry meaning`

**Intent**: The reader is curious about the intersection of mandala iconography and sacred geometry. Many are spiritual seekers; some are working artists; some are designers researching for projects. The article must be rigorous — sacred-geometry content online is often loose with history.

**Structure recommendation**:

1. **Opening**: Define the relationship — mandalas employ sacred geometric principles, but not all sacred geometry is mandala-form. Frame the article as clarifying the intersection.
2. **The geometric principles**: Circle and center. Radial symmetry. Polygonal symbolism (3-, 4-, 6-, 8-fold). Recursion and self-similarity.
3. **Specific symbols**: Flower of Life (history, geometric construction, mandala applications). Metatron's Cube (derivation from Flower of Life, Platonic solids). Sri Yantra as sacred-geometric mandala par excellence. Vesica Piscis.
4. **Sacred geometry across traditions**: Brief survey — Pythagorean, Islamic geometric pattern, Gothic rose windows, Hindu yantra, Buddhist mandala. Separate the historically grounded from the speculative.
5. **Contemporary practice**: How working artists today (including Adrian) use sacred geometric principles in mandala work. Link to `/artists`.
6. **Closing**: Invitation to deeper reading. Link to `/articles/flower-of-life` and `/articles/sacred-geometry-history` (Phase 2 articles).

**Source folder**: `~/Documents/Obsidian Vault/5 Sources/mandalas/sacred-geometry-mandala-art/` + `flower-of-life-symbolism/` + `sacred-geometry-history/` (when populated).

**Watch for**: A lot of sacred-geometry content on the web is loose with historical claims (Atlantis, ancient aliens, etc). Be rigorous. State what is historically verifiable; mark speculative claims as such.

### Article 3 — "Commissioning A Custom Mandala Artwork"

**Route**: `/articles/commissioning-a-mandala`

**Target keywords (primary)**: `mandala art commission` (140/mo), `custom mandala artwork` (210/mo)

**Target keywords (supporting)**: `commission a mandala`, `how to commission a mandala`, `custom mandala art`

**Intent**: Low volume but very high intent. The reader is considering commissioning a mandala. This article must be the clearest, most useful guide on the web for that decision.

**Structure recommendation**:

1. **Opening**: Why people commission mandalas — a personalized symbolic object made for a specific intention, space, or life event.
2. **What a commissioned mandala is and is not**: Original artwork vs. print, hand-finished vs. printed-on-demand, custom intention vs. selecting from existing work.
3. **The commission process**: A generalized step-by-step (intention conversation → sketch → approval → creation → finish → delivery). Editorial — describe the general shape, then note that each artist has their own variation.
4. **Materials and mediums available**: A brief survey of how different mandala artists work — paint on canvas, ink on paper, layered wood, metal, mixed media. Use this to introduce readers to the range of contemporary work.
5. **What to expect on price**: Honest ranges. Small framed paintings start lower; large multi-dimensional sculpture work is significantly more. Frame ranges, not specific numbers (so they age well).
6. **Timelines**: Typical lead times from commission to delivery.
7. **How to find the right artist**: Link to `/artists` hub. Editorial framing: "These are the mandala artists we feature."
8. **Closing**: Soft CTA — "For commissioning a mandala from one of our featured artists, see their individual artist pages." Links to `/artists/adrian-rasmussen` as the immediately actionable path.

**Source folder**: `~/Documents/Obsidian Vault/5 Sources/mandalas/mandala-art-commission/` — 28 sources.

**Adrian's specific commission details**: Used only in his `/artists/adrian-rasmussen` page or on adrianrasmussen.com — not in this general article. This article stays editorial.

### Article 4 — Two halves, one across both domains

This is the only article that gets a unique structural treatment because it crosses domains.

**Part A (on mandalacodes.com)**: `/articles/laser-cut-wooden-mandalas`

**Target keywords**: `laser cut mandala art` (1,600/mo), `wooden mandala sculpture` (590/mo), `layered wood mandala`

**Intent**: Reader is curious about the laser-cut wooden mandala medium specifically. Some are collectors; some are makers; some are designers researching.

**Structure**: Editorial overview of the medium — what it is, how artists work with it, the relationship between digital precision and physical craft. Names and links to artists working in the medium (Adrian primary). Stays editorial.

**Part B (on adrianrasmussen.com)**: `/writings/how-i-create-laser-cut-wooden-mandalas`

**Target keywords**: same as Part A, but reinforced from artist-entity perspective

**Intent**: Reader is curious about Adrian's specific process.

**Structure**: First-person essay. Adrian's voice. His process, his materials, his Bali studio, his approach to layering. The pieces that "made him" his medium.

**Cross-linking**: Part A on mandalacodes links to Part B on adrianrasmussen.com as "the artist's first-person account." Part B on adrianrasmussen.com links back to Part A as "the broader medium context."

This split is the cleanest example of the two-domain strategy in practice. Same keyword cluster, two different angles, two different domains, both reinforce each other via cross-linking.
