## ICHING

### Combination
Mountain sits over Earth. A single firm line rides at the very top, and beneath it five yielding lines have climbed up out of the ground, eating the solid from below. The name of this hexagram, Bo, is a character whose old picture is a knife at work, the trenchant blade that flays, peels, strips a thing back to what it is. This is the ninth month, late autumn, the beauty of summer already gone and the dark creeping up from the floor of the year. What looked like a mountain is being quietly taken apart underneath.

### Upper trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain, Gen. A firm line laid across two yielding ones, the way a peak comes to rest and stops. Limit, frontier, the place past which you cannot see. Stillness, the foot that has stopped walking. On top, this trigram is the one thing left standing while everything under it gives way.

### Lower trigram — Earth (Kun)
Earth, Kun. Three yielding lines, the surface of the world, the ground that bears everything and asks for nothing. Yielding, receptive, the mother. Below, this trigram is the soft dark that supports the mountain and, in this hexagram, the same soft dark that is wearing its base away.

### Reading
The situation this hexagram names is the slow stripping of a thing from underneath. Not a blow. An erosion. The old form, the worn-out way of carrying yourself, the structure that no longer holds weight, is being peeled back one layer at a time, and the peeling starts low, where you are least watching. A bed loses its legs before it loses its frame. By the time the loss reaches the skin of the one asleep on it, the season is almost over.

The picture is a mountain resting on earth with too narrow a base. It stands tall and steep and proud, and it has forgotten that height is borrowed from the ground. So the ground takes it back. The counsel is not to fight the dark climbing up the year. You cannot push autumn back into summer. What is asked is stillness, the combat crawl, staying low and generous to what is below you, because the only thing that survives a stripping is what was never propped up by force.

There is one yang line left, at the very top, and the whole teaching turns on it. The great fruit is not eaten. Inside the fruit is a pit, and the pit is the seed of the next season. Lose the pit and you die. Keep it, and the very stripping that took everything else becomes the turn back toward growth. Most of the lines are warnings, beds breaking, disaster nearing. The last is a promise. What is essential cannot be stripped. It can only be uncovered.

### Judgement
- Splitting apart. The worn form is peeled away.
- It does not favour setting out with a fixed aim.
- Stay low, and let the dark complete its work.

### Image
- The mountain rests on the earth, its base too narrow to hold it.
- So the one above is generous to those below, and steadies the ground under the house.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ the legs of the bed splintered from below. → becomes Hexagram 27, Nourishment.
The stripping begins where no one is looking, at the feet, the foundation. It seems slight. A leg gone is a small thing. But the harm runs ahead of the size of it, and to push your own correctness against the rising dark here only breaks it faster. The undermining has started. Do not pretend the bed is whole.

**Line 2** · _image:_ the frame of the bed splintered, the legs already gone. → becomes Hexagram 4, Youthful Folly.
What ate the legs has climbed to the frame. The erosion is no longer at the floor, it is near the body now, and still there is no ally in sight, no hand reaching up to meet yours. Hold no fixed position. A hard, upright stance brings misfortune here. Yield, adjust, and watch the line of the damage.

**Line 3** · _image:_ stripping, and yet without fault. → becomes Hexagram 52, Keeping Still.
One yielding line among the destroyers refuses to belong to them. It looks above instead, toward the one firm thing still standing, and aligns with that. To strip away here is to cut the tie to the crowd you were placed among. It loses both the company below and the company beside. That loss is the whole of its correctness.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the stripping reaches the skin of the one on the bed. → becomes Hexagram 35, Progress.
The bed is gone. Now the blade is at the skin. This is the last warning the hexagram gives, the final rap on the frame before the house burns, and there is no remedy left, only escape. The disaster is no longer coming. It has arrived at the body. A soft, yielding answer survives it. A proud one does not.

**Line 5** · _image:_ a string of fishes led in like court ladies to the palace. → becomes Hexagram 20, Contemplation.
The tide turns here. One fish leads, and the whole school follows it as if strung on a single thread, and the leader brings them all into the favour of the house. The scattered yielding lines, which were tearing the mountain down, are gathered and ordered and made to serve. Nothing is now unfavourable. What was splitting apart is led, quietly, back into one.

**Line 6** · _image:_ the great fruit left uneaten, the pit still inside it. → becomes Hexagram 2, The Receptive.
At the top, one yang survives the whole stripping. The hard fruit was not taken in. Inside it is the seed, and the seed is the turn of the cycle, the next spring folded into the last fruit of autumn. The one who keeps the pit is carried again by the people, given a carriage. The one who devours it strips his own roof from over his head.
