# The writer's brief

Locked 2026-09-16 by Adrian, after card 1 was read section by section on the page (reading, I Ching, Gene Keys, Human Design, Body). This is the template the 64 are written from. A change to it needs a correction from Adrian on a page, recorded in todo/plans/writing-guideline/adrian-said.md, and the date here moves. Relations was paused the same morning and locked the same evening: Adrian chose the direction (where this energy goes next), read card 1's rewrite on a preview and said it holds, so the Relations paragraph below is locked with the rest.

You are writing one card of sixty-four: one energy, spoken in six voices. It will be read by someone who has never heard of the I Ching, the Gene Keys or Human Design, in any country, on a phone, at a moment in their life when they went looking for something true. The card is literature. Not a chat, not a reference, not a lesson. A page of plain English that a person who loves English enjoys reading, made of chosen words, each sentence one whole thought, every sentence carrying something real about a life that the reader could not have said for themselves. Adrian: "Simple can be one of the most poetic if you pick your words correctly." "Powerful is the way that it speaks to someone and speaks to their heart and really connects with something that is true, rather than trying to be big and powerful."

This brief teaches the register by showing it. Read it whole before you open the packet.

## The model

Adrian wrote the essence of card 1 himself, after rejecting several of ours. It is the register.

"Creation itself moves through you, the power that makes something from nothing. This energy comes in waves, and between the waves there is nothing. No matter how hard you try, the wave will only return on its own."

Read what each sentence does. The first names the thing in its opening words and says what it is before the full stop; a reader with nothing else on the page knows what is being talked about. The second gives one picture, whole, and uses it in the same breath: the wave is placed, with the gap between waves placed beside it. The third carries a fact from the source (creativity cannot be controlled; when it is absent there is nothing to do but wait) with no source in sight, in words a reader can test against their own life. Each sentence is one thought. Each is finished. No word could be swapped for a plainer one, and no word is doing nothing.

He had written a fourth sentence and cut it: "And every time it returns it is the beginning of something new." It was pleasant. It began with And; it added nothing the first three had not already made true. That cut is the craft. Most of writing this card is cutting what is pleasant and empty.

Never begin a sentence with And. Never put two ands in one sentence. This came from Adrian last, and it overrides anything older that said otherwise.

## The sentence he called very bad English

From card 1's reading as written under the previous brief: "It is the push in you to make something that was not there before, and to make it your own way, and it keeps no schedule."

Three thoughts roped together with and. "Push" where the energy needed its name. "Keeps no schedule" is how a person talks, standing in for an image the sentence never gave. Written as literature, the same three thoughts are: "This is the energy of creation itself. In you it makes what was not there before, in a shape no one else could have made. It arrives in its own time and leaves the same way." Three sentences, three thoughts, each finished; the energy named; the timing given as a fact rather than a phrase.

The same paragraph had: "While it is in, you are all the way in: the days have colour, the work gets you out of bed, and stopping is not something you could do if you tried." That is speech. The previous brief told the writer to say every sentence out loud to a friend before writing it, so the writer did, then wrote the talk down. Do not. Speech is loose; written prose is composed. Test a sentence by ear for a chain of ands or a word that clunks, but build it on the page, one thought at a time, then choose the words.

## Before you write: the true things

With the packet open, write down every specific thing the sources say about this energy, as plain statements a stranger could understand and check. For card 1: creativity cannot be controlled, so when it is absent there is nothing to do but wait. The low comes on suddenly; left alone, it passes as suddenly. Hunting the reason for it, or trying to fix it, is what fixes it in place. Something is being made in the low that cannot yet be seen, and it needs aloneness. Numbness is a feeling; knowing you are in it is most of the way out. Nothing new has ever been made without hundreds of mistakes. An idea moves one way, then another, as it comes into the world, and it has to be let. You are the midwife, not the author. What you make remakes you, which is what beauty is. A strength that never rests burns what it has made before the thing has its shape. What begins passes through four seasons before it is yours. Find one who has walked further, then let them see you. Speak when you are asked, not before. The liver is the organ that starts again. Thirty or forty lines, most of them subtle. The subtle ones are the reason the card exists.

This list is the work. A section written without it comes out as voice with nothing inside. Every empty sentence on the earlier cards ("It comes when it comes." "It is the oldest thing in you.") was written by someone who had not made the list or had stopped looking at it.

Then allot the lines across the six sections so each is said once, where it belongs. The reading takes the few that say what the energy is and how a person holds it. The obvious thing is said there, nowhere else. Every other section is a different facet of the one energy. On card 1 the reading holds creation moving through you; the sections hold timing with the stages an undertaking passes through, the fertile low with freshness, your own way with being heard, the organ that renews, the kin a beginning needs. A card whose six sections say one thing six ways is a card with one page in it, and Adrian reads it as one: "Is it only about the creative power? What about the other aspects?" Write each section from its share, nothing else. Say in your final message which lines went nowhere.

## The reading

Three paragraphs, written last, with every source open, from the whole list and never from the essence alone. It is the whole card for a person who reads nothing else. It says what this energy is and what it is to carry it in balance. The first paragraph names it, says what it is, and says where it lives in a life; it is shown alone after a throw, so it stands whole by itself. The second says what it is like to carry it well, with the nuggets in it, and opens in the card's own words: 52 of 64 opened "Carried well, this energy", and a reader with two cards open saw a template. The label rule for the Gene Keys heights holds here too; no paragraph across the deck opens on the same phrase as the same paragraph of another card. The third is the rhythm or the way, ending a beat early.

Before the reading is done, put it beside the I Ching Reading. A sentence that could sit in either loses its place in one of them; on card 2 both said "not yours to start, go first and you are lost, come behind," and Adrian read the two panels as the same page. The reading holds what the energy is and how it is carried; the I Ching holds the situation and its counsel, the scene, the kind you keep to, the body once. A sentence that carried the previous card's I Ching (card 1's "everything that starts passes through four seasons") is not this card's, even where the source repeats it.

The difficult time is not a paragraph. Some readers are not in it. Where it belongs it is a sentence or two inside the third paragraph, said as part of holding the energy, so a reader who meets it can understand what it is turning them into. Never the formula "here is your challenge, here is the solution." Never a verdict. "You are numb" accuses. "Numbness is a feeling, though almost nobody knows they are in it" can be recognised, and the reader is still standing after it.

## How a sentence is made

Enter, and name the thing. The first sentence of every section says what you are about to talk about, in its opening words, as anyone begins. "What begins things in you rises on its own" opened a reading with no thing named, and Adrian read it as a conversation joined in the middle: "Are you talking about making bread?" "This is the energy of creation itself" names it. On card 3: "This is the energy of a beginning." The entry is different on every card. What stays is that there is one. Then say what it is, plainly, before anything about what it is like when it is hard.

One thought, one sentence, finished. Not chopped into fragments ("Quiet holds it."), which is what Adrian called ten sentences in a paragraph. Not chained with and, which is this morning's fault. A thought may need twenty words and its commas; give it them. When the thought ends, the sentence ends. Two thoughts share a sentence only when the second follows from the first.

Choose the word. "A force that begins things is up in you" has a nondescript verb where the sentence needed its one live word; "is alive in you" is the same sentence chosen. Watch for push, thing, stuff, in, out, gets, keeps, goes, comes: words that fill a slot. On the second pass go word by word and ask whether this is the word or only the first that came. Choosing is mostly cutting: the second adjective, the word put in to sound like an oracle, the sentence that says what the paragraph already meant. Cut, never compress. Compression makes riddles.

The picture, whole, then used. One picture, given so the reader sees it before it does any work, with the plain meaning beside it. "This energy comes in waves, and between the waves there is nothing" gives the wave and uses it in one breath. "An idea held too tightly goes out like a small fire under a closed hand" makes the reader decode, and a reader decoding is not feeling. Pictures the reader has touched with their own hands cross every border: a wave, fire, rain, the ground, morning, a child, a hand on a chest, a table. A system's furniture stays in its own section. The dragon lives in the I Ching; everywhere else the card says rises.

Carry the fact, never the source. The sources are full of nuggets, and the card hands them over with no source in sight. "Creativity can never be controlled. It simply comes when it comes, and when it is absent there is nothing for you to do but wait and relax" becomes "No matter how hard you try, the wave will only return on its own." "If you try to comprehend it, find a reason for it, or worst of all try to fix it, then the natural process will not complete itself cleanly" becomes "Asking what is wrong with you is the one thing that makes the quiet last." The classical lines are kept and completed rather than replaced; in Adrian's words, "we don't have to change it majorly, just add connecting words and make the sentences more complete." Never "the tradition says," never a book, never a teacher.

Describe the state; never assign a life. "Anyone who has started a piece of work or a family knows this hour" and "every time you start a piece of work, cook for people or set out to build something" were both cut by Adrian, because they tell the reader what their life contains. Say the state. The reader supplies the moment.

No riddle, no pronouncement. "It is the oldest thing in you. Held well, it is light." sounds like wisdom and says nothing a person could check. The test is whether the reader can say what it means, then test it against their own life. If not, say the thing or cut it.

Two examples at most, and words every culture uses. From card 1's Shadow: "You will do nearly anything rather than feel it: reach for the phone, the fridge, someone to ring, the next thing to do, and each one works for about a minute." Adrian: "I wouldn't do four examples," and "I would use someone to call." One example carries a sentence; two is the limit; a list of four is padding that reads as a list. "Ring" is one country's word; "call" is everyone's. When a word has a regional form and a universal one, the universal one. The sentence, repaired: "You will do nearly anything rather than feel it, reach for the phone or call someone, and each works for about a minute."

Read it back as a person who loves English. Find the sentence a good novel would keep; find the one that is chat, then rewrite it. Find the chain of ands, the word that says nothing, the accusation, then break, choose, turn.

## One energy, six voices

Each section answers one question only it can answer. The reading: what is this energy, and how do I stand in it? I Ching: what is the situation, and what does it counsel? Gene Keys: what does this energy become, at its lowest and its highest? Human Design: where does it press in me, and what does it drive me to do? Body: what is my body doing while I feel this? Relations: who else carries this, and what does it become when it meets its kin?

Speak to you, always. Never "a person," "the reader," "this card," "this reading," "the deck." The card never talks about itself. The page shows the card name, the Gene Keys names, the organ, the amino acid and the keynotes as labels, so the prose never re-announces them. The essence sits under the keynotes once and is never repeated on the card. No system word in prose (hexagram, trigram, gate, centre, shadow, gift, siddhi, defined, open) except where Human Design names its gate, centre and channel; the prose says "at its lowest," "the part of you that." Never the em dash: comma, colon, full stop, or a new sentence.

The I Ching's scene governs the card, and the I Ching section shows it whole; the moving lines vary it six times, the Gene Keys vary it by height. The section has sizes, because the page shows each unit in its own place and a unit that runs long reads as long: the Combination is the picture the two forces make, 40 to 70 words and nothing else; each trigram 70 to 110; the Reading 180 to 260, the situation as a person lives it, what it asks, the body once, the posture; each moving line 50 to 70, the scene of that line in one clause and then what it asks, with the counsel in its last sentence. Card 1's first draft under this brief ran the section to 1,500 words and every line to 110, and Adrian read it as too long; a line that re-tells the whole scene before its own counsel ("What was under the water is out in the open now, in a field where anyone can see it. The place has changed from deep water to open ground") has spent its space before it says anything. The Judgement and Image speak as the classical text, rendered fresh. Each moving line stands alone, read alone after a throw, with its counsel last.

Gene Keys: one energy at three heights, one picture across them, the arc from a thing pressed down to a thing let through to a thing no longer held as yours, never named as seed, plant or flower. Each height opens on what is present and stands strong on its own; the gift is never written as the exit from the shadow, because some readers were born there. The three heights never open on the same stock phrase from card to card: "At its lowest, this energy is", "At its best, this energy is", "At its height" opened cards 1 and 2 alike, and Adrian read it as formula ("I do not want that a part of the template"). The labels above the paragraphs already say which height this is; the first sentence names the thing itself, in the card's own words, as a person would begin. On card 2 the shadow opens on stepping off the world, the gift on being carried, the height on being home; the phrases that place them on a ladder are cut. Sizes: Shadow 150 to 200 words, each nature 35 to 55 (a whole person in three or four sentences, and the two natures do not open with the same words), Gift 150 to 200, Siddhi 110 to 160. Card 1's first draft under this brief ran the Shadow to 268 and the Gift to 294, and Adrian read the section as long; the second paragraph of a height is not a second essay, it is the turn.

Human Design is the lens of this energy, never the energy itself. It may name its gate, centre and channel, explaining each in body terms inside sentences that do work. It says what defined or open is like, never which the reader is, and it never claims the chart. Region and pressure, never the organ. Sizes: The drive 150 to 200 words, Where it lives 180 to 240, What completes it 70 to 100.

Body: one organ, one amino acid, both from the frontmatter, and what each is doing in you while this energy is felt. A fact the reader can check within a minute, at its own scope, where one exists; never advice, illness or healing. Two paragraphs each: the thing, then the mechanism or the kinship. Sizes: Physiology 120 to 180, Amino acid 100 to 150. Food in words every kitchen has (meat, beans, eggs), never a nutrition term. Body is the floor of the card: every other section says what the energy is like, this one hands over something that is true whether or not any system is, and it lands because the reader can put a hand on it. That is also why it fails hardest when written as information, and why it fails worst of all when the writer invents the relation. On card 1 the liver works and three versions of lysine did not, and the difference is the whole craft of the section.

How the organ's join is found and said. Body is written from the organ's reference entry in the vault (`body/organs/<organ>.md`, in the packet), which carries three threads: the Gene Keys assignment, the Human Design centre the organ belongs to and what that centre holds, and Chinese medicine's account of the organ, its season, its work, what it houses. Find the one thing the three say from three sides; on card 1 the liver is the organ of spring, of the plan, of everything in you moving freely, in the centre that holds a life's direction, given to the energy that begins things. That is the join, never the three sources listed, and no source is ever named. It is said as a reason a reader could repeat to someone else, in the first sentence, with the reason a word the reader already owns: "In the body this energy is given to the liver, and the reason is spring." Spring needs no decoding; everyone has stood in one, and spring is creation's own season, so the word lands on the card and in the body at once. The reason was found, in three sources that agree, which is the only kind of reason this section may give. The next sentences unfold it as things the liver does in you, in the card's own verbs (it is the first to wake in the year, it starts the growing, it keeps the life in you moving), then say what that is like from inside, so the reader can test it against a day they have had: "When the liver is at ease, everything in you moves. When it is tight, everything waits." The second paragraph is the checkable thing with its meaning in the same breath: the liver grows back; it sits under the right ribs; "rest your hand there and you are resting it on the part of you that starts over." The gesture arrives somewhere. What failed on the way to this, and must not come back: the join said as an equivalence to decode ("what begins things in you is built to begin again"), which Adrian read as abstract and a waste of his time; a row of anatomy facts instead of one thing told fully; a stage direction that arrives nowhere ("breathing in pushes it down toward your fingers, where some people can feel its edge arrive").

Why the amino acid is different, and what the source gives. The organ has three threads that agree. The amino acid has one, and it is silent about the molecule. The Gene Keys give each card an amino acid because the card's ring shares it, and what the chapters say about it is the ring: a chemical family within your body, the two keys "chemically bonded," the creative fire that gathers everyone while it burns and leaves you unnoticed when it drops, a spark that has to be fanned into action and is there from birth. Nothing in the source says what lysine does in a person. So the writer went looking for a relation in biology, and three times invented one: "the reason is that it cannot be made," joined as "Creation is the same"; "the reason is the pull," a tendon with no card in it, read on the page as "I still don't relate"; "the reason is growing," true biology chosen because it rhymed, with a moral pinned to it, read as "it makes it sound like something that is just made up." That last read is exact. A reader can tell a found reason from a made one, and every reason for lysine was made, because the source holds none. The rule that follows holds for every card: Body never gives the amino acid a reason the source did not give. It says what the molecule is, in one plain clause each, as fact and never as symbol: what it is built into, whether the body makes it or must be given it, where it comes from in food. Then it says the one thing the source does say, that this energy has a chemistry in you and shares it with its kin, as a fact about the body, without the ring or the codon named and without repeating what Relations says the two make together. Where a chapter does speak of its amino acid in the body (key 35 ties tryptophan to serotonin and a hunger nothing ends; key 4 speaks of valine), that is the join, carried as fact with no source in sight, and the reference entry records it with its page. Where the chapter is silent, the paragraph is shorter and honest, and no biology is dressed as a reason.

How the amino acid's paragraphs are made, the shape that holds sixty-four times. The reference entry (`body/amino-acids/<amino-acid>.md`, in the packet, built the way the organ entries are; lysine is the model) carries the ring, the keys that share it, whether the body makes it or it must be eaten, what it is built into, where it comes from in food, every line in the Gene Keys sources that speaks of this amino acid or this ring with its file and page, and a join paragraph per card that says which of those lines that card may carry, so siblings never spend the same one. Twenty amino acids and a stop signal stand behind sixty-four cards, most carried by two or more, leucine by six, and two cards must never say the same thing about one molecule. The reader is in every sentence. A version of card 1 that stated the facts and the chemistry correctly, with the reader present only as "you will never feel lysine at work," read to Adrian as "something is missing," and what was missing was him. So the paragraphs are written from the ring's lived lines, in the body's terms, with the two facts inside them: "The spark you were born with has a substance in you, and the substance is lysine. You cannot make it; your body takes it in, from meat or beans, or goes without." Body may carry the ring's lines (the spark from birth that has to be fanned, the fire everyone gathers round and the flicker nobody notices, the cost of forcing it back by will) even where Relations or KEYS spend them, because Body's job is what the body is doing while the energy is felt and no other section says those lines as chemistry; it says them in new words, in the reader's morning, never in Relations' sentences. The join is the source's own: the spark has a substance, the substance has to be taken in, so what fans the spark is what feeds the body, from outside and in its own time. The second paragraph is the kinship as chemistry, the fire with something to feed that the card's energy grows into, one substance in the body, said without naming the kin card (Relations names it), then the cost of forcing and the return when fed, each traced to a source line. Where the sources give a check, as the organ has, the second paragraph is the check with its meaning in the same breath; where they do not, no check is invented, and the tendon, the scar and the plate stay in the entry as facts a writer may use in one clause, never as the reason. Words the reader owns throughout: food, meat, beans, fire, spark, morning; never building block, residue, molecule, codon, or a cell the reader cannot find. One sourced line per sentence, traced in the final message. Never "It also." Never a moral attached to a fact ("it cannot be forced, only fed"); a fact about what feeds a fire, said as what happens, is not advice. Never the ring or the codon named, never where the assignment came from, never the assignment announced. Never advice, supplements, illness. The test before you leave is Adrian's: read it as someone who has never heard the word and ask whether it sounds found or made up, and whether it is about you. If any sentence gives the molecule a reason, find the line in the source that gave it, or cut the sentence; if any sentence has no you in it, it is about a stranger.

When either entry is missing, write from established anatomy, Chinese medicine or biochemistry at a scope you can stand behind, record it under meta.lore, and report it. A Body section that never names the energy it belongs to failed on card 3 and again on card 1; one that names it as an equivalence failed on card 1 twice; one that joined it to a body fact with no card in it failed a third time; one that gave the molecule a reason the source never gave failed a fourth.

Relations: where this energy goes next. The five sections above look into one energy; this one looks out from it, and the reader leaves with a map with themselves on it and one card to open. Each kin is a direction the energy moves in a life: the pair is the energy a season on, the partner what it is missing, the ring the family it belongs with, the channel what completes it (from DESIGN, not written here). Each paragraph is read alone, the kin's name already above it, so it opens on this energy, said the way the entrance says it: the energy as subject, what it does through you, and the edge where it runs out or turns, before any card is named. "This energy starts what it cannot carry, and the carrying is the next thing it needs." Never on a scene the reader is placed in ("You have begun something and it will not land" was the first draft of this rule and Adrian read it as a story), never on the kin card, never on the lineage's mechanics. One sentence then names and places the card in words a stranger could repeat: "Beyond the Shell is where it goes next: the ground that carries a thing to its end once it is handed over." Then why you would open it, with its reason: "Open it when the starting is done and the rest has to be carried. A beginning cannot carry itself." Card 1 as written was a list with nobody in it: "Turn the six lines over and they come back the same, one of only eight that do." "This is a family of two, the smallest kind there is." Both open on the mechanics; none of it comes back. The lore (Tarot, Immortals, sky and letter) is not the reading path. It sits under "Gathered around it", short and honest about its distance: one picture, how it came here, one line. Sizes: Pair, Programming partner and Codon ring 50 to 90 each, Inverse 40 to 70, Tarot lead one sentence plus ring arcana bullet 30 to 50, Immortals 40 to 70, each Deeper bullet 20 to 35, under 550 for the section. Headings exact, em dash and tail included; the one-line underscore intro required, on what the kin have in common; every name and fact from relations_data, never inferred; lineages named here and nowhere else; the chart never claimed; the panel shows that.

## The order and the sheet

Write ICHING, KEYS, DESIGN, BODY, RELATIONS, then CODE from every source, then the keynotes, then the entrance last of all. The keynotes, five to seven on the `_Keywords:_` line, each name a state of a life that a stranger could say yes to with nothing else on the page.

## The entrance

Three prime directives, in this order, and a card fails on any one of them: it describes the energy EMBODIED, the person living it well and what it gives (never the lack, the fix, the struggle, the wound, the guard); it is RELATABLE, grounded in something the reader has felt, so they are inside the first sentence; and it is PROPER ENGLISH, complete sentences, tenses agreeing, the words Adrian would say aloud.

The shape. Three short sentences, four at most, each carrying one idea, connected so each grows out of the one before, the way card 1's do. Sentence one opens on the card's Universal Language title in quotation marks as its subject, then a verb that means the energy passes through the artwork, then the energy: the title is the vessel, never the energy itself (so never "is", "lives as", "In [title]", and never the title buried at the end). Approved verbs, each used at most four times across the deck and never on two cards in a row: expresses, embodies, carries, brings through, gives form to, gives voice to, makes visible, brings to life, opens onto, is the frequency of, holds, is aligned to, transmits, reveals, radiates, awakens, breathes, releases, emanates, is a window to, opens a door to (the last nine added 2026-09-18 when the cap dropped from eight to four; emanates, is a window to and opens a door to are Adrian's; channels and conveys were tried and struck). Adrian, 2026-09-18: every verb is a way to see the energy; the artwork is never the energy itself. After sentence one the title never acts again; the energy named after the verb is the subject of everything that follows. "Rhythm of Life" expresses joy that grows by being shared. "Creation Oscillation" expresses the urge to start something new. Sixty-four titles make sixty-four openings with no formula. What follows the verb is the whole card in one phrase, checked against the I Ching, the Human Design and the Gene Keys sections; if any of the three is missing from the entrance as a whole it is not finished. Never a dictionary entry, never a scene, never a claim about the reader's situation or character, never "some people". Sentence two says how it behaves, one fact. The last sentence is the choice and what grows from it, said as what will happen: "So wait to be asked and your words will be received as support." Never "this energy" as a subject in the body of the entrance; the frame is given once, if at all, as "The energy of this code is".

Where it comes from. Read the whole card first. The entrance is a summary of the totality: the I Ching, the Human Design drive, the Gene Keys arc and the body together, never one section. Card 58 is the joy of being alive AND the wish to make things better AND the growth by sharing; "the joy of being alive" alone was a quarter of it. Every fact in the entrance must be on the page; nothing invented.

The words. No "not X but Y", no negation as a reveal, no fragment for effect, no line that could be quoted on its own, no adverb, no adjective that carries no fact ("clean", "calm"), no simile, no picture standing in for the thing (feet set for a fight), no card image where the reader's own word exists ("feel true", never "come up"), no verb doing a trick ("land"), one word for one thing (never "sense" and "sight" in a sentence). Every actor is a person or the energy doing a plain verb; the energy "gives" and "makes" nothing. Name the thing that changes, so the reader does not read it as themselves. One "it" at most per sentence and never one that could point two ways; "this", never "the", to point back. No content word twice in the entrance. Commas only where English demands them; "It often comes long before anyone is ready to hear it", never "It comes early, long before". A feeling is "of", felt from inside, never "at". After writing, cut every word whose removal keeps the meaning: "begin when all of you agree", not "begin only when all of you agrees and then let what you started grow".

The three that hold, on the page: card 1 (Adrian): "Creation itself moves through you, the power that makes something from nothing. This energy comes in waves, and between the waves there is nothing. No matter how hard you try, the wave will only return on its own." Card 46 (approved): "Some people are simply lucky, and the luck is not a gift: it is what life does around a body that has stopped fighting it. This energy is that ease, and the moment you clench around how things must go it leaves you; the moment you let go it returns." Card 38 (Adrian, 2026-09-17): "Everything that lasts was held by someone who chose to stay and show up for what is important to them. This energy is about choosing what it is that you hold on to. Not pushing against things, but staying till it matures." And the three written to this rule with him, 2026-09-18:

- 53: "The urge to start something new is a force of its own. It comes before any plan or any agreement. So begin when all of you agree and then let the thing grow in its own time."
- 17: "The sense of where something is heading is a force of its own. It often comes long before anyone is ready to hear it. So wait to be asked and your words will be received as support."
- 58: "Rhythm of Life expresses joy that grows by being shared. It wants to make things better for the people around you. So give it away and it will come back to you as more life."

Why this rule exists. Three rounds in two days. The first sixty entrances repeated the reading.
