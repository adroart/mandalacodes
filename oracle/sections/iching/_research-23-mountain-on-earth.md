# Hexagram 23 — "Mountain on the Earth" — Wilhelm vs. Cleary cross-reference

Research note feeding `iching/23.json`. Question for the card: how literal or how poetic
should the Great Image (大象) integration be?

## The source line (大象 / Great Image)

> 山附於地，剝。上以厚下安宅。

Literal gloss of the operative half:
- **厚下** — thicken / enrich what is below
- **安宅** — make secure the dwelling, the home (宅 = house, dwelling)

So the original image is domestic and structural: the high thing rests on, and is held up by,
the low thing; therefore enrich the foundation to keep the home standing.

## Wilhelm / Baynes

> "The mountain rests on the earth: the image of Splitting Apart. Thus those above can
> ensure their position only by giving generously to those below."

Commentary frame: a mountain that is steep and narrow without a broad base must topple; its
position is strong only when it rises broad out of the earth. Rulers rest on the broad
foundation of the people and must be generous like the earth that carries all.

**Wilhelm's move:** abstracts 宅 (home) into **position**, and 厚 into **giving generously**.
He reads the political-ethical meaning. The frame is hierarchy: ruler and ruled.

## Cleary

> "Mountain on the earth: Stripping Away. Those above secure their homes by kindness to
> those below."

**Cleary's move:** keeps 宅 as **home / dwelling** and 厚 as **kindness**. He stays close to
the literal characters. The frame is domestic and intimate, not political.

## Where they agree

Both read the same load-bearing structure: **the high depends on the low; you hold your
place only by tending what holds you up.** Neither treats "mountain on earth" as scenery.
The image is about dependence and foundation, never about a landscape.

## Where they split — and why it matters for the card

One character, 宅, does the work. Wilhelm generalizes it to *position* (a ruler's standing);
Cleary keeps it as *home* (a personal dwelling). Wilhelm's reading is about power; Cleary's is
about belonging. For a devotional card spoken to one person, **Cleary's home frame is the
warmer, more personal anchor** — "tend what holds your home up" lands more intimately than
"a ruler secures his position."

## Recommendation: ~70% poetic, with the literal anchor being *home*, not *rule*

The existing `iching/23.json` already integrates this image well and leans poetic
(the eroding peak, late autumn, the seed in the fruit). One thing to adjust: its current
`image_lines` and `reading` carry **Wilhelm's political frame** —
"Those above hold their place by giving to those below" / "if you are the one in the higher
position." That is the ruler reading.

For the devotional pillar, consider re-pointing that one practical beat from Wilhelm's
*position* to Cleary's *home*: the counsel becomes "secure what you live in by tending the
ground that holds it up," which is personal rather than hierarchical. Keep the poetry; swap
the literal anchor underneath it from rule to dwelling.

Net: don't make the image more literal overall. Keep it ~70% poetic. But let the single
literal foothold be Cleary's home/foundation, not Wilhelm's ruler/position — it fits a card
that speaks to one person far better.
