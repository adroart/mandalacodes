# UL 30 — Sparking the Blaze

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Wanting Something Badly · Empty Again After · Burning Bright on Something · The Crash After the Hope · Felt but Not Followed · Laughing at Yourself · On Fire With Longing

Desire is the energy here, the wanting that lives in a body before it has found anything to want. It was in you at birth, with no instruction for it. It is not a flaw. It is what leans a person out of themselves toward the world, and it takes any shape, a person or a place, the same reach under every one. It is where a life gets its heat.

Carried well, this wanting is felt in full and followed only sometimes. A desire is a guest: it comes, it is let in, then it goes in its own time or stays to insist, listened to either way. What makes it dangerous is the mind, which fans a small want the way wind fans a flame. A need is a different thing, food or sleep. A desire is a craving for what you could live without, and telling them apart is most of the art. Follow one to its end sometimes, because some things are learned no other way, and forgive yourself the ones that cost too much.

The rhythm is a cycle that never ends. The wanting is fed, there is a moment of rest, then it is back, because it was never for the thing it said it was for. There are seasons when it burns so hard that everything else in you goes dark, and that is the fire clinging. It cannot be escaped, only accepted, the way being mortal is. Let it be felt all the way, lightly, and it burns off what it was feeding on, until one wanting is left under all the others. That one you can trust.

## ICHING

### Combination
Fire over Fire: brightness, and under it brightness again. A flame has no shape of its own; it clings to the thing it burns, and at its heart it is hollow, a soft dark line held between two bright ones. The traditional character shows a bird of fire with brilliant plumage, beside deer keeping close in a herd. Two flames stacked, each bright because it holds on to something.

### Upper trigram — Fire (Li)
Above, the fire is the light already risen: the sun at the height of the day, the lamp the whole room sees by. Light is what tells one thing from another, so this is the part of you that sees clearly, that can tell what is worth wanting from what merely shines. In a family it is the middle daughter, the one with the quick eye. In you it is the brightness that draws people in, the warmth a room gathers to. Its risk is the sun at noon: a light at its height has nowhere to go but down, so a brightness used but never fed goes out early.

### Lower trigram — Fire (Li)
Underneath is fire again, the flame just catching, the heat at the root that the light above was lit from. Here it is the fire that has to hold on: with nothing under it to burn, it is gone. In the world it is the hearth, the first spark on dry wood, the appetite that comes before the meal. In you it is the wanting itself, the heat that leans out toward whatever will keep it lit, and it does not choose its fuel carefully. Its risk is the flare, the fire that comes all at once, burns what it touches and dies, leaving nothing it could stay on.

### Reading
You are burning on something, and the whole question is what you are holding on to. Fire is the one force that cannot stand alone: it lives by clinging, and it takes the shape of whatever it clings to. The sun and the moon hold to the sky. The grain and the grass hold to the ground. What you hold to is what your fire will look like, so choose it, and hold to what is true. Then the brightness lasts, because it is fed by something that does not run out.

What it asks of you is gentleness with the strength you have. The old counsel is to keep a cow: an animal that is docile, that yields, that gives every day without being forced. A fire this bright needs a yielding thing beside it, or it eats its own ground. Feed the light before you use it. Light that is spent before it is nurtured goes awry at the first step, and light that is only ever used, never tended, sets like the afternoon sun. One light after another, that is how it lasts: the brightness handed on, never held.

In the body this energy is the eyes, the two of them, which is where a fire in a person shows first. Notice what yours are on right now, and how long they have been there. Let the gaze soften, and look plainly at what you are holding to, because that is what your fire is going to become.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ footprints crossing every way at the start, and a step taken with care. → becomes Hexagram 56, The Wanderer.
At the very start the footprints run every way, because the fire is eager and does not yet know how to go. You want to use the light before you have learned to tend it, which is how a flame is put out at the first step. Nothing is lost yet. Slow the feet, treat each step as if it mattered, and there is no fault.

**Line 2** · _image:_ yellow light, the colour of the centre. → becomes Hexagram 14, Great Possession.
Yellow light, the light of the middle of things, neither the glare of noon nor the last of the evening. You know you are not the brightest in the room, you say so, and you are lit by what others know. That is the fire held at its centre, giving heat and blinding nobody. Keep to the middle, and it is the best fortune there is.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the sun going down, and a jar to drum on. → becomes Hexagram 21, Biting Through.
The sun is going down, and no fire climbs forever. What is asked here is the plainest thing: beat the jar and sing, take the evening as an evening. Groan instead at the light going, cling to the height you had, and the going down turns into a pit. Fear of the ending is the one error you can still avoid. Sing while the light is on the hills.

**Line 4** · _image:_ it comes all at once; it flames up, it dies down, it is thrown out. → becomes Hexagram 22, Grace.
It comes all at once, flares up, dies down, and is thrown out. This is fire that arrived before it had anything to hold, a want seized on in a leap, sure of itself and fed by nothing. It burns what it lands on, then there is no place for it. Do not wait until you have burned out to see that a flare is not a fire. Come slowly.

**Line 5** · _image:_ tears in floods, and a sigh of grief; then good fortune. → becomes Hexagram 13, Fellowship.
Tears in floods, sighing, grieving, and out of it good fortune. You have seen that you are not the light you thought you were, and the seeing is what the tears are. Nothing rash comes of them, because a person weeping is not a person striking out. This is the eating of bitterness before the sweet, the pride shed with the tears. Weep, keep your place, and it turns.

**Line 6** · _image:_ the king marches out; he strikes the leader and spares the followers. → becomes Hexagram 55, Abundance.
At the top of the doubled light the king goes out to put things right. He strikes at the head of the trouble, letting the followers go, because the rest only went where they were led. In you the leader is the one wanting that runs the others. Take that, and the small ones scatter on their own. Aim at the head, spare the rest, and there is no blame.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Desire · _Gift:_ Lightness · _Siddhi:_ Rapture

### Shadow — Desire
Desire is hunger that has left the body and gone looking. It began as the plain need to eat, and it still keeps that shape, only now it is a hunger for experience, for every taste there is, the dark ones as much as the light. It is not for anything. You were made as a creature that yearns, and the yearning was in you before any object was, so the object is never the point. That is the trick in it: the hunger is not there to keep you safe, it is there to drive you into every experience there is, so that something larger than you can learn from your mistakes. It clings, whatever you do.

The suffering is what you do against the wanting. Fight it and it grows. Call it unholy and it grows faster. The wanting to be one with God and the wanting to destroy an enemy are the same fire wearing two faces. So you neither give in fully, which would be too much, nor let go, which feels like death, and you live half-heartedly between. The fire itself was never the problem, only the war with it.

### Repressive nature — Over-Serious
The one who pushes the wanting down loses more than the wanting. The life in them goes down with it. The whole being stiffens, the body and the face. Life becomes a serious matter, with rules laid over every appetite. Under it is the fear of being burned by what they feel.

### Reactive nature — Flippant
The one who feeds it everything follows every want without care, scorns every rule, and answers the world's scorn by throwing themselves further in. They mean to end the wanting by wearing it out, and it wears them out first, often young. A life spent on every desire belongs to desire.

### Gift — Lightness
Lightness comes the day you give up winning. The wanting cannot be beaten, nor can it be dropped, and when you stop trying to do either, the whole of you gets lighter, not because life got easier but because you went further into it. What dies then is distrust, the belief that life is against you. Life is playing its game through you. Once you see the joke of it, that things had to be this way, you see your own place in the story and feel at home there. From then on there is a glint in your eye.

This does not make you immune to wanting, nor does it make you obey it. You go on wanting, in all its mystery, with a wide view of it now: whether or not you follow a desire, you know it will bring no lasting peace, so it has no hold on you. When you do follow one, you find that getting the thing was not what you were after. Then you can laugh, at yourself first, at the whole tragic comedy of wanting. Real lightness is never afraid of being overwhelmed. Only the pretended kind is.

### Siddhi — Rapture
Rapture is what wanting becomes when only one want is left. Under the thousands that pass through you in a day there is one growing stronger, the longing to be done with suffering, the longing for the source, and when you step into it willingly it burns the rest away. Nothing is held back, not even the wish to be free of wanting. You become your longing. That is what belonging is: being your longing entirely, and nowhere else.

From here the fire is bliss, burning you over and over. The smallest thing sets you alight, and whoever comes near catches it, because the heat is carried outside the body now. There is nobody left in there that wanting could cling to.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 30, Passion
The wanting to feel is this energy in the body, and Gate 30 is where it enters. It is the desire to feel everything, twinned with the fear of feeling, and it is the most intense door in the whole body: whatever else is in you, this turns it up. Feeling here rises wanting to become a vision, and a vision wanting to become a fire you can live by. You do not want a thing so much as you want the burning of it, and you will go a long way to keep the burning alive.

The cost is burnout, and it is learned young: leaping the moment the heat rises, then finding the fire gone before the thing was built. What the drive asks is to keep a flame going for the long road, to know which dream is worth the fuel, and to wait for the time when it can be shared. Passion fed by devotion lasts, and passion fed by depletion burns out. Whoever carries this all their life becomes the keeper of their own fire, and people warm themselves at it.

### Where it lives — the Emotional Solar Plexus Center
This wanting is felt in the belly, above the navel, the Emotional Solar Plexus Centre, the place where feeling is made. It is the only part of the body that is both an engine and a way of knowing, which is why a feeling here moves you before you understand it. It runs in a wave, from hope to disappointment to hope again. The wave is a chemistry: a feeling lasts a few minutes, then passes through, unless it is held, packed into the body as tension. The one rule of this place is that there is no truth in the moment. What is certain at the crest is not certain at the trough, so the honest decision waits until the wave has passed.

In about half of people this centre is switched on, and they have their feelings all the time. For someone like that the wave never stops, and the work of a life is to watch it rather than be it: not I am angry, but there is anger rising. In the other half it is open. Other people's feelings come in and are made louder, so that a room's mood is felt as one's own, then passes when the room is left. For someone like that, being nice can be a defence, and the calm they find alone is real. Done well, it is empathy: knowing what a person feels as if it were yours.

### What completes it — the Channel of Recognition (30–41)
Wanting to feel has no picture of its own. Its other half is Gate 41, at the base of the spine, the pressure to imagine, to dream an experience before it is had. Together they make the Channel of Recognition, the desire for experience, a dream with the heat to live it. It is a channel that waits, needing to be seen before it is lived, and its crash comes when the thing hoped for does not arrive. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you, the dream only catches fire where the two meet.


## BODY

### Physiology
Your body gives this energy to the solar plexus, and the reason is hunger. The solar plexus is the largest knot of nerves outside your head, on the front of the spine behind the stomach, with fibres going out from it in every direction like the rays of a sun. In front of it, under the tip of the breastbone, is the soft hollow you know as the pit of the stomach. That is where hunger arrives: your stomach, empty, tightens on itself, and the pang comes up here an hour past a missed meal, wanted or not. It is the same place your fear arrives. Your body feels a want there before you have decided anything about it, which is what this energy does everywhere else in you.

Lay a hand flat in that hollow and breathe in sharply. It pushes out against your hand, because the muscle above it tightens, then falls back as you let the breath go. About a hand's depth behind that spot is the part of you that wants first.

### Amino acid
The wanting in you is a fire in the body, and when you give in to it, it takes all of you, the body first. That fire has a chemistry in you, and the substance of it is glutamine. You take it in from meat, fish or eggs, and in your body it is the safe way the waste from a burned protein travels, packed where the muscle made it, unloaded where it does no harm.

What is left in you after a wanting is followed is partly this chemistry, a heaviness the next day. Your fire burns to purify, and the same substance is in the part of you that listens as in the part that burns: one chemistry that lets go of wanting by burning through it. As the longing grows the body begins to fill with light, not by anything you do, only as the fire burns.
