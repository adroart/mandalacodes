# UL 16 — Grand Rising

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Awake in Your Own Life · Waiting to Be Ready · Plans Still on the Table · Learning as You Go · Good at Many Things · Nothing Wasted · Ordinary and Alive

Enthusiasm is this energy, the surge of life that gets a thing out of your head and into the world. It is the feeling of being awake in your own life, of caring what happens, so that the day is one you got up for rather than one you got through. It lives in your talent, in whatever you do so well that you forget the time, and it is made to be shared. Where it is missing nothing is wrong, and nothing moves.

Carried well, this energy begins before it feels ready. You start on the thing you keep saying you will start when there is more time, and it turns out that you learn as you go, which is the only way anyone ever learned. Two things happen the moment you begin. A surge of energy comes up from your own excitement, more than you had a minute before, and you stop bending your path to please the people around you. Nobody is coming to help you begin; that part is done alone, and it is the part that costs. Once you are moving you are all there, a whole person standing in the room.

The caring has to be kept alive, because the flat days come. There are stretches when nothing seems worth getting up for, and it feels like tiredness, though it is fear: the fear that if you open your heart to a life you will be drowned in it. It will not drown you. A heart cannot be overwhelmed; it opens further to take the change in. Far from draining you, waking up floods you with purpose. Begin with the thing you actually enjoy, and let it carry you the rest of the way.

## ICHING

### Combination
Thunder over Earth: the first thunder of spring breaking up out of the ground, with the whole field lying quiet under it a moment before. What was stored below comes up as sound and shakes everything awake. The old character shows a child beside an elephant, careful, reverent, strong; the word means to prepare beforehand, to be ready, at ease, and from there, joy.

### Upper trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder is the shock that starts things. It is the first crack of spring coming up out of the earth, the seed splitting, the chick breaking the egg, the push that gets a heavy thing going. In a family it is the eldest son, the first up and the loudest, who wakes the house. In you it is the surge: the rush of excitement that has you on your feet before you have thought, the quickening that comes with a new thing. Above, it is the shaking that moves what lay still. Its risk is noise, a shock spent on its own sound, over before anything has moved.

### Lower trigram — Earth (Kun)
Underneath is the earth, the ground the thunder comes out of, open and quiet. It is the field before the first thunder, everything still under the surface, ready and not yet moving. In a family it is the mother, whom everything rests on, who gives and asks nothing back. In you it is the part that rests and receives: the ease of a body that has slept, the stores laid down in quiet years, the readiness of having provided. Below, it is what the shock rises through. Its risk is indolence, when rest runs past its time and the ease meant to ready you becomes the reason you never rise.

### Reading
You are at the point where something in you is ready to move, and the whole field can feel it. The thunder that breaks out of the ground in spring does not push; it has been gathering under the surface all winter, and when its time comes everything wakes at once. That is the strength you have now, a readiness that moves other people without your having to argue for it. It is the time for setting helpers in place and setting out with them, because what you begin now people will follow gladly.

It asks you to prepare, then to go. Think beforehand, lay in what you need, study the mark before you let the arrow go; the readiness is in the preparation, and the joy comes after it. Then do not hesitate, because a strength like this spoils if it is held. Move with the time as the sun and the moon do, neither early nor late, so nothing in you strays. The danger is on the far side of the joy: enthusiasm slides into indolence, and what was gathered for the start is spent on the pleasure of having started.

Your foot is where the body feels this. Notice where your weight is now, and whether you are ready to stand. A start is felt there first, in the foot that has already turned toward the door. Hold yourself the way thunder holds inside the earth, gathered and quiet, with everything in you pointed at the moment it moves.

**Line 3** · _image:_ wide-eyed, looking up at someone else's joy. → becomes Hexagram 62, Small Preponderance.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the source of the joy, and friends gathering to it like hair into a clasp. → becomes Hexagram 2, The Receptive.

**Line 5** · _image:_ a long illness that does not kill. → becomes Hexagram 45, Gathering Together.

**Line 6** · _image:_ joy gone dark, the moon on the night it begins to wane. → becomes Hexagram 35, Progress.

Change now, even this late, and there will be none.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Indifference · _Gift:_ Versatility · _Siddhi:_ Mastery

### Shadow — Indifference
Not caring is where this energy goes when it is afraid, and it does not feel like fear. It feels like nothing much: a life lived inside the comfortable part of it, the emails and the job, with the attention on what does not matter so it never rests on what does. You are full of plans. The book or the business you keep meaning to start stays on the table, because nothing in you feels strongly enough to move it, and the dream goes on assuming it will happen by itself. The excuse is time, and it was never about time.

The instrument you were given sits in the corner. It is the thing you were born good at, and you have not touched it in years, because touching it means being different from everyone around you who is not bothering either. There is safety in numbers and nothing else in them. A bright, competent life can be built out of skills and systems while it stays untouched; that is what an expert is. What ends this is not a better plan. It is one real experience, actually had, and the caring comes back with it.

### Repressive nature — Gullible
This person has decided nothing can be done, because nobody else is doing anything. They take the news as the truth about the world, their own smallness as the truth about themselves, and hide their not caring behind everyone else's. What they call being realistic is fear with its head in the sand.

### Reactive nature — Self-Deluded
Here the fear hides inside systems. This person builds a wall of methods so high that the reason for it is forgotten, then lives behind it, believing their own account. They are not indifferent at all: behind the wall is a great anger, which will bring it down unless they see through it.

### Gift — Versatility
Care about one thing enough to be absorbed in it and this energy opens the whole world. You pick up the instrument at last and give it years. It takes about seven for a skill to be written into the body so deeply that the hands know it without you; on the far side of those years the skill has become a talent, which is a skill you no longer have to think about. Then the strange thing happens. Having learned one thing all the way through, you find you can learn anything, because the same pattern runs under every art and every science; what you learned was the pattern. The discipline that looked impossible from outside turns out to be easy once the heart is warm.

This is the polymath's energy, the one who moves between painting and business with the same ease, using whatever skill the moment needs, wasting nothing. It runs on enthusiasm, the plain feeling of doing something thoroughly enjoyable that also makes other people's lives better. It can see a child's talent early and put it where it will grow.

### Siddhi — Mastery
Living well is the whole of it, and it has nothing to do with skill any more. The heart, which opened, closed, then opened again for years, stops needing to close. The person it happens to is ordinary: cheerful without pretending, giving because they want to, passing unnoticed in every room they light up. They may not be rich, and you can see that they are prospering. There are no more levels. The greatest player can still improve; here the improving is over, because the one who played has gone and left the playing.

Every piece ever written for the piano is already in the body of everyone alive, and from here it could be played by someone who has never touched the keys. The word enthusiasm once meant to be filled with the breath of God. Here it means God is at play, and nobody can stay indifferent to that.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 16, Zest
Leaping at a new thing and getting good at it is the pressure this energy puts on the body. It enters at Gate 16: the zest that says yes before the details are in, the love of learning that dives to the bottom of a subject, the wish to share what you can do. It is the drive to master a skill, and it masters by repetition, the way an instrument is learned, one pattern played a thousand times until it is yours. From outside the leaping can look unreliable, someone off on an adventure with no map and no food.

The cost runs two ways. One is never sharing the skill, because it is not perfect yet and you are not ready, which can keep a talent in the drawer for years. The other is having been burned by a leap that went wrong, until the zest itself feels dangerous and every risk is refused. What the years teach is that enthusiasm needs a vessel: pack the bag, then jump, because the preparation was never the enemy of the leap.

### Where it lives — the Throat Center
This pressure is felt in the throat, the Throat Centre, the place where what is inside becomes something outside, a word or a deed. More roads run into it than into any other part of the chart, so it is the town square of a life, where everything that wants to be real comes to be said or done. It makes no energy of its own; it turns what reaches it into speech and action. What comes through Gate 16 is skill made visible, put where others can see it.

This centre is switched on in about seven people in ten, and the voice is consistent: one voice in one register, broadcasting rather than receiving, sometimes blunt. For someone like that the talent gets said; the trouble is that it gets said the same way to everyone. For the other three it is not, so the voice is borrowed from whoever is in the room, with a range the fixed voice never has, meeting each person in their own tone. For someone like that the pain began as a child nobody listened to, with two answers, to go quiet or to get louder. The pressure to speak arrives with nothing to say, and words come out before they were chosen. What is their own is the moment they are asked, when the words come to meet the person, and sometimes speak for the whole room.

### What completes it — the Channel of the Wavelength (16–48)
Skill on its own is only surface. Under it is Gate 48, in the body's knowing, the part of you that knows moment to moment whether you are safe: the depth under a skill, and the fear of not being good enough that comes with it. Between them they make the Channel of the Wavelength, a talent: enthusiasm with something under it, mastered over years by repeating one pattern. That fear sends you deeper. The talent only sounds where the two meet, whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you.

## BODY

### Physiology
The parathyroid holds this energy in the body, and the reason is the store.
