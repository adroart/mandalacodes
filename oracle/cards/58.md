# UL 58 — The Joyous Lake

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Restless for More · Aimed at Tomorrow · The Ache Everyone Has · Glad for No Reason · Making Things Better · Better for Being Shared · Nothing Left to Chase

Joy is the energy here, the plain joy of being alive, and on most days you meet it as a wanting. Something in you says the day should feel more alive than it does, without saying what is missing. It is not sadness, which has a cause, nor worry, which has an object; it is a restlessness with no name, and nearly everyone you know carries it. In a life it is the pull toward a better arrangement always a little ahead, the plan or the move that will finally make things feel right.

The ache stops being a fault when this energy is left alone. Nothing you do can bring the joy on, no plan or practice, because the ache is the joy itself, pressing to be felt, and the future is only where you have been pointing it. Seen that way, the restlessness turns in your hands into aliveness. You are glad without a reason, the days have their colour back, and the wanting that drove you outward has become a wish to make things better for whoever is near. That wish is the truest use of it. What you give away comes back as more life in you, which is an open secret anyone can test in a week.

Let the seeking wear itself out, since it will, and be there when it does. There is a stretch when every answer has been tried with nothing left, and that emptiness is what this energy was working toward all along, the one thing that could turn you around. Look into your own restlessness long enough to find it funny. Under it, the whole time, was the gladness you were looking for, still as water.

## ICHING

### Combination
Lake over Lake: open water, and under it open water again, the two surfaces touching with nothing between them, each feeding the other so that neither runs dry. The traditional character is a mouth with vapour rising from it, speech, read another way as a figure singing as it dances, arms up and feet moving. Its scene is gladness that is shared.

### Upper trigram — Lake (Dui)
Lake is open water in low ground, and what a lake does is give back. The sun draws vapour off it, the vapour falls as rain on the fields around, and the lake is fuller for what it gave. In a family it is the youngest daughter, the one the house lightens for when she comes in. In you it is gladness that shows: you have to tell someone, your face gives you away, the pleasure grows in being passed on. Its risk is surface, a brightness with nothing under it, pleasing whoever is near so as to be liked.

### Lower trigram — Lake (Dui)
Underneath is the lake again, and this time it is the depth, the part nobody sees. Whatever gladness shows at the surface is held up from here, by something solid that stays put when the surface is stirred. In the world it is the spring that feeds a lake from below, so the level holds through a dry month. In you it is the gladness that needs no occasion, the good mood you woke in, the contentment that was there before anything had happened. Its risk is the opposite of the surface's: water that is all depth with no opening goes stale, and a joy kept to yourself keeps badly.

### Reading
You are in a season of gladness, and the gladness is meant to be shared. A lake left on its own dries down in the sun; two lakes side by side keep each other full, and that is the whole of the counsel here. Whatever you have found, say it to someone who can answer, then go over it again together, because a thing understood alone is only half understood. Old friends, a table, talk that runs late: that is the work of this season, not a rest from it.

What it asks is that the softness on the surface have something firm beneath it. Gladness in you shows the way a lake shows the sky, on the face, in the colour of the skin, and it keeps that glow only while it is honest. Win people with warmth, never by force, and be a little careful whom you open to, since not everyone who comes to your gladness comes for your sake. Joy that goes in front of people makes them forget how tired they are, and joy that meets a hard time beside them makes them forget their fear. That is the power in it.

In the body this energy is the mouth. Notice what yours is doing now, held or easy, and whether the last thing you said had any pleasure in it. Let the jaw go, let the face be seen, and speak from the depth of the lake rather than its surface.

### Judgement
- Gladness. What is shared goes through and grows.
- It goes well for the one who stays true underneath.

### Image
- Two lakes rest one on the other, and each keeps the other full.
- So the wise gather with their friends to talk a thing through, then go over it again.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ gladness at peace with itself, needing no one to agree. → becomes Hexagram 47, Oppression.
At the bottom of the lake, with no one above to lean on, the gladness here is your own, at peace before anyone has agreed with it. It comes without effort, copies nobody, and wants nothing from the room, which is why nothing about it needs a second thought. A joy that asks for no answer is the one nobody can take from you, so keep it that plain.

**Line 2** · _image:_ gladness that is honest, and the regret goes out of it. → becomes Hexagram 17, Following.
Next to someone who would lead you toward an easy pleasure, you hold to what is real, and the regret that was gathering leaves. Sincerity in the middle of you shows on the outside without your arranging it, and people trust an aim they can see is honest, so that trust in your purpose wins the trust of others. Keep delighting in what is true rather than in what flatters.

**Line 3** · _image:_ pleasure sent for, brought in from outside to fill the inside. → becomes Hexagram 43, Breakthrough.
Out of place and empty, you are sending out for pleasure. Company kept for the lift it gives, or flattery paid to be liked back, works for an evening and leaves the hollow where it was, while the people used for it feel used. What is borrowed from outside cannot fill what is missing inside. Stop sending out, and let the emptiness say what it wants.

**Line 4** · _image:_ two pleasures weighed against each other, and no peace until one is let go. → becomes Hexagram 60, Limitation.
Between two pleasures, weighing them, you cannot rest, and the unrest is a melancholy sitting inside the festivity. It stays wedged there, souring the evening, for as long as the choosing is put off, since the ease you are looking for arrives only when the weighing stops. Look at what pulls you toward the lower of the two, decide against it, then decide quickly.

**Line 5** · _image:_ trust given to what is wearing you away. → becomes Hexagram 54, The Marrying Maiden.
Sure of yourself, you have opened your trust to someone who is wearing you thin, and the danger is that you cannot see it. Strength that likes to be agreed with stops hearing the honest word, and its company drifts toward those who only please. Trust that stays awake can turn even one who has fallen, but only while awake. Look at who is nearest, and at what they take.

**Line 6** · _image:_ gladness stretched like a bow, drawing others after it. → becomes Hexagram 10, Treading.
At the top, the gladness is drawn out to attract, a bright manner with clever words and nothing sure behind it. It pleases so as to be followed, pretends to be full while it is empty, and draws in people who go home no gladder than they came. Nothing about it shines yet. Before you lead anyone toward a joy, make sure you have one.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Dissatisfaction · _Gift:_ Vitality · _Siddhi:_ Bliss

### Shadow — Dissatisfaction
A restlessness with no cause runs under this energy, and most people carry it so long they take it for their character. It is not sadness, boredom or worry, each of which has a shape; it is the flat sense that this is not it, aimed always at the future. The mind takes it as an order to improve something, the house or the work, and every improvement leaves the restlessness where it was. When enough of the world has been rearranged the search turns inward, to a practice or a teacher with a peaceful future promised at the end, which is the same search in other clothes. Some people go against the rhythm on purpose, doing anything to get a reaction from life, and take a dark kind of relief in that.

What the restlessness does is wear you out. It will not let you rest until every answer has been tried, and the emptiness left when they have is the first honest thing it has given you. Nobody is spared it; under every life you look at is the same ache, the ache of wanting to come home, and there is a beauty in that.

### Repressive nature — None
No one holds this energy down, because nobody can. It is the life in a body, and a body cannot refuse its own life without dying. So there is no person here who has swallowed the restlessness and gone quiet; anyone who tries finds it coming back out sideways, as the next thing to fix.

### Reactive nature — Interfering
This person meddles with life. Having once tasted joy arriving by itself, they want it again and set about producing it, pushing a life already moving. Every push brings more discomfort, which calls for another. They stop when they see that the restlessness was the life in them, asking to be let alone.

### Gift — Vitality
Aliveness for no reason is what this energy becomes once you give up on arriving. The force that was pouring out into a future turns back into the body, and what felt like discontent is felt now as being alive. You wake glad without a cause. The space between thoughts widens, the grip on what happens to you loosens, and a body that is old or tired feels younger for the joy welling up in it. Loss is part of it. Everyone you have lost is still part of you, and the life in you is larger for them, not smaller.

What it is for is giving. The wish to make things better is still here, only it is no longer trying to relieve your own ache; it helps because it can feel where life is stuck and how to free it without forcing anything. People with this alive in them walk into a room and the room moves better. Things happen quickly around them, because once this has started it does not turn back. The more you give, the more of it there is, which is an open secret.

### Siddhi — Bliss
Bliss is the lake gone still. The seeking has turned so far inward that it arrives back where it started, something lets go at the very bottom, and the point inside you that you took yourself to be is not there. Nothing stirs the water after that, because nobody is holding it. Consciousness comes in like a flood and wipes you clean in less than a breath. The gaze goes soft, the heart fills past what it can hold, and the body becomes the one place all of this pours through, so that it shows in the face, where it cannot be hidden.

It cannot be reached by seeking, though seeking usually comes first and wears itself out on the way. Look at your own restlessness until you can laugh at it, and the joy turns out to have been inside the ache the whole time, the way the fruit is inside the seed.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 58, Joy
The drive here is to make things better, because being alive is good. Gate 58 is its opening in you: a pressure felt low in the body that looks at whatever is in front of you, sees at once how it could be more alive, and takes pleasure in the seeing. Joy and the urge to improve are one movement in you, not two. It is the wish to get better at a thing for the pleasure of it, and it is fed by practice, the same thing done again because the doing is a joy.

From outside it can look like discontent, or like criticism, and there is a cost. Said before anyone has asked, what could be better lands as fault-finding: what you offered is refused, and it feels as if you were. The other cost runs inward. Some people carrying this were taught that pleasure has to be earned, so they ration the thing they love and call it discipline. People who carry it all their lives learn that the joy was the point, and that a correction offered when it is wanted is taken as help.

### Where it lives — the Root Center
The base of the body is where this pressure rises, the Root Centre, the square at the bottom of the chart that holds the pressure to move. It is not a place that knows or decides; it presses, the way a held breath presses, and it presses in pulses. When the pulse is on, you can begin anything. When it is off, nothing you know you should do can be started, and no amount of wanting turns it back on. The whole art of this centre is timing: knowing the pulse, resting in the gap, and not pushing when the pressure is not there.

Six people in ten have this pressure switched on, steady and their own. For someone like that, stress is familiar company, and the danger is the burn: corrections offered that nobody wanted, work forced through a gap in the pulse, until the body gives out. In the other four it is not, so the pressure is taken in from whoever is nearest and made louder. For someone like that, another person saying the kitchen needs cleaning is felt as an order, and they are on their feet before they have chosen to be. The task done, the next appears, and the pressure was never theirs. What both learn in the end is the same: felt rather than obeyed, the pressure stops being stress and becomes the hum that brings a hummingbird to a flower.

### What completes it — the Channel of Judgment (18–58)
Joy on its own knows something could be better without knowing what. Its other half is Gate 18, in the Spleen Centre, the part of you that knows when something is off and sees the flaw. The two make the Channel of Judgment: the pleasure of making things better joined to the sight of what needs it. Joy gives the why; correction gives the what. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone you are drawn to, what the two make is the upgrading of whatever is in front of you, once it is asked for.

## BODY

### Physiology
The floor of your body is the perineum, a small sling of muscle between the legs, and this energy is given to it because it is the first place fear takes hold. Before you know you are afraid it has tightened, and from there the grip climbs the spine into the jaw, so the whole of you braces from the floor up. Held tight, it puts you on automatic, acting on the next urge without being there to weigh it. The restlessness this energy brings lives there first, as a base clenched for years with the breath pulled up high and shallow, before it reaches you as a thought.

The same floor is where your joy comes back. Laugh, and it tightens a moment before the laugh arrives, with nothing decided; it is the muscle you stop the flow of urine with. Hold it now, and notice your breath go high. Let it go, the breath drops on its own with the shoulders after it, and what was going out of you toward tomorrow turns back into you as warmth.

### Amino acid
The restlessness in you has a substance, and the substance is serine. It is one of the twenty small pieces every protein in you is built from, plentiful in meat or beans; the juices that take what you ate apart into those twenty pieces cut with a serine at their edge. The ache you share with everyone you know is chemistry too: one family of pressures in the body, each driving you to seek an end to the longing, and this is one of them.

The seeking in you is fuel. Turned outward it burns as the wish to fix or to become; turned inward, when you relax enough, it is the vitality that was underneath. As the pressure falls away, the life in you runs clearer. Bliss itself, when it comes, is made in you, a release of chemicals in the brain, felt as gladness with no cause.
