# UL 64 — Communion

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Imagination · Something New From the Pieces · Building What You See · The Nerve to Make It · Pain Turned Into Art · Nearly Across · A Work in Progress

Before completion, a thing is almost made and still open to change. Imagination lives in that stretch, showing you the shape and colour of what you are making long before your hands get there. The pictures come whole and out of order, often in the middle of a change.

Seeing the picture is half of this gift, and making it is the rest. That takes nerve, because what you make will change the life you have. You can ride imagination the way you would ride a wild horse, only as far as it agrees to go. It cannot be hurried: try to work it all out in your head and it shuts down. Leave it be, and it hands you the next piece as you build. So your life becomes a work in progress, and nothing in it is ever quite finished.

There are stretches when nothing will settle and no picture is clear. Your mind will want a reason and something to do. Every step you take just to end the not knowing makes it worse. What these stretches need is time. Given that, the pictures come back. Near the end, keep your eyes on the work in front of you, because the picture can make a thing feel done before it is.

## ICHING

### Combination
Fire over Water is the hexagram of being nearly across. Fire is the clearness in you that tells one thing from another. Water is the risk you are still standing in. The clearness sits above the risk because you can see where you are going long before you are out of danger. That is why this hexagram has long been read as a young fox crossing a river. A step from the bank it thinks it is across, its tail goes in the water, and it cannot go on. Fire climbs and water sinks, so the two draw apart until each is set in its place. You can see clearly and still be in danger.


### Upper trigram — Fire (Li)
Fire is the part of you that sees how things fit. It lights up the whole of what you are making, so you can sort out the pieces and set them in order. It sits on top because it shows you what lies ahead: the plan, and the shape of the finished thing. Fire has long been read as a flame, which warms what is near it and uses up what feeds it. On its own, your seeing runs ahead of where you are, the way a flame climbs away from what lies under it.


### Lower trigram — Water (Kan)
Water is the part of you that has to take the risk. It is the test you are in the middle of, where everything rides on the next step. It lies underneath because it is where you actually stand: however settled the plan looks, you are still in the river. Water has long been read as a gorge, a steep place where the stream runs fast and one wrong step takes you down. On its own, it pulls you down into the danger, the way water always runs to the lowest place.


### Reading
You are nearly across, and the last stretch is the one that decides it. Most of what you set out to do is done. The far bank is so close that watching your step now feels foolish. This is the moment the fox wets its tail. It acts as if it were across before it is, and loses the whole crossing at the last step.

What this time asks is that you check what is done before you call it done. Look at each part of what you have made and ask what it is really doing. Fire can warm water or boil it away, and water can cool a fire or put it out. The same two things help or harm depending on where they sit. So put each thing where it does its good.

Your feet are the last of you to cross, so keep your attention on them until you are out. Take the last step as carefully as the first. Wanting to be across is not the same as being across.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ its tail in the water before the first real step. → becomes Hexagram 38, Opposition.
Your tail is wet before the first real step. You set out to finish the whole thing from the very bottom, before you knew where your reach ended. The shame is small and it passes. Learn where your reach ends before you take the next step.

**Line 2** · _image:_ a wheel dragged back, the cart held on the bank. → becomes Hexagram 35, Progress.
You drag the wheels and hold the cart back on the bank. From outside it can look like lost nerve, the way a skilled hand can look clumsy. It is strength kept for the moment it is needed. Hold to the middle of the road and it goes well.

**Line 3** · _image:_ far bank in sight, the army wanting to march. → becomes Hexagram 50, The Cauldron.
The far bank is in sight and you want to charge at it. Set out from here on your own strength and it goes badly. The river can still be crossed with help. Let someone who has been further carry you over.

**Line 4** · _image:_ thunder loosed on the demon country, three years of it. → becomes Hexagram 4, Youthful Folly.
You stay true and the regret burns off. Now comes a long campaign against old habits that will not leave on their own. It takes three years, because what is true is slow to come back and what is false is slow to go. Keep at it and the reward is a whole country.

**Line 5** · _image:_ a clear person's brightness, true all the way through. → becomes Hexagram 6, Conflict.
The false shine has gone and a steadier light stands in its place. You are calm and quick at once. Nothing here needs regret. Your light is true, so let people see it.

**Line 6** · _image:_ wine drunk in good faith, then the head going under. → becomes Hexagram 40, Deliverance.
The crossing is made, and you drink with the others in good faith. There is no fault in that. Drink until your head goes under and the good faith is gone. Too much trust at the end is the same mistake as too much doubt at the start. Keep your measure now.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Confusion · _Gift:_ Imagination · _Siddhi:_ Illumination

### Shadow — Confusion
Confusion is what a mind feels when everything in it has come loose and nothing has its order yet. It comes when what you knew about yourself stops holding. In itself it is full of what could be. It turns painful only when the mind tries to explain it. The mind wants a reason, then something to do, then the old order back.

Being unfinished is hard to bear, so the pull is to keep things as they were. A change that was trying to move you gets pressed back into its old shape. While it lasts you feel alone in it.

### Repressive nature — Imitating
Holding that much unease down takes the help of everyone else. So this nature copies. It follows its parents and its friends, and while everyone agrees nobody has to feel anything. Copying is the one thing imagination cannot live beside, though some manage to look original while they do it.

### Reactive nature — Confused
This nature cannot keep the unease down at all, so it spills out into the life. Closeness breaks in the same place again and again. The anger is aimed at a world that seems to have everything settled. The whole life becomes a way of getting even with life.

### Gift — Imagination
Imagination begins when you stop trying to make the not knowing go away. The mind that was busy explaining goes quiet. The energy it was using comes back to you, and you start to make things with it. What arrives first is often the ache you were trying to avoid. Give it a shape in paint, in words or in a song, and it begins to change in your hands. Keep going, and the fears you drew at the start turn out to hold something good.

Imagination is also the end of copying. It goes where nobody has been, too quick and too wild to stop and explain itself. It will not keep to what you did last year. It asks you to let your life keep changing.

### Siddhi — Illumination
Illumination is imagination that no longer belongs to you. The pictures and words still come, and they are made for whoever needs them. You stay empty while they pour through you.

Near such a person the eyes seem to shine and the room seems to shimmer. You hear such people only once you stop listening for the meaning. In the quiet between their words something true gets across.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 64, Confusion
Gate 64 sits at the top of your head, where the question how begins. It fills with pictures: images, dreams and pieces of the past that arrive out of order. You can see them long before you can explain them. Along a straight line they read as a mess, which is why the gate is named for confusion. The pressure is to answer at once how each picture becomes real. Answered too early, that question shuts the dreaming down and leaves you tired and low.

### Where it lives — the Head Center
The Head Center sits at the crown of your head and does one thing: it asks. It is one of two centres in the body that run on pressure, so a question and the push to answer it arrive together. It has no motor of its own and cannot act on anything it asks. It is not where a decision is made either. Most of us treat its questions as orders and move before the answer has come. Others turn the pressure inward, and it becomes worry.

About three people in ten have this centre defined. Their minds keep asking all day, and even while the pictures are unclear they know the mind is working on something. The other seven in ten have it open. They take in the questions of everyone around them and feel them louder than their own. Asked what they think, they can go blank. For them the relief is in noticing whose question it is.

### What completes it — the Channel of Abstraction (64–47)
The Channel of Abstraction joins this gate to Gate 47, just below it in the Ajna. The head hands its pictures down, and Gate 47 turns them over until they mean something. Together they think in images and leaps, and they learn best with no set order. Without Gate 47, in you or in someone you are with, the pictures keep arriving and never add up.

## BODY

### Physiology
The pineal gland carries this energy, and the reason is light. It is the size of a grain of rice and sits in the middle of your head, where no daylight ever reaches. It learns whether the sun is up from your eyes, by a long road down into the neck and back. When the eyes report that the light has gone, it lets out the hormone of the dark and your body begins to slow. So a gland that lives in the dark keeps your day by light.

Sit in a dim room in the evening and within an hour or two you feel yourself grow heavy. Switch on a bright screen and the gland reads it as day, and the heaviness holds off. In some lizards its relative is a third eye under the skin of the crown, with a lens of its own.

### Amino acid
The light that lies hidden in you until it is ready to show has a substance, and it is glycine. It is the smallest of the twenty pieces your proteins are built from. Your body makes its own and takes in more from meat, fish and the jelly a cooled stock sets into. In your brain it sits at the gate where memory is laid down, and that gate will not open until glycine is in its seat.

You share this substance with three kin, and together the four change one state into the next. That change has long been told in colours. It starts black and passes through white and yellow to the red of gold. So whatever feels dark in you now is where that change begins.

## RELATIONS

### Pair — UL 63, Adornments of Time (After Completion)
Imagination runs out the moment the thing it pictured is finished. Adornments of Time is where it goes next: the curiosity that asks how the finished thing works and finds its flaw. Open it when what you made has to be looked after. A thing that is built still has to be kept.

### Inverse — UL 63, Adornments of Time
This energy stands in the river and looks at the far bank. Turned end for end it stands on that bank and looks back. Adornments of Time is that view. It is this energy's pair, partner and inverse at once.

### Programming partner — UL 63, Adornments of Time
This energy moves by leaps, and a leap needs somewhere steady to land. Adornments of Time holds what it lacks: reasoning that tests each step before it takes the next. One is the way of the artist and the other the way of the scientist. Open it when the picture is clear and nobody can follow how you got there.

This energy turns a loose state into something new, and it does not do it alone. It belongs to a family of four called the Ring of Alchemy. UL 6 breaks down the walls between people, UL 40 builds that openness into communities, and UL 47 lets old ways change. This one opens a fresh set of possibilities.
