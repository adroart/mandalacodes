# UL 28 — Becoming the Mystery

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Needing It to Matter · Going Toward the Hard Thing · The Days With No Point · Afraid to Live, Not to Die · Holding Nothing Back · The Part That Is Yours

This energy is the hunger for purpose, the need to know that being alive is for something. Under the hunger sits the oldest fear there is, the fear of death, and every other fear you have is that one wearing different clothes. It lives in the question you ask when a day is over, whether any of it mattered, and in what you would give everything for. Where getting through is no longer in doubt the fear does not leave. It comes back as the fear that none of it means anything, which frightens more people now than dying does.

Carried well, this hunger lives beside the fear instead of running from it. The amount of life you feel is exactly the amount of fear you are willing to look at, because what stands between you and what you want is never the fear itself, only the mind's picture of it. The mind would rather put your purpose somewhere ahead, next year, when things are settled, and you can spend a whole life waiting for that year. What you are for is not up the road. It is inside the fear you keep stepping around, which is why the old stories send the hero down into the dark before anything is won. Knowing your purpose turns out to be a phase you pass through. The hunger was for being alive, and being alive is done now or not at all.

The way is to give the whole of yourself to whatever is in front of you, with nothing kept back for a better moment. There will be stretches when you cannot say what any of it is for, when the days go through you without leaving a mark. That flatness is the fear with the sound turned down, and changing the job or the town to cure it only gives the flatness a new address. Turn toward it instead, and let it be as big as it is. The fact that you will die is what gives the days their edge, so spend them.

## ICHING

### Combination
Lake over Wind: the water has risen above the trees it was meant to feed, and the wood is going under. Its shape is a roof beam, thick through the middle, thin at both ends, loaded until it bows. The name sets great beside going past: a will fixed hard on one thing, carried beyond what was built to hold it.

### Upper trigram — Lake (Dui)
Lake is open water, the surface of a marsh with vapour lifting off it, and what the lake does is gather. It lies low so everything runs into it; it is the mouth of the land, where things meet, are exchanged, are enjoyed. In a family it is the youngest daughter, the one everyone is glad to see. In you it is the part that is pleased: the ease, the readiness to say yes and be lifted by whatever is going. Its risk is rising. Water that keeps gathering has no way to stop, and here it has climbed above the trees it was feeding to stand over them.

### Lower trigram — Wind (Xun)
Underneath is Wind, which is also wood: the tree with its roots down and its branches up, the breeze that finds its way into every crack unseen. What wood does is hold; it is the base a thing is set on, the root that lets the rest stand. In a family it is the eldest daughter, the one who keeps the house going. In you it is the quiet persistence that goes on carrying, a little at a time, past where anyone notices. Its risk is giving way. A root that yields to everything above it is a root under water, and the tree it holds up drowns standing.

### Reading
Something in your life has grown past what was built to carry it. A purpose or a love, whatever it is, it is greater now than its supports, the way a roof beam bows when the load is all in the middle and the ends are thin. This is an extraordinary time, and it is not wrong. Extraordinary means great and hard together; it asks for gifts of the same size.

What it asks first is that you move. The old text says it furthers to have somewhere to go, and here that is the whole of the counsel: a weight like this cannot be held still, only carried in a direction. The ends will not brace it, so the strength has to come from the middle, from you, from a balance of holding firm and giving way that nobody can set for you. The company for it is the ones who have carried something too big and are still standing. The ones who tell you to put it down have not seen the size of it.

In the body this energy is the back, the long beam everything else hangs from. Notice how you are holding it now: braced, curved under the load, or straight. Stand alone without fear, the way the wise do in this season, and be someone who could walk away from the whole age without a grudge if it came to that. That is not giving up. It is what makes the weight bearable.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ a mat of white grass laid on the ground before the offering is set down. → becomes Hexagram 43, Breakthrough.
With white grass spread under the offering, you are at the bottom of a heavy time and the care is all yours. Nothing has been set down yet, so nothing can crack. Put something soft under whatever you are about to carry, ask for no notice, and let the ones ahead of you go first. Care this early looks like too much and is exactly enough.

**Line 2** · _image:_ a withered willow putting out shoots, an old man taking a young wife. → becomes Hexagram 31, Influence.
Green shoots on a dry willow, and a marriage nobody expected to bear anything. What looked finished in you is not finished. Old strength has found young life, and each has what the other lacks, so the mismatch is the point. Take the worn idea and give it a new shape. Everything furthers from here.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the ridgepole bending. → becomes Hexagram 47, Oppression.
The beam is bending, and there is no help for it. Strength used as strength alone, going forward with no way back and nothing at the side to brace it, bends under its own weight. Nobody can help a strength that will not be helped. Stop pressing on what is already giving, because what breaks here breaks from the insisting, not from the load.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the ridgepole raised, curving up and holding. → becomes Hexagram 48, The Well.
The beam holds, curved upward over the whole house, and this is good. The great has learned to be small as well, so the load is carried without the middle giving. The one danger is wanting more than to carry it: looking about for props below, or for praise. Hold what you hold and want nothing past it.

**Line 5** · _image:_ a withered willow in flower, an old woman taking a young husband. → becomes Hexagram 32, Duration.
Flowers on a dry willow, and this time nothing will come of it. A bloom on dead wood is real, roots nowhere, draws the eye for a season, then the wood is what it was. This is a quick answer reached for to keep a fading strength, and it earns neither blame nor praise. Do not mistake the show for the growth.

**Line 6** · _image:_ wading on until the water closes over the head. → becomes Hexagram 44, Coming to Meet.
You walked into the river for everyone's sake, and the water has gone over your head. The heart was right and the strength was not enough: misfortune with no blame in it, since the motive was to serve. The water is your own doing, nobody else's. Get to the far bank however you can, then never again mistake the size of your heart for the size of your strength.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Purposelessness · _Gift:_ Totality · _Siddhi:_ Immortality

### Shadow — Purposelessness
There is a fear under every fear you have, and it is the fear of being put out like a light. Most of a life is arranged so it never has to be looked at. It comes in three layers. First the fear of living, the one most lives are built around. Then, some way past forty, the fear of dying, which arrives as a panic with no name, called a crisis. Then the fear of not having lived, which is the one that finally moves you.

Avoiding it is a dance around a hole, and you can dance for decades. The usual way is to pick a fixed set of beliefs and live inside them instead of beside the fear; a settled answer numbs it, since the mind hates a surprise. Fear is a chemistry in the body, which knows one answer to it older than any thought: a hand laid on the belly, yours or one you trust, then a breath that reaches lower than the fear let it. The body is not afraid to die. Only the fear is.

### Repressive nature — Hollow
This person has turned from the dark in themselves, and the life has gone hollow. Glamorous or dull, it has no centre. They work at seeming happy, even to themselves, and anyone who has met their own dark sees straight through them. They live a half-life without knowing how visible it is.

### Reactive nature — Gambling
Here the fear is turned into speed. The moves are rash, the stakes keep rising, and there is never a still minute to feel what is driving it. It looks like nerve and it is flight. They will try anything for purpose except stopping, since the one room they cannot enter is their own silence.

### Gift — Totality
Fear can be lived alongside rather than run from, and that is where this energy turns. You stay open to whatever comes and committed at the same time, neither the one who cannot change nor the one who cannot stay. The whole of your nature is let in, the pleasure with the pain, and the mind is no longer allowed to run the life. The only risk left is the real one, building something whose shape you cannot see until it is finished, on trust in the rhythm under it.

Life is a play from here, and you have a part. Whatever the part, lover or fool, you play it all the way, and underneath the giving a strange calm opens, a detachment that is the reward for holding nothing back. Every situation that comes is a chance to change or to stay as you are, and each fear you accept makes you lighter. The demons, looked at, were angels in disguise. In a moment lived to the full, fear cannot survive, and as your own quietens, what you have turns outward to others, which is where purpose was kept the whole time.

### Siddhi — Immortality
Time can stop, and when it does there is nothing left for death to happen to. The mind pictures living forever as more and more time, which is the one thing it cannot be; the mind knows only time. What opens here is a present so complete there is no next. Picture the theatre after the last act of a tragedy: the actors and the watchers gone, the lights down, you still in your seat before the empty stage. That is the thing in you that was there before the play and is there after it. It watches, listens, breathes. The self that could be lost has dissolved into it, and what is left cannot die because there is nobody in it to die. It comes through living wholly, miserable wholly or glad wholly, and it does not mind which. Near someone who has arrived here your own fears show themselves, which is the only way they ever leave.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 28, Adventure and Challenge
Struggle for a life that means something is the pressure here, and Gate 28 is where it presses. It is the drive that goes toward the hard thing on purpose, to find out what the struggle is for, and it will change a whole life, the work or the country, to get nearer the answer. Nobody taught you to want the test. A road with nothing hard in it reads to you as a slow kind of death, and a difficulty you chose feels more alive than a comfort you were handed.

From outside it can look like courting trouble. What is going on is the search for what is worth dying for, which is the same as what is worth living for. The cost is a fear the same size as the drive, that it will all be too hard, end in failure, so why begin; that fear can hold you still for years. People who carry this all their lives learn that difficulty is not failure, that a hard thing survived is worth more told than kept, and that the struggle only pays when it was worth fighting.

### Where it lives — the Spleen Center
Low on the left side of your body, under the ribs, sits the place this pressure rises from, the Spleen Centre, the oldest awareness you have. Every animal has it: the alertness that reads danger in the moment, through the senses, faster than thought, and speaks once, softly, without repeating itself. It keeps you alive, so it keeps the fears about staying alive, one to each of its seven gates. Gate 28's is the fear that life has no point, that it will be too hard. The struggle for meaning rises through the same place that handles fear: what you go toward and what you run from come up one channel.

In just over half of people this centre is switched on, and the alertness is steady whoever they are with: a cat on a doorstep in the sun, eyes shut, ears turning. For someone like that the read arrives once, in the body, and the risk is pushing past a warning because the body has always been strong. In the rest it is not, so the alertness is borrowed from whoever is nearest and goes when they leave. For someone like that every fear in a room is felt as their own, and the pull is to hold on to a job or to someone past the point of safety because letting go feels like not surviving. What they have instead is a fine sense of what is whose.

### What completes it — the Channel of Struggle (28–38)
The drive to struggle for meaning has no force of its own: it knows what it wants and cannot push. Its other half is Gate 38, at the base of the body, the pressure that will not lie down and keeps fighting whatever stands in its way. The two make the Channel of Struggle, the sense of which fights are worth having, for your own survival or someone else's, with the stubbornness to stay in them. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you, the struggle only finds its point where the two meet.

## BODY

### Physiology
The kidneys are where this energy is kept, and the reason is fear. What they hold is the store you were born with: a reserve spent over a life, never remade, at a pace they set. Two fears live there as one, the fear that life has no point with the fear of running out, and the pair is quiet exactly as long as the store is being spent on something. Deciding what to keep is their other work. The whole of your blood passes through them dozens of times between one morning and the next; out of all of it they hand back nearly everything.

They lie high at the back, one either side of the spine under the last ribs. Put your hands on your waist behind you, thumbs forward, and your fingers are over them, though nothing can be felt through the muscle. You are resting your hands on the part of you that was full at birth, that is being spent, and that holds more than one life needs.

### Amino acid
The hunger for purpose has a chemistry in you, and the substance is asparaginic acid. Your body makes this one for itself, out of what is left when food has been burned, and asks nothing from the plate for it. It is built into every protein you are made of, and half the nitrogen your body lets go of leaves by way of it. What is yours in it is the oldest fear, of death itself, and the life put off because of it, saved for a year when it will mean something.

The same substance is the moment given everything, whatever part you are playing, and the calm that runs under it when nothing is held back. It is one chemistry either way, the postponing with the giving, and in your body it is shared with one other energy, which carries the other half of the fear.
