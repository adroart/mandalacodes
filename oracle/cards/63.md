# UL 63 — Adornments of Time

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Curiosity · Asking How Things Work · Taking It Apart · Keeping What You Built · An Open Mind · Doubt Turned Inward · Rest After the Search

After something is finished, the questions begin. Curiosity lives in that time, and it wants to know how the finished thing works, part by part. It goes back over what everyone else has stopped looking at. There is always one more question behind the last one.

Turned outward, this curiosity is one of the most useful things a person has. It takes a theory or an engine apart piece by piece, and what you learn on the way is worth more than any single answer. It makes life easier and better ordered, and it has built the tools that bring people together. Every discovery begins with a question like this.

When the questions turn on you, say them out loud to someone you trust, and they move on. Take no step just to be rid of not knowing.

## ICHING

### Combination
Water over Fire is the hexagram of what comes after a thing is done. Fire is the clarity in you that sets each part of your work where it belongs. Water is the part of you that knows where the danger still is. The watching sits above the order because once a thing is done, keeping it is the work that is left. This hexagram has long been read as a pot of water over a flame. Tend both, and the water boils. Let either run wild, and it puts an end to the other. You are never more at risk than when everything is in its place.

### Upper trigram — Water (Kan)
You keep an ear out for the danger that remains after the work is done. That watchfulness sits on top because a finished thing is most exposed once you stop listening. Water has long been read as the stream that runs down to the lowest place and fills it. Your watchfulness goes the same way, down to wherever a leak would start. On its own it goes under, lost in the danger it was watching for.

### Lower trigram — Fire (Li)
You see how each part of a thing fits, and you put it where it belongs. That clarity lies underneath because the order had to be made before there was anything to keep. Fire has long been read as the light that tells one thing from another. By that light you can see what each part of the work is for. On its own it keeps checking until it finds danger where there is none.

### Reading
The crossing is behind you, and what you set out to do is done. Every part of it is in its place. What is left is small, the settling in and the tidying up. This is where the danger lies. A thing in perfect order can move only one way, toward disorder. People stop watching once a matter is settled, and that is when they are most exposed. So the good fortune comes at the start of this time, and the disorder at its end.

Think about trouble while there is none, so you are ready when it comes. Keep going in the small things the way you did in the large ones. Think of the people who depend on what you have built, and put their good before your own. Know more than you use, and keep some of your strength back.

Your eyes and ears do the work now. The eyes go over what is finished. The ears catch the first drip of a leak. Sit the way someone sits when the day's work is done, with loose hands and open ears.

### Judgement
- After completion. Success in small things, and it goes well for the one who keeps on.
- At the beginning good fortune. At the end, disorder.

### Image
- Water over fire: the pot on the flame, in balance for as long as it is watched.
- So the wise take thought of trouble before it comes, and are ready for it in advance.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ the wheels held back, and the fox with its tail in the water. → becomes Hexagram 39, Obstruction.
You brake the wheels at the water's edge, and the fox's tail is wet from the crossing. The work is done and the next has not begun. The wheels skid because you are ready to go before it is time. Go slowly until you know where you are heading, and no blame comes of it.

**Line 2** · _image:_ a woman who has lost the curtain of her carriage. → becomes Hexagram 5, Waiting.
The curtain is gone from your carriage, and people can see in. Something that kept you private is lost, and you want to run after it. Chasing it would cost you your balance. Left alone, it comes back by the seventh day. Stay where you are and let it return.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the great ancestor who took three years to subdue the far country. → becomes Hexagram 3, Difficulty at the Beginning.
The old king takes three years to win the far country, and he is worn out by it. Some things are finished only by a long campaign, well past the point where the end seemed near. Leave small-minded people out of it, since they can neither see the plan nor help carry it. Call on the unusual helper, and give it the years it takes.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the finest clothes turning to rags, and rags kept ready for a leak. → becomes Hexagram 49, Revolution.
The finest clothes turn to rags, so keep rags ready for the leak. In the middle of plenty something is already going thin. Doubt is useful here, the kind that looks for the drip before the boat fills. Be careful all day long while things are good.

**Line 5** · _image:_ the neighbour in the east who kills an ox, the neighbour in the west with a small offering. → becomes Hexagram 36, Darkening of the Light.
One neighbour kills an ox for the offering, and the other brings a little. The little is what is blessed. What counts is that you mean what you give, and give it at the right time. A cup already full has no room for anything to arrive. Give the small, honest thing, and the blessing comes in full.

**Line 6** · _image:_ the head gone under the water. → becomes Hexagram 37, The Family.
Your head goes under the water at the end of the crossing. You kept going at full pace after the work was done, unable to stop. Now what you gained is slipping back into the river. Nothing here lasts, and that is the warning. Leave a finished thing while leaving is still your choice.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Doubt · _Gift:_ Inquiry · _Siddhi:_ Truth

### Shadow — Doubt
Uncertainty is a pressure most people cannot bear. Whatever you settle, the doubt moves on to the next thing, and nothing ends it. So people take on a firm set of beliefs and hold them up against the doubt like a wall. Anyone who offers to end your uncertainty for good is selling you something false. Doubt is not the enemy. It turns harmful when you make it personal. Pushed out, it falls on the people closest to you. Turned in, it becomes doubt about yourself.

Here the mind works in the wrong order. Shown a sunset, it hears only its own words about it and is already thinking of tomorrow. Take a thing in first, and examine it after.

### Repressive nature — Self-Doubt
Held down, the doubt turns on the one holding it and runs on comparison. They measure themselves against everyone, come up short, then believe the measuring. Fear fixes it there, and it eats away at them as a steady worry. Nothing they do or say is ever good enough.

### Reactive nature — Suspicion
Let out, the same doubt becomes suspicion, and it falls on those closest. They doubt a partner, a boss, even their own child. The other person grows defensive, which looks like proof, so every round feeds the next. They call it being sceptical. A true sceptic has not made up their mind. This one made it up long ago.

### Gift — Inquiry
Inquiry is doubt put to work. You can ask all your life without needing an answer to end it. A mind that fixes on one answer as the truth must defend it, and someone will always attack. An open mind has nothing to defend. It has the lightness of a child at play.

Ask deeply enough into anything and you find you are part of what you are looking at. From there the asking turns inward. What am I, asks the mind. What is love, asks the heart over and over, and it needs no final answer. A mind kept open grows soft, and an open mind opens the heart.

### Siddhi — Truth
Truth arrives when the question swallows the one asking it. You climbed for years toward an answer and wore yourself out on the way. At the top you found it had been at the foot all along, before you took a step. Trusted far enough, doubt turns out to be the truth itself, and the doubter dissolves into it.

Then there is rest, like sitting under a tree on a warm evening after walking all day. Every sigh brings you nearer to it, and so does every time your face softens. It is your plain state, closer than your own heartbeat. It is there at every step of the way.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 63, Curiosity
Gate 63 sits at the top of your head and asks what comes next. It looks ahead to see which path will hold, what to follow and what to leave. It works by testing the next step before you take it, and it can tell a real teacher from a fake. Its fear is being wrong. Under the fear is a craving for certainty that no next step can meet.

### Where it lives — the Head Center
The Head Center sits at the crown and is where questions start. It presses for an answer and cannot act on one, so every question it raises is carried down to be worked on somewhere else.

About three people in ten have this centre defined, and they always know what they think. Ask them where to eat and they already have an answer. Their thinking runs within set lines, so what they cannot picture they tend to refuse at first, and they are not always right. The other seven in ten have it open. They can see every side of a question and hold no fixed view. A thought does not stay with them for long, and a whole answer sometimes drops in from nowhere. A mind at rest is possible for them.

### What completes it — the Channel of Logic (63–4)
A question alone is only pressure. Gate 4, in the mind just below, is the drive to turn a doubt into a formula. Together they make the Channel of Logic, which turns a doubt into a way of checking. It finds the flaw others miss, then proves a claim or drops it. It works best when someone has asked for the answer. Gate 4 may be in you or in a person close by, and the question is answered only when both are present.

## BODY

### Physiology
Sleep is the reason this energy is given to the pineal gland. The gland is the part of you that says the day is finished. About two hours before your usual bedtime it begins to tell your body that night has come. It says only that it is night, and leaves the sleeping to you. On some evenings your head lets go when it hears this, and on others the questions go on.

It keeps a clock that runs a little longer than a day. Each morning the first light through your eyes sets it again. Kept in dim light, a person drifts later day by day, and a morning outdoors puts it right. In animals that breed by the season, the length of its night is how the body knows the time of year.

### Amino acid
The doubt in you has a chemistry, and one substance in it is proline. Every protein in you is built from twenty pieces, and this is the one that turns the chain wherever it sits. When fear takes hold of doubt, heavy hormones go through you. Self-doubt taken all the way drains the body at that level, until your strength is simply gone.

Every person carries the same discomfort of not remembering who they truly are, and all people are equal in this one suffering. You share its chemistry with three other energies.

## RELATIONS

_UL 64 is this card's pair, its inverse and its partner at once: the crossing still ahead, where this one has it behind._

### Pair — UL 64, Communion (Before Completion)
This energy asks how a finished thing works, and it runs out when the last question is answered. Communion is where it goes next: the open time before completion, where a new picture forms. Open it when every question has been answered and something new is waiting to begin. Each finish is the start of the next crossing.

### Inverse — UL 64, Communion (Before Completion)
Turn this energy end for end and what is finished becomes what is not yet done. Communion is the same river seen from the bank not yet reached. Open it when the order you made starts to feel too tidy to trust. Nothing stays finished for long.

### Programming partner — UL 64, Communion
This energy moves one step at a time, testing each before the next. Communion holds what it lacks: the whole picture that arrives at once and cannot be taken apart. The mind that asks feeds the mind that imagines, and neither knows much without the other. Open it when your questions have run out and nothing new has come.

### Codon ring — Codon Ring of Divinity
This energy asks, and asking is how every search begins. It belongs to a family of four, the Ring of Divinity, whose theme is suffering that opens you. UL 22, Treasure of the Way, carries grace. UL 36, Crystal Creation, carries the dark stretch that tests you. UL 37, Journey Home, carries the warmth of a family. This one carries the first question.

### Tarot
Five of the tarot's major cards, its Arcana, arrive through the ring and the two trigrams.

- **Ring Arcana — V · The Hierophant** (Tradition, Teach): The Hierophant hands down what has been tested, and doubt keeps the teaching honest.
- **Water (upper) — II · The High Priestess** (Perceive, Listen): The High Priestess listens before she answers, which is the order a busy mind forgets.
- **Water (upper) — XII · The Hanged Man** (Pause for Perspective): The Hanged Man waits upside down, because pushing on after the crossing would spill it.
- **Fire (lower) — IV · The Emperor** (Dominion, Lead): The Emperor sets each thing in its place, the order a finished work is made of.
- **Fire (lower) — XIX · The Sun** (Glory, Clarity): The Sun shows a thing exactly as it is, the plain light that checks the work.

### Immortals
Water above carries Li Tie Guai, the healer who keeps his medicine in a gourd for the day it is needed. Fire below carries Lu Dong Bin, the scholar who turned his sword on his own delusion. One keeps the cure ready, and the other cuts through a false certainty.

### Deeper correlation
- **Sky — Taurus:** the fixed earth that keeps what has been built, steady and in no hurry to chase the next thing.
- **Hebrew letter — Vau, the Nail:** the nail that fastens one thing to another, so what is finished does not come loose.
