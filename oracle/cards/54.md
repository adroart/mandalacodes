# UL 54 — Everlasting Bounty

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Wanting More · Coming In Low · The Climb · Never Enough · Standing in Your Worth · Turned Inward · Rising Slowly

Wanting more is this energy, the drive in you to rise above the place you were given. It is one of the great pressures that move people, and there is nothing wrong with it: it built the cities. You feel it as a want that does not ask whether to want, only what is next. It is strongest of all in love, where one taste makes you reach for more.

Carried well, the want keeps its trust. Greed is the same wanting with the trust gone, and it gives itself away: the desperation in it can be sensed by others before you have said a word, so the door you wanted opened closes from the inside. A want that will trade its own honesty for what it wants loses what it wanted. So the part of you that says I want is not the enemy here. It is fuel, the engine that will one day carry you inward, and the work is to refine it rather than to feed it or fight it.

Nothing out there fills the hole the wanting comes from. Sooner or later the want turns round to face inward, and that is when things get interesting. Until then the promise of some future fullness keeps you from the fullness around you now.

## ICHING

### Combination
Thunder over Lake: the storm breaking over still water, the eldest son above the youngest daughter. A young woman goes to the house of an older man at her own family's asking, not fetched, and comes in beneath a wife already there. The traditional character sets a woman beside not-yet: someone who has arrived before her time, in a house already arranged.

### Upper trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder is the shock that starts things. It comes up out of the ground in spring, the first sound of the year, and everything asleep under the field wakes. In a family it is the eldest son, the one who moves first. In you it is the jolt: the moment the wanting turns into a move, the bud that breaks its skin. Here it stands above, so it is the house you are moving into, the older force with the standing, the noise you will have to live beside. Its risk is alarm, when what shook you awake keeps shaking, so that you act only to make the trembling stop.

### Lower trigram — Lake (Dui)
Lake is the open water, the marsh where the streams gather, with the mist rising off it in the morning. It is easy, pleasant, free, the part of you that meets people and delights in them; in a family it is the youngest daughter. In you it is the pull toward what pleases: you are drawn to the higher place, the better house, by delight, before you have thought. Here it stands below, so it is the one who wants in, moved from underneath by attraction. Its risk is being taken lightly, when the delight that draws you is all that anyone sees of you.

### Reading
You have come into a place below the one you wanted, and the wanting has not gone quiet. The house was already arranged when you arrived, someone else holds the first place in it, and you were not sent for; you came. Set out from here to force the matter and it goes wrong. Nothing pushed will further you. A change has come that was not in your hands, and you are inside it now.

What it asks of you is to know your worth and stand in it quietly. When you are sure of it you need not convince anyone, so win your own approval first. Do not take the first offer, because a delay here works for you. Do not raise yourself by any means that is beneath you; the ways that are dignified are the only ones that hold. Whatever happens, let nobody see you lose your composure. You begin with no power. By the end you are the one holding it, if you spend your effort in moderation and pay only the cost you decided you could pay.

In the body this energy is the feet, the part of you that carries you into a room and shows, before you speak, whether you came to stand or to grab. Notice where you are holding yourself now. If the weight is on your toes you are leaning at the next place. Set the whole foot down where you are.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the maiden draws out the time; a late marriage comes in its season. → becomes Hexagram 19, Approach.

This is what a rise made for its own sake comes to. Nothing here will further you. Before you go through with a hollow thing, see what it holds.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Greed · _Gift:_ Aspiration · _Siddhi:_ Ascension

### Shadow — Greed
Greed is the wanting with the trust taken out. Doves on a square look like peace until you watch them feed: they live on scraps and fight each other for every fly. That is the wanting at its lowest, base and hungry, each one reaching for a higher place in the order. In you it is the drive to be at the top, and to look it, in the car or the house that says where you stand. It is greediest in love: you taste it, want more, reach, it goes, so you try another and blame them when it goes again. At the root of it is fear, that if you do not rise you will not survive, and fear makes the wanting grab, so that it trades its own honesty in a moment for what it is after. That is its downfall. It cannot trust its allies, since they want the same things.

None of this is wrong. A child says I want that, and the wanting is honest. Seen for what it is, the greed shows the lack it comes from, a lost sense of being part of something, and that seeing is what turns it round.

### Repressive nature — Unambitious
This person began with ardour and gave up. Haunted by the fear of never reaching the goal, they decided it was better not to set out, so the drive went passive, then dark. Some turn the same refusal on the body, feeding the spirit while starving the flesh.

### Reactive nature — Greedy
This one has let the wanting run with no brake. Owning more is their rage made visible; the hunger feeds itself and never eats. They may get rich. What they lack is any feeling that their life is of use to anyone, and with the heart closed they see other people as things to own.

### Gift — Aspiration
Aspiration is the wanting with its trust kept, pointed higher. The word is a cousin of breathing: you take in the raw fuel of the want and refine it, so that what comes out is a reach for something higher than any goal, without needing to know what the higher thing is. Ambition is for a particular thing. This is wider, and it begins in honesty about the will to rise in you.

What changes is where the energy goes. At its lowest, everything gained went back into gaining more; here, what you gather is passed down to the ones below you, the way a tree's fruit feeds its own roots. The question that cuts through it is how you can be of use. It works in the flesh: fear has a smell, so does support, and someone who has stopped grabbing puts a room at ease before they speak. Money kept moving makes more; held too long, it stops. The work is to bring the higher thing down into ordinary rooms, for the ones stuck in lives of compromise who need to see that more is possible, and to be brought back to earth whenever the reach overheats.

### Siddhi — Ascension
The climb goes on past every ladder. When the social one runs out, the same pressure that wanted the top keeps rising, up through the body now, the thing the old stories called a serpent coiled at the base of the spine. No step can be skipped. Every hard thing in your life has to be passed through the heart, and the one person who always causes trouble cannot be walked around. The wanting is not overcome; it is softened, the way rocks in the wind lose their edges.

The road up is a warrior's road, all effort, all sacrifice; just short of the top you find that what you have become is pure yielding. To reach the highest you go first to the lowest. At the end the body itself changes, in a heat like liquid fire, then goes still, and there is no more technique. One aspires, and sooner or later, one ascends.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 54, Ambition
Rising is a pressure in the body, and Gate 54 is where it enters: the drive to move up in life, to better your situation, to reach a higher standing than the one you have. It comes with a feel for how the ladder is built. What it does not come with is a sense of what is fair in the climbing, so that is yours to supply. For some the ladder is money or rank; for others it is a skill, or how deep their understanding goes.

From outside, a life driven like this can look like never being satisfied, and from inside that is how it feels: the present is never enough, other people's positions are measured against yours, the sense of never having enough sits under everything. The pull is to let the climb take over the rest of the life, to use people as steps, to work until you burn out. People who carry this all their lives learn to want what answers something in them rather than what is merely higher, to hold an inspiration until it is ripe rather than forcing it into shape, and to be content in the same breath as ambitious.

### Where it lives — the Root Center
Low in the trunk, at the very bottom of you, is the Root Centre, where this wanting is made. It is the foundation the rest stands on; all life begins with its stress, the pressure to keep going, to adapt, and of its nine pressures this one is the pressure to rise. It is a motor with a pulse. It switches on and off in its own time; when it is off no dream of yours can be forced, however clear the dream.

In about six people in ten this centre is switched on, and the drive to rise is theirs to keep. They are at ease with the stress it takes to get somewhere, and other people's pressure to act does not move them; what wears them down is a dream pushed on the wrong day. In the other four it is not, so the pressure comes in from whoever is near, louder than it was, then goes when they go. For someone like that, big dreams are still real, and the frustration is trying to make them happen by pushing, which never works when the drive was borrowed. What they find, over a life, is which dreams are theirs and which came in with whoever was near.

### What completes it — the Channel of Transformation (32–54)
The drive to rise, alone, burns whatever it climbs. Its other half is Gate 32, in the gut, the sense of what will last and what will not. Together they make the Channel of Transformation: the motivation to improve a life, in business or on the way to waking up, driven underneath by survival. Off its track the fear of failing drives you to overwork, so the success that was yours slips away. Whether the other half is in you or in someone near you, the rise only holds where the two meet.

## BODY

### Physiology
The tailbone is where your body keeps this energy, and the reason is the base. It is the last bone of the spine, the bottom of the column, curving forward under you. It is what is left of a tail: at five or six weeks after you were conceived you had one, ten or twelve segments long; over the next weeks it was taken back into you, and this stayed. The floor of your body is anchored to it. The two chains of nerves that run down either side of your spine meet in front of it and become one. Everything that has ever risen in you rose from here.

Sit upright on a hard chair and the weight is on the two bones either side of it, with the tailbone hanging free between them. Slump, lean back, and it takes the weight. It is the bottom of you, taking the load the moment you stop holding yourself up.

### Amino acid
The drive in you to rise runs on a chemistry it shares with its kin, and the chemistry is serine. Serine is one of the twenty pieces every part of you is built from, and one of its carbons goes into the letters of your DNA. In you the pressure it belongs to points two ways. Turned outward, it is the urge to gather more than you have. Turned inward, the same pressure is the urge for meaning, and it is one pressure, not two.

There are six roads out of the seeking, and this one in you is the road of effort: you yoke yourself to a practice or a teacher, then climb by degrees, refined a little at each step. The road begins in the body, at its foundation, before it goes anywhere higher.

### Tarot
Everything in this card is reaching for something higher, and the Tarot marks that reach at three seats. The Ring of Seeking gives the Hermit, the one who climbs the mountain alone with a lamp, seeking by his own effort. The two trigrams give their own faces: Thunder above as the Tower, the shock that breaks the false structure open; Lake below as the Star, the quiet hope that draws the seeker upward. The work this asks for is unglamorous: stop defending the hollow towers, and keep walking toward the light that is genuinely higher.

One walked out of the court he was born into, the other waved his fan over the dead, so the rise that counts begins by giving up borrowed rank and going down first.
