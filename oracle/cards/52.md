## CODE

_Keywords:_ Stopping · Patience · Deep Breath · Inner Quiet · Right Timing · Focus · The Long View

You do not turn around. The room fills up behind you, the phone lights, someone says your name in the voice that means now, and you finish the breath you were on first. Your weight is down through your heels and the base of you is soft, and from there nothing can hurry you. Whatever needs doing will still need doing in a minute. People slow down near you without being told, and the things that looked urgent sort themselves into an order you can see.

The same weight that rests so easily can lock instead, and then you are an engine idling in a shut garage: all that fuel burning, the fumes thickening, the needle climbing, and not one wheel turning. From outside it can pass for patience. Inside, every minute is owed to something, and you either rev to get ahead of the clock or sit jammed with the door shut, breathing what you already burned.

Let the weight down. Let it fall through the seat and the heels until the breath follows it to the floor of you and the floor lets go. Your stillness is your drive, held low until it finds its aim. Nothing is lost in the waiting. The growing is going on under the ground where you cannot see it, and when the moment comes to move it will be as plain as a lamp on a hill at night. Until then, stand where you are. This is the place.

## ICHING

### Combination
Mountain over Mountain: the same peak set on itself, and nothing between the two of them moves. The old name for it is a stare, an eye fixed on the one near thing in front of it, which is what stopping looks like from outside. Upper and lower do not answer each other; nothing climbs. Out of that comes the scene: someone standing with the back held still, walking their own courtyard, seeing no one in it. The stop is in the right place, and so it costs nothing.

### Upper trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain, Gen. One firm line resting over two yielding ones, the weight risen as far as it can and stopped there. The youngest son, the limit, the foot that ceases walking. Above, this trigram is the summit you cannot see past, the stillness that closes the day.

### Lower trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain, Gen. The same firm line over the same yielding ground, repeated below. The foot set down, the back turned on what is behind. Below, this trigram is the base of the stillness, the ground that holds so nothing is dragged forward before its time.

### Reading
Something in your situation has reached its edge, and the move now is the one that looks like no move at all. Every other part of you reaches for what it wants, the eyes after what they see, the hands after what they can take. The back reaches for nothing. It only has to stand straight and carry you, and if you rest your attention there the wanting loses its grip, and you can walk through your own house with people in every room and not be pulled at by any of them. Your foot has stopped because it has arrived. You are not straining to stay.

The lines climb your body to show the difference between stopping and seizing. At the toes the stop is easy and nothing has gone wrong yet. At the calves you are halted while the one ahead of you keeps going, and your heart is sour with it. At the waist the stop turns into injury, the spine locked, the top of you cut off from the bottom, heat smoking up into the chest. Above that it eases. The trunk governs itself, the jaw comes to rest and the words fall into order, and at the top the whole of you is still without anything holding it.

Stop low. Stop at the back and the quiet goes all the way down; stop at the waist and you have not stopped, only clamped, and a deeper layer hears the clamp's other name, which is stress.

### Judgement
- Keep still at the back, and the body drops out of mind.
- Walk your own courtyard and see no one there.
- No fault.
- Stop when it is the season for stopping, move when it is the season for moving, and the way stays bright.

### Image
- Mountains stand joined, one beside the other.
- Thought keeps to the place it stands in and does not go out past it.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the waist locked, the spine wrenched, the heart smoking. → becomes Hexagram 23, Splitting Apart.
Here is the stop that does harm. You have clamped at the waist, the one place built to bend, and the top of you no longer speaks to the bottom. What could not go down goes up instead, into the chest, where it smoulders as the craving to win and be done. Force in the costume of stillness. Unclench, and let the stop go lower before it breaks you in half.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the trunk held still, the self governed. → becomes Hexagram 56, The Wanderer.
The stillness has reached your trunk, the centre of you, and here it is real. You keep your own house. You answer for what is yours and push nothing onto anyone else, and since most faults start in the body, in the reach and the flinch, stopped there they never become anything. No blame in this, only the quiet of someone who governs themselves without needing to be strong.

**Line 5** · _image:_ the jaws at rest, the words in order. → becomes Hexagram 53, Development.
The stop has come up to the mouth. The jaw rests, so it no longer opens at random, and what you say has a sequence to it, weighed before it leaves you. Balanced here, you can measure a sentence before it is spoken. Whatever you have regretted saying dissolves. Keep the hardest silence, the one about what matters most to you, and speak only when the words have found their order.

**Line 6** · _image:_ stillness kept generously, through to the end. → becomes Hexagram 15, Modesty.
At the top of the doubled mountain the stop is complete, inside and outside at one rest. Nothing is being held down. The craving for a quick finish has simply gone, and what remains is generous and thorough. Resting here you are steady as the peak and lasting as the ground, and it brings good fortune, because a beginning is finished and an end is made whole.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Stress · _Gift:_ Restraint · _Siddhi:_ Stillness

### Shadow — Stress
Stress is a pressure you did not make and cannot walk out of. It is in the room before you are, a field every person alive adds their fear to, and in a city you are never out of it, not even alone in your own bed. What it does first is raise the breath. Somewhere in growing up the breath leaves the belly and moves to the top of the chest, thin and quick, and from there the whole day runs on short supply. Time changes shape. It stops being a river you are standing in and becomes something you are behind on, too fast or too slow and never yours.

What you do with it, usually, is hand it to the mind. The mind agrees to fix it and starts running, and the running is the stress, so every plan it makes to end the pressure feeds the pressure. That loop can go for years. You push through the discomfort, you get used to the alarm, you stop hearing it ring.

The alarm was never the enemy. The body sounds it when your rhythm falls out of step with the larger one around you, and it is asking one thing: slow down, breathe low, come back to the base of yourself. Trust the discomfort long enough to hear what it is for.

### Repressive nature — Stuck
Under it, one kind of person folds. The pressure lands and they go down, and a heavy jammed feeling settles into the body and stays, and they read it as a wall when it is a pace they have not agreed to yet. The way out of this one is to be of use to someone else.

### Reactive nature — Restless
The other kind cannot sit down. They outrun the pressure by staying busy, filling every hour, one step ahead of the quiet that would let them feel how shallow the breath has gone, and the frustration under it lands on whoever is nearest. It passes for drive. What fuels it burns out first.

### Gift — Restraint
Restraint sounds like a brake, and it is closer to the reason anything alive lasts. Everything that grows has its own speed. A sunflower seed and a yew seed are much the same size; one is a sunflower by autumn, the other takes ten years to reach that height and then lives for thousands, and hurrying either disturbs the ground it was sown in. Restraint is knowing this in the body. No hurry, and you mean it.

It shows up as the long view. You care how things move across seasons rather than minutes, so you take your time as a matter of course, and the pause everyone else panics in is where your growing happens, because the buds form in the outer pause. You cannot be stuck while you are still in touch with what you meant. You can only be waiting.

Anyone living this way is an anchor, and you can feel it from across a room. An anchor does not hold the ship out of fear. It lets the ship rest and find its bearings, and the calm goes out from that person into whoever is near without a word said. Then clarity comes up on its own, and the way forward is obvious.

One question does most of the work: how can you be of the greatest use? Answer it and the rest paces itself.

### Siddhi — Stillness
Turn any energy far enough either way and you reach the same place. Slowed to nothing, it stops. Quickened past all measure, it merges, which is also a stop. Stillness is what waits at both ends, and it is full, more alive than whatever it replaced.

The body goes on moving, rebuilt cell by cell every hour, and underneath the movement something does not move at all. Thoughts arrive with gaps between them. A feeling rises so slowly you could reach out and turn it over before it breaks the surface, then spreads through you like a coin dropped in a pond and dissolves back into the quiet it came from. There is only the breath, going in and out on its own, and the point at the centre it goes in and out of.

Nothing in you wants back into the drama. You watch it from a little way off. Near someone this still, other people's thinking falls quiet without being told to and time seems to widen, and whatever moves from here moves only to touch the same stillness in someone else.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 52, Stillness
You feel it as a charge held low in you with nowhere yet to go. The pressure is real and large, enough to drive a whole life, and what it wants is a target: the bow is drawn and the arrow has not been told where. Meanwhile you can see a great deal. The whole field opens in front of you at once, the shape of what needs to happen and the order it needs to happen in, and the pull is to freeze in front of it, spread thin across everything you can see, or to spend the charge on whatever is nearest just to be rid of it.

From outside this gets called slowness. Detached, they say, lost in your own world, unable to switch off once you have started. What they are watching is someone refusing to fire until the aim is true, and then not stopping once it is.

### Where it lives — the Root Center
The pressure sits low, at the very bottom of the trunk, in the part of you that starts things. It is a motor, and one that pulses rather than runs: on, and you could move mountains, then off, and you cannot make yourself do what you know you could do yesterday. Living with it means living at the mercy of that timing. When the pulse is on and no target has been found the charge backs up, and you feel rattled, agitated for no reason you can name, and the urge is to force it down any channel at all just to relieve the pressure. That is stress, felt as a place.

What the placement asks is strange for a pressure this strong: do not spend it. Let the pulse pass through you and keep the weight low while it does. The aim comes up from below when it comes, never down from the head, and the waiting is the work. The stillness here is the drive itself, kept low until it has somewhere to go.

### What completes it — the Channel of Concentration (52–9)
The pressure needs a partner, and the partner is the part of you that narrows, the focus of Gate 9. It answers with a gut yes or a gut no before any reasoning, and it picks one detail out of the whole field and stays on it. When your held charge meets that narrowing, the pulse gets translated into something methodical: a logical, patient concentration that can sit on one task for hours while the room forgets you are in it. Other people feel it. Concentration like this pulls whoever is nearby into the same track.

Between the two ends there is a waiting that cannot be shortened. You have the pressure; the belly has not yet said which thing. That gap is the dynamic stillness the pairing runs on, the charge held without being spent while the yes finds its object. Land on the wrong thing and you get obsession, hours poured into a task that was only ever there to relieve the pressure. Land on the right one and the stress you were carrying turns into work.

## BODY

### Physiology
The tissue under all of this is a small sling of muscle at the very bottom of the trunk, between the legs, where the two halves of the pelvis meet: the perineum. It is a second diaphragm. As the lungs fill, the floor of the pelvis drops to make room, and as they empty it lifts, so every breath that goes all the way down arrives here and every breath that goes all the way out starts here. At the far end of the spine the soft spot at the crown answers it, and between the two of them hangs the whole column that stands you up.

Put your attention on that muscle now and see whether it is tight. It usually is, and letting it go slack is the simplest thing this energy asks of you: the breath finds the floor again on its own, and the pressure that was running you unhooks without a decision being made.

Left tight, it does the opposite. Fear grabs the perineum before you have registered a threat, and the grip does not stay put; it travels up the spine the way a cramp travels, into the neck and the jaw and the skull, tightening the whole column and lifting the breath out of the belly until a life is being run on the top quarter of the lungs. Held long enough, the grip stops feeling like a grip and starts feeling like you.

### Amino acid
Serine is where a protein is told to wait. Along the chain of a protein there are a few places where the cell can attach a small charged group and take it off again, and most of those places are a serine: add the group and the protein changes shape and stops, or starts; take it away and it goes back to what it was. Much of what the cell holds in check, it holds at a serine. Five other codes are built on the same amino acid, and in you it is the switch that keeps a thing from firing until it is meant to.

So the stop has a floor smaller than a cell: one side chain that can be held or released. Your stillness is your drive, held low until it finds its aim.

### Immortals
Zhang Guo Lao holds both peaks here, since the two are the same. He is the oldest of the eight, the recluse who was summoned to court by emperors and kept not going, and who rode his white donkey facing its tail. At night he folded the donkey flat like a sheet of paper and put it in his sleeve. In the morning a mouthful of water brought it back. Doubled here he makes two pictures of the same stop: the man who does not go when called, and the ride that can be folded away and unfolded again with nothing lost.
