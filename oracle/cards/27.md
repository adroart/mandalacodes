# UL 27 — Inner Majesty

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Caring for Your Own · Fed Before You Feed · The Full Heart · The Tight Season · Nothing Wanted Back · Knowing Whose Load Is Yours · Time Given Freely

Nourishment is the energy here, the care in you for whatever cannot yet feed itself. Everything alive is fed by something else, and each living thing feeds another in turn. Giving and receiving are one law seen from two sides. This care is the hand that reaches out before you have thought.

Nourishment comes from a full heart, so it has to begin with you. Looking after yourself cannot wait for plenty, because by that measure there is never enough. Ask what would help the larger whole you are part of, rather than what would serve you. Generosity has little to do with what you own; what you give is your time. Give it freely and it stretches. A day with an open heart runs like a river through wide country. A day spent holding on is over before you have drawn a breath.

Give to what will grow. There are seasons when life closes in, every day is worry, and you have no room left for anyone else. In that season you tell yourself you will give once you have enough to spare, and that day never comes. Holding on to stay safe is what keeps you hungry, because a life sealed against loss shuts food out too. Open your hand a little, and the law turns round: what you give freely fills you.

## ICHING

### Combination
Mountain sits over Thunder. Stillness rests on top of movement. The hexagram is interpreted as a mouth: the solid lines above and below are the jaws, and the broken lines between them are the teeth. Feeding is its whole meaning.


### Upper trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain is the stillness on top. In you it is the part that knows when to stop taking, whether food, work or what people ask of you. Without it everything gets in.


### Lower trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder is the movement underneath. In you it is appetite, the reach for what you need before you have thought. Without the stillness above it the reaching never ends. You take everything and stay hungry.


### Reading
You have gathered something, and now the time has come to eat from it and to feed others with it. That is where you stand: at the mouth, where food goes in and words come out. The whole question of this time is what you let through in either direction. Heaven and earth feed every creature without being asked. The wise feed the ones worth feeding, so that food reaches everyone in the end.

This time asks for temperance, in what you eat and drink and in what you say, because words are food too. What you let out feeds the people around you or poisons them. Keep to the kind who feed rather than take. Some people beg from those above them when they should be feeding the ones below. They will drift away from you for a while, and that is part of the season.

In your body, nourishment is the mouth. Notice how you hold yourself now: is the jaw set, and is the mouth already open for what comes next? Sit with the jaw loose and the lips closed, and chew what you take in before you swallow it.

### Judgement
- Nourishment. Holding to what is right, it goes well.
- Watch what you feed, and watch what you reach for to fill your own mouth.
- What fills the mouth is sought from its source, and the source is yourself.

### Image

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ the tortoise that lives on air set down, and a mouth watering at another's plate. → becomes Hexagram 23, Splitting Apart.
The tortoise needs nothing, because it lives on air. You have set that tortoise down to stare at what someone else is eating. The wanting makes your own share feel like too little. Nobody respects a mouth hanging open at another's table. Turn back to what is yours, and eat from that.

**Line 2** · _image:_ food sought from the hill-top, against the way it flows. → becomes Hexagram 41, Decrease.
Food should flow down from the strong to the weak. You are reaching up the hill for it instead, toward someone who has nothing to spare either. Reaching that way costs you the company you had, because you turned away from the ones who could have walked with you. Ask nothing of the hill, and feed yourself from where you stand.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the back turned on what feeds, for ten years. → becomes Hexagram 22, Grace.
You have turned your back on what truly feeds you, and care only for food and clothing. Keep that up with a will and it ends badly, because the part you leave hungry is what keeps you alive. Ten years can pass that way with nothing to show for it. Stop now, while the cost is ten years and not the whole of a life.

**Line 4** · _image:_ a tiger's stare, fixed and hungry, and no fault in it. → becomes Hexagram 21, Biting Through.
Now the hunger is right. You look down for what you need the way a tiger looks, unblinking and ready to spring, and there is nothing wrong in that. To ask below you for the strength you lack is no shame, so long as you ask in earnest and force nothing. Keep your eyes on what feeds you, and go when it moves.

**Line 5** · _image:_ off the usual road, and holding the ground where you stand. → becomes Hexagram 42, Increase.
You are off the usual road, responsible for people you do not have the means to feed. Stay upright where you are, and lean on the one above you who has what you lack. Leaning is no weakness; it is how the wise find what is right. This is no season for the big leap, so keep what is in place and do not cross the water yet.

**Line 6** · _image:_ the one the food comes from, in a high and dangerous place. → becomes Hexagram 24, Return.
You are the one the food comes from now, and everyone below you eats from your hand. The place is dangerous because so much rests on it, and it goes well since you know that. You have fed others in a hard time, so you can do it in any season without waiting to be told. The big leap is yours now, so make it.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Selfishness · _Gift:_ Altruism · _Siddhi:_ Selflessness

### Shadow — Selfishness
Everything alive eats something else, and one thing's death is another's birth. The chain runs all the way up, and you are in it. Holding on to what you have is the oldest move in that chain, the one that kept your blood line alive. It is not a sin, whatever you were told. Selfishness is a small room, where life is tight and worry runs under everything. The pain inside takes up the space that thinking of anyone else would need. At the table you eat with your arm around the plate.

You came into the world unselfish, and a house or a school taught you to take. What you learned you can unlearn. Under the selfishness is a missing purpose, a life with no reason in it bigger than itself. People will do almost anything to avoid feeling that gap. Once they went to war to fill it; now they go shopping. Sit in the small room long enough to ask what the larger reason would be, and the walls begin to move back.

### Repressive nature — Self-Sacrificing
This person gives everything away, and none of it comes from the heart. What they hand over is their own strength, with no limit set. So people use them, or resent the strain in the giving. A thin guilt runs under all of it. Slowly the body pays for it.

### Reactive nature — Self-Centred
Here the giving comes with a hook in it: this person gives to get something back. It looks generous until the return does not come, and then the anger underneath breaks the surface. That shock is the point of the hook. That giving comes from the head, never the heart.

### Gift — Altruism
Care that wants nothing back is your nature, and nobody taught it to you. When one dolphin in a pod is in trouble, the whole pod turns toward it at once, with no decision taken. The knowing passes through all of them as if they were one body. An older one will give its life for a younger without a pause. That is the shape this care takes in you. When your arm comes off the plate it goes to the middle of the table, and you are fuller for it.

This care knows, untaught, what is worth giving to whom. It will not feed anyone's wish to stay helpless. It runs on the same pull that keeps a parent beside a child through the first seven years, the ones that shape everything. A wound of your own from that age heals as you give what you did not get. What comes off you then is trust, and before you speak people near you lower their guard, some for the first time in their lives. Purpose wells up out of this like warm water from a spring, and spreads to whoever stands close.

### Siddhi — Selflessness
There is nobody at the head of the table now, and the feeding goes on with no one doing it. What remains is a hunger the body always carries, for a harmony so complete that food is only its outer sign. This is different from doing good. The ones who help so that others see them, or to cover a pain of their own, still have a self doing the helping. Here no self remains, so such people go unnoticed, as ordinary as anyone in the street.

These people give without end and never run dry, fed by the love around them. Where an ounce of life remains in anything, this care reaches it. From here the world feeding on itself sounds like music, and love is all they hear. You arrive with no self and leave with none, and the one you seem to have in between is a dream.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 27, Caring
Low in you sits the pressure to look after what is yours. Gate 27 is the name for it. It drives you to feed and protect the people you are responsible for, yourself among them, before anyone has asked. This is the pull that checks the door at night and counts the ones asleep. Nobody has to tell you, and if the pull has nowhere to go you are restless without knowing why.

Left unwatched, the same pull feeds people who did not need it and holds them so close they cannot grow. It takes on loads that never belonged to you, until you are tired, unthanked and sour. The question is whose problem this is. Those who live with it learn to treat the other person as able. They let go with love, so the other can find their own strength. They also learn that looking after themselves is not the selfish part.

### Where it lives — the Sacral Center
This care sits in the Sacral centre, low in the body between the navel and the thighs, the strongest energy you have. It brings a child into the world, raises it, feeds it and keeps a house. It does not decide; it answers, with a yes that lifts you or a no that leaves you flat. In anyone who carries it, the energy is warm and motherly. A true yes gives it back as fast as you spend it. A yes out of guilt drains it. People learn to take from it, because a yes they talked you into looks like your own.

About seven people in ten have this caring energy running on its own, all the time. They can trust their gut about who is theirs to look after. The risk for them is the role of the one who is always there. They keep it up long after the wanting has gone, until they get ill and have to stop. The other three in ten have no caring energy of their own running, and borrow it from the people around them. They can care for a whole household for a while, then find themselves empty, because they missed the moment when enough became too much. What they have instead is the pause, the moment they can stop and see that someone has had enough.

### What completes it — the Channel of Preservation (27–50)
On its own, the drive to feed does not know what deserves feeding. What completes it is Gate 50, the part of you that keeps the values a group lives by. It sits in the Spleen, the centre that knows by instinct what is worth keeping. Together they make the Channel of Preservation, and that joining is how a family stays whole across years. Not everyone needs your care, and Gate 50 knows it. It may sit in you or in a person close to you, and the keeping only happens when the two are joined.

## BODY

### Physiology
Feeding has a far end in you, and that end is the sacral plexus. It is a weave of nerves on the inside of the flat bone at the base of your spine. What began at your mouth this morning ends its road on orders from here. Nerves from this weave move the last of what you ate along, and order the letting go when it comes. Low in the back beside it sits the store of life you were born with, which every meal since has added to.

You are doing it now: something you ate is on the last of its road, and this weave moves it without a thought from you. Put your hand on the small of your back, where the bone goes flat. It rests on the part of you that finishes what feeding began.

### Amino acid
This care has a substance in you, and its name is leucine. Your body cannot make it. It comes in with meat, eggs or beans, or not at all. It is the first thing your muscle notices after a meal, and between meals it is not there. The selfishness that shuts you in and the care that gives both run on this one chemistry. The two pull opposite ways, the closing to death and the giving to life.

Give with nothing wanted back, and something real moves low in your body, a current that you can feel. Five other cards in this deck share leucine with this one.

## RELATIONS

_Read alongside UL 28, this is the whole knot of feeding and purpose, the mouth that takes in and the weight that decides whether any of it was worth the taking._

### Pair — UL 28, The Dark Side of Light (Great Preponderance)
These two are nourishment and the question of what it is all for. One is the open mouth, the daily taking in. The other is the load grown too great, the beam that bends under its own weight. Feeding without a purpose overloads you. A purpose without feeding starves you.

### Inverse — its own reflection
Turn this code upside down and it looks the same. It is one of the eight codes that reflect into themselves, an open mouth that reads the same from either end. What that symmetry teaches is steadiness. Whether you are the one being fed or the one feeding, the law is the same: to give is to receive. What you do to the chain, it does to you.

### Programming partner — UL 28, The Dark Side of Light
UL 28, The Dark Side of Light, holds the reason for feeding and is the keeper of purpose and risk. With no reason to live, feeding becomes eating for its own sake. A life without purpose tips into selfishness. Feed the body and find the reason, or neither holds.

### Codon ring — Ring of Life and Death
Within the Ring of Life and Death, this code is the act of feeding, one thing keeping another alive. Five siblings share the ring with it. UL 3 is where a life begins and UL 20 is where you stand fully in it. UL 23 is where it falls apart, UL 24 is where it comes back and UL 42 is where it grows on. Together they hold the turning of life into death and back again.

### Tarot
Two jaws and a ring bring five of the tarot's Arcana to this card. The codon ring brings Death. The Mountain above brings the Hierophant and the Hermit, and the Thunder below carries the Tower and Judgement. Together the five cards do what a mouth does: they take one form down and give it back as another, handing on and breaking open.

- **Ring Arcana — XIII · Death** (Metamorphosis): Here Death is a feeding, the passage where one form goes down so another can rise.
- **Mountain (upper) — V · The Hierophant** (Tradition, Teach): The Hierophant is the one who passes down what feeds a people across the generations, the still upper jaw keeping what is worth handing on.
- **Mountain (upper) — IX · The Hermit** (Wisdom, Right Path): The Hermit feeds himself from within; the tortoise that lives on air is his lesson, the one the person in the first line forgets.
- **Thunder (lower) — XVI · The Tower** (Shock, Upheaval): The Tower is the sudden break from below that empties a hoarding hand and cracks open a closed and starving life.
- **Thunder (lower) — XX · Judgement** (Salvation, Renewal): Judgement is the call that raises new life out of the ground; hunger is that waking, an appetite answering a sound it cannot refuse.

### Immortals
Zhang Guo Lao stands above, the old immortal who rode his mule backward and carried the secret of long life. Cao Guojiu stands below, the one who walked away from the court he inherited. Between them what truly feeds a person comes as two lessons: the span that sustains, and the appetite that stops taking what others hand it.

### Deeper correlation
- **Sky — Scorpio:** the sign that holds death and rebirth as one, the depth that consumes what enters it and remakes it.
- **Hebrew letter — Nun, the Fish:** transformation without loss, life going on through change the way a fish keeps living in moving water.
