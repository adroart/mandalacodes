# UL 27 — Inner Majesty

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Caring for Your Own · Fed Before You Feed · The Full Heart · The Tight Season · Nothing Wanted Back · Knowing Whose Load Is Yours · Time Given Freely

Nourishment is the energy here, the care that moves in you toward whatever cannot yet feed itself. Everything alive is fed by something else, and everything that feeds is fed in turn. Giving and receiving are one law seen from two sides. In you it is the hand that goes out before you have thought about it, to the child or to the one who is sinking, to whatever is small and in your keeping. It lives there, and it lives in how you keep yourself.

Nourishment comes from a full heart, which is why it has to begin with you. Looking after yourself is not a luxury to be earned once there is enough, because by that measure there is never enough; it is what makes the giving real. Then the question in any room is not how you can best serve yourself but what would best serve the larger life you are part of, and the two questions give different answers more often than not. Generosity is not measured in what you own, since someone with nothing can be the most generous person alive; what you give is your time. Given freely, time stretches. A day spent with an open heart runs like a river through open country, and a day spent holding on is over before you have drawn a breath.

Give to what will grow. There are seasons when the life closes in, every day is worry, and there is no room left to consider anyone. The thought in that season is that you will give when you have enough, and that day does not come. Holding on to keep yourself safe is the thing that keeps you hungry, because a life sealed against loss cannot be fed either. Open the hand a little, to one person or one living thing that can use it, and the law turns round: what you give freely is what fills you.

## ICHING

### Combination
Mountain over Thunder: stillness above, and under it the shock that starts things. Seen whole, the six lines are a mouth, a firm line for the upper jaw, a firm line for the lower, and four open lines for the hollow between. The old character is the same picture, open jaws, and the word means the cheeks, the chin, to take in, to feed, to rear.

### Upper trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain is the limit, the place where the going stops. It stands still, it does not give way, and past it nothing can be seen. In the year it is the start of winter; in a family it is the youngest son, the last one the house is built around. In you it is the part that can stop: the mouth that closes, the hand that says enough. Over the jaws it is the upper jaw, the one that does not move, which the appetite below has to work against. Its risk is that stopping becomes all it does, and a mouth that will not open is not fed.

### Lower trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Below the mountain is Thunder, the shock that rises out of the ground in spring and wakes what was sleeping. It rouses, it stirs, it is the chick breaking the egg and the bud breaking the twig; in a family it is the eldest son, the first to move. In you it is appetite, the reach for what feeds you before the mind has said a word, the hunger older than any manners. Set under the mountain it is the lower jaw, the one that works, chewing against the stillness above. Its risk is that it never stops reaching, and a mouth that takes everything is never full.

### Reading
Something has been gathered; now it is time to feed on it and to feed with it. That is where you stand: at the mouth, where food goes in and words come out. The whole question of this time is what you let through in either direction. Look at what you are feeding, in yourself and in the people who lean on you, then at what you reach for to fill your own mouth. Heaven and earth feed every creature without being asked; the wise feed the ones worth feeding, so that it reaches everyone in the end.

What it asks is temperance, which is not going without. Be careful in what you eat and drink, careful in what you say, because words are food too: what you let out feeds the people round you or poisons them. Keep to the kind who feed rather than take. The ones who reach upward for what they should be giving down will fall away from you for a while, which is part of it. Hold to what is true and it goes well.

In the body this energy is the mouth. The upper jaw does not move; the lower does all the work, and it works against that stillness. Notice where you are held now, whether the jaw is set, whether the mouth is already open for what comes next. Sit with the jaw loose, the lips closed, and let what you take in be chewed before it is swallowed.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ the tortoise that lives on air set down, and a mouth watering at another's plate. → becomes Hexagram 23, Splitting Apart.
The tortoise needs nothing; it lives on the air, and you have set it down to stare at what someone else is eating. What you have is enough to feed you, and the wanting is what makes it feel like too little. Nobody respects a mouth hanging open at another's table. Turn back to what is already yours, and eat from that.

**Line 2** · _image:_ food sought from the hill-top, against the way it flows. → becomes Hexagram 41, Decrease.
Food is meant to come down from the strong to the weak, and you are reaching up the hill for it, toward someone who has nothing to spare either. Reaching that way leaves you without the company you had, because the ones who could have walked with you are the ones you turned from. Ask nothing of the hill; feed yourself from where you stand.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the back turned on what feeds, for ten years. → becomes Hexagram 22, Grace.
The back is turned on what feeds you; only the belly and the coat are looked after. Kept up with a will, this ends badly, because the part of you that is starving is the part that lives. A whole cycle can pass, ten years, with nothing to show for it. Stop now, while it is ten years and not the whole of a life.

**Line 4** · _image:_ a tiger's stare, fixed and hungry, and no fault in it. → becomes Hexagram 21, Biting Through.
Now the hunger is right. You look down for what you need the way a tiger looks, unblinking, wanting it, ready to spring, and there is nothing wrong in that. Asking below you for the strength you lack is no shame, so long as the asking is earnest and nothing forced. Keep your eyes on what feeds you, and go when it moves.

**Line 5** · _image:_ off the usual road, and holding the ground where you stand. → becomes Hexagram 42, Increase.
You are off the usual road, responsible for people you do not have the means to feed. Stay upright where you are, and lean on the one above you who has what you lack; that is not weakness, it is how the wise find what is right. This is not the season for a great undertaking. Keep what is established, and do not cross the water yet.

**Line 6** · _image:_ the one the food comes from, in a high and dangerous place. → becomes Hexagram 24, Return.
You are the one the food comes from now, and everyone below you eats from your hand. The place is dangerous because so much rests on it, and it goes well because you know that. Able to feed others in a hard season, you can feed them in any season, without waiting to be told. The great crossing is yours to make now, so make it.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Selfishness · _Gift:_ Altruism · _Siddhi:_ Selflessness

### Shadow — Selfishness
Everything alive eats something else. Nothing exists that cannot be food for something, and one thing's death is another's birth, all the way up the chain, with you in it. Holding on to what you have is the oldest move in that chain, the one that kept your blood line alive, and it is not a sin, whatever you were told. It is a small room. The life is tight, the mind is tight, the worry runs under everything, and the pain inside takes the space that considering anyone else would need. At the table you eat with your arm around the plate.

You were not born to it. You came into the world unselfish, then were schooled in this by a house or a school that taught taking, and what was learned can be unlearned. Under it is a purpose missing, a life with no reason in it larger than the life, and people will do almost anything not to feel that gap. Once they went to war for it; now they go shopping. Sit in the small room long enough to ask what the larger reason would be, and the room begins to widen.

### Repressive nature — Self-Sacrificing
This person gives everything away, and none of it from the heart. What goes out is their own strength, handed over with no edge to it, so they are used, or resented for the strain in the giving. A thin guilt runs under all of it. Slowly the body pays.

### Reactive nature — Self-Centred
Here the giving comes with a hook in it. It is given to get something back, and looks generous until the return does not come, when the anger underneath breaks the surface all at once. The shock is the point. That giving came from the head, and the heart was never in the room.

### Gift — Altruism
Care that wants nothing back is your nature, not a thing you learned. Watch a pod of dolphins: one is in trouble, the whole pod turns toward it at once, no decision taken, the knowing passing through all of them as if they were one body. An older one will give its life for a younger without a pause. That is its shape in you. When the arm comes off the plate it goes to the middle of the table, and you are fuller for it.

This care has eyes. It knows, untaught, what is worth giving to whom, and it will not feed anyone's wish to stay helpless. It runs on the pull that keeps a parent beside a child through the first seven years, the years that set everything, and a wound of your own from that age heals as you give what you did not get. What comes off you then is trust, felt before you have spoken; near you people lower their guard, some for the first time in their lives. Purpose rises out of this the way water rises from a spring, warm, and spreads to whoever stands close.

### Siddhi — Selflessness
There is nobody at the head of the table now, and the feeding goes on with no one doing it. The one who gave is not there to be thanked. What is left is a hunger the body always carried, for a harmony so complete that food was only its outer sign. This is not doing good; the ones who help to be seen, or to cover a pain of their own, are still someone. Here no self is left to be noticed, so such people pass unnoticed, ordinary as anyone in the street.

They give without end and are not emptied; the love around them feeds them as they go. Where an ounce of life is left in a thing, this reaches it. From here the world feeding on itself sounds like music, and love is all they hear. You arrive with no self, you leave with no self, and the one in the middle was the dream.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 27, Caring
Low in you sits the pressure to look after what is yours. Gate 27 is the door it comes through: the drive to feed and protect the ones in your keeping, the young, the family, the small circle, yourself among them, before anyone has asked. It is the pull that checks the door at night and counts the ones asleep. Nobody has to tell you to do it, and if it has nowhere to go you are restless without knowing why.

From outside it can look like too much. The same pull, unwatched, feeds people who did not need it, holds them so close they cannot grow, takes on loads that were never yours, until you are tired, unthanked, and sour with it. The question this pressure has to learn is whose problem this is. People who carry it all their lives learn to treat the other person as able, letting go with love so they can find their own strength; they learn too that looking after themselves was never the selfish part.

### Where it lives — the Sacral Center
The care is kept low in the body, in the Sacral centre, between the navel and the tops of the legs, the strongest engine you have. It is the energy that brings a child into the world, raises it, feeds it, keeps a house; it does not decide, it answers, a yes that lifts or a no that goes flat. Through this pressure the engine is pointed at the people in your keeping, tribal, warm, motherly in anyone who carries it. Run on a true yes the energy comes back as it is spent. Run on guilt it drains, and this is the engine people learn to take from, since a yes they talked you into looks like your own.

In about seven people in ten this engine runs all the time. For someone like that the answer to whether this one is theirs to feed is the most reliable thing they have, and the risk is the identity that grows around the feeding, the one who is always there, kept up after the yes has gone, until the body says no for them. In the other three it is open. For someone like that the energy is borrowed from whoever is near; they can look after a whole house for a stretch, then find nothing there, having missed the moment enough became too much. What they have instead is the pause, the eye for when someone has been fed.

### What completes it — the Channel of Preservation (27–50)
The drive to feed does not know, by itself, what deserves feeding. Its other half is Gate 50, in the Spleen, the part of you that keeps the values a group lives by and knows by instinct what is worth keeping. Together they make the Channel of Preservation: the pull to feed joined to the sense of what should be fed, which is how a family stays whole across years. Not everyone needs your care, and this half knows it. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you, the keeping only happens where the two meet.

## BODY

### Physiology
Feeding has a far end in you, and it is the sacral plexus, the weave of nerves on the inner face of the flat bone at the base of your spine. What began at your mouth this morning ends its road on orders from here: the last stretch of what you ate is moved along by nerves out of this weave, and the letting go, when it comes, is ordered from the same place. From this low place your body also draws the energy to bring a child into the world, raise it and feed it. Beside it, low in the back, is kept the store you were fed from before you could feed yourself, the given part of you, which every meal since has added to.

You are doing it now: something you ate is on the last of its road, moved by this weave without a thought from you. Put your hand on the small of your back, where the bone goes flat: you are resting it on the part of you that finishes what feeding began.

### Amino acid
The care this energy gives has a substance in you, and the substance is leucine. Your body cannot make it; it comes in with meat, eggs or beans, or it does not come. Of everything you eat, it is the first thing your muscle notices after a meal, and between meals it is not there. What closes in you and what gives in you run on this one chemistry; the two pull opposite ways, the closing toward death, the giving toward life, in you as in everyone alive.

Give with nothing wanted back and something moves low in your body, a current that is real, one you can feel. Five other things in you share this substance, and it is the same substance in all six.
