# UL 27 — Inner Majesty

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Caring for Your Own · Feed Yourself First · A Full Heart · Giving Freely · Your Own People · Knowing When to Stop · Looking After the Young

When a person close to you needs looking after, the care in you is already moving. You remember the birthday, and you look in on the friend who is sick. Near you, others lower their guard and let themselves be looked after, sometimes for the first time.

At times you give more than you have, until it wears down your health. Resentment creeps in, or you hold people so close they cannot grow. Feed yourself first, with food, rest and time. Before you step in, notice who truly needs you.

Look after yourself as closely as you would anyone you love, and let others care for you in turn. Give to what is alive and able to grow, without keeping count. A day given freely moves slowly and feels full. Given this way, your care feeds you as it goes out, and you do not run dry.

## ICHING

### Combination
Mountain over Thunder is the hexagram of how you are fed. Thunder is the movement in you that reaches for what you need. Mountain is the stillness in you that knows when you have had enough. The stillness sits above the movement because reaching on its own never stops. That is why this hexagram has long been read as a mouth. A mouth opens to take food in and closes when it is full. The same mouth speaks. So the care you take in eating is the care you take in what you say. You are fed by what you reach for and by when you stop.


### Upper trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain is where you set a limit and keep it. It says no to one more plate, or to a word you would regret. It sits on top because a limit is what other people meet first, when they ask more of you than you have. Mountain has long been read as sunset, the hour when the day's work stops. The rest you take then is what feeds the next day's work. On its own, the limit turns hard and stubborn, and you stop seeing past it.


### Lower trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder is the jolt that gets you moving. It puts you on your feet when a person close to you needs feeding, before you have thought about it. It lies underneath because every move you make begins inside you, well before anyone can see it. Thunder has long been read as a power that rises out of the earth and breaks open the bud. Anything that grows in you begins this way, with a push from below. On its own, it keeps you moving until the effort wears you out, and then you need feeding yourself.


### Reading
You have gathered something, and now it is time to eat from it and feed others with it. You stand at the mouth, where food goes in and words come out. What matters now is what you let in and what you let out. Feed the people who will go on to feed others, and what you give reaches everyone.

This time asks for care in two things: what you eat and drink, and what you say. Words are food too, so what you say to someone is something they have to eat. Keep to the kind who feed rather than take. Feed the ones below you before you go asking from the ones above.

Your jaw is the place to check. When you are reaching for more than you need, it hangs a little open, ready for the next thing. Close your lips, let the jaw go loose, and chew what you already have before you swallow it.

### Judgement
- Nourishment. Holding to what is right, it goes well.
- Watch what you feed, and watch what you reach for to fill your own mouth.
- Seek what fills your mouth from its source, and the source is yourself.

### Image

- So the wise are careful in what they say, temperate in what they eat and drink.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ the tortoise that lives on air set down, and a mouth watering at another's plate. → becomes Hexagram 23, Splitting Apart.
You have what the tortoise has, enough in yourself to live on. You have set that down to stare at what someone else is eating. Your own share is enough, and the staring is what makes it feel small. Nobody respects a mouth hanging open at another's table. Turn back to what is yours and eat from that.

**Line 2** · _image:_ food sought from the hill-top, against the way it flows. → becomes Hexagram 41, Decrease.
Food flows down from the strong to the weak; that is its proper way. You are reaching up the hill for it, toward someone with nothing to spare either. Going that way costs you your friends, because you have turned from the ones beside you. Ask nothing of the people above you. Feed yourself from where you stand.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the back turned on what feeds, for ten years. → becomes Hexagram 22, Grace.
You have turned your back on what truly feeds you and care only for food and clothes. Keep that up as firmly as you like and it still ends badly. The part of you that you are starving is what keeps you alive. Ten years can pass that way with nothing to show for them. Stop now, while it has cost you ten years and not a life.

**Line 4** · _image:_ a tiger's stare, fixed and hungry, and no fault in it. → becomes Hexagram 21, Biting Through.
This hunger is the right kind. You look down for what you need the way a tiger looks, steady and about to spring, and there is no fault in it. Asking those below you for the strength you lack is no shame, so long as you mean it and force nothing. Keep your eyes on what feeds you and go when it moves.

**Line 5** · _image:_ off the usual road, and holding the ground where you stand. → becomes Hexagram 42, Increase.
You are off the usual road, responsible for people you have no means to feed. Stay upright where you are and lean on the one above you who has what you lack. Leaning is no weakness; it is how the wise find what is right. Keep what is in place and start nothing new. Do not cross the great stream yet; the big leap is not for this season.

**Line 6** · _image:_ the one the food comes from, in a high and dangerous place. → becomes Hexagram 24, Return.
You are the one the food comes from now, and everyone below you eats from your hand. The place is dangerous because so much rests on it, and it goes well because you know that. You have fed others through a hard time, so you can feed them in any season without being told. Cross the great stream: the big leap is yours.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Selfishness · _Gift:_ Altruism · _Siddhi:_ Selflessness

### Shadow — Selfishness
Selfishness is holding on to what you have, and it is the oldest move there is. Everything alive eats something else, and one thing's death is another's birth. You are in that chain, and holding on is what kept your blood line in it. So it is not a sin, whatever you were told. But it makes a small room, where life is tight and worry runs under everything. When you hurt inside you have no room to think of anyone else. At the table you eat with your arm around the plate.

You came into the world unselfish. A house or a school taught you to take, and what you learned you can unlearn. What is missing underneath is a purpose, a reason for your life bigger than yourself. People will do almost anything to avoid feeling that gap: once they went to war to fill it, now they go shopping. Stay in the small room long enough to ask what the larger reason would be. That question is what makes the room bigger.

### Repressive nature — Self-Sacrificing
Held down, the selfishness turns into giving everything away, and none of it from the heart. What this person hands over is their own strength, with no limit on it. So people use them, or resent the strain in the gift. A thin guilt runs under all of it, and slowly the body pays.

### Reactive nature — Self-Centred
Turned outward, the giving has a hook in it: this person gives to get something back. It looks generous until the return does not come, and then the anger underneath breaks the surface. The shock is how generous it looked at the start. This giving comes from the head, never the heart.

### Gift — Altruism
Care that wants nothing back is your nature, and nobody taught it to you. When one dolphin in a pod is in trouble, the whole pod turns to help at once. Your care moves the same way, toward whoever is in trouble, before you have decided anything. When your arm comes off the plate it goes to the middle of the table, and you are fuller for it.

This care knows, without being taught, what is worth giving to whom. It will not feed anyone's wish to stay helpless. It is the same pull that keeps a parent beside a child for the first seven years, the years that shape everything. A wound of your own from those years heals as you give what you did not get. What comes off you is trust, so people near you lower their guard, some for the first time in their lives. Purpose wells up out of this like water from a spring and spreads to whoever stands close.

### Siddhi — Selflessness
Nobody sits at the head of this table now. You still feed people, and there is no you doing it. What remains is a longing your body carries, for everything to be at peace with everything else. Food is only the visible part of it. This is different from doing good. The ones who help so they will be seen, or to cover a pain of their own, still have a self doing the helping. Here no self remains, so the people who live this go unnoticed, as ordinary as anyone in the street.

They give without end and never run dry, fed by the love around them. Where an ounce of life remains in anything, this care can reach it. Listen from there and everything eating and being eaten sounds like music. What you hear in it is love. You came into the world without a self and you will leave it without one. The self in between is something you dreamed up.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 27, Caring
Low in you sits the pressure to look after what is yours. Gate 27 is the name for it. It drives you to feed and protect the people you are responsible for, yourself among them, before anyone has asked. It is the pull that checks the door at night and counts the ones asleep. Nobody has to ask, and when it has nowhere to go you are restless without knowing why.

Left to itself, the same pull feeds people who did not need it and holds them so close they cannot grow. It picks up loads that were never yours, until you are tired, unthanked and sour. The question to ask is whose problem this is. Treat the other person as able, and let go with love so they can find their own strength.

### Where it lives — the Sacral Center
This care sits in the Sacral centre, low in the body between the navel and the thighs. It is the strongest energy you have, the one that brings a child into the world and feeds it. It does not decide; it answers. Put something in front of it and your gut says yes or no before you think. In anyone who carries it the caring is warm and motherly. Spend it on what you said yes to and it comes back as fast as you spend it. Spend it out of guilt and it drains. People learn to take from it, because a yes they talked you into looks like yours.

Most people, about seven in ten, have this energy running on its own all day. They can trust their gut about who is theirs to look after. Their risk is the role of the one who is always there. They keep it up long after the wanting has gone, until they get ill. The other three in ten have no caring energy of their own and take it in from the people around them. They can look after everyone in a house for a while. Then it is gone all at once, because nothing in them said when enough was enough. What they can do instead is stand back and watch the caring without joining it. They know what is theirs to do and what is not.

### What completes it — the Channel of Preservation (27–50)
On its own, the drive to feed does not know what deserves feeding. What completes it is Gate 50, the part of you that holds the values a family lives by. It sits in the Spleen centre, which knows by instinct what keeps you safe. Together they make the Channel of Preservation, two gates joined into one line of energy. With both you guard what you value. Not everyone needs your care, and Gate 50 knows it. It may sit in you or in someone close to you, and the channel runs only when both gates are present.

## BODY

### Physiology
The sacral plexus is where feeding ends in you, and that is why this energy is given to it. It is a weave of nerves on the inside of the flat bone at the base of your spine. The same low place holds the energy to carry and feed a child. What went in at your mouth this morning ends its road here. Nerves from this weave move the last of it along and say when to let go. Low in your back beside it, you keep the strength you were born with. You lived on that before you could eat, and every meal since has added to it.

You are doing it now. Something you ate is on the last of its road, and this weave is moving it without a thought from you. Put your hand on the small of your back, where the bone goes flat. Your hand is resting on the part of you that finishes what feeding began.

### Amino acid
The care in you has a substance, and its name is leucine. Your body cannot make it. It arrives in meat, eggs or beans, or not at all. After a meal your muscle notices it first, and between meals it is gone. Whether you hold on or give, you are running on the same chemistry.

Give for the sake of giving and you start healthy currents deep in your body. You share its chemistry with five other energies.

## RELATIONS

_UL 28 is this card's pair and its partner at once: this care keeps a life fed, and that one gives the life its reason._

### Pair — UL 28, The Dark Side of Light (Great Preponderance)
This care takes on whoever needs feeding, and it runs out when there are more people than you can carry. The Dark Side of Light is where it goes next, the weight of everything you have taken on. Open it when the load has grown heavier than you can bear.

### Inverse — its own reflection
This care looks the same from either end. Turn it end for end and it comes back as itself, with the one who fed now the one being fed. When you are the one who needs feeding, come back to this card from the other end.

### Programming partner — UL 28, The Dark Side of Light
This care keeps a body alive and cannot say what the life is for. The Dark Side of Light holds what this one lacks: a reason to live, and the nerve to risk something for it. Care keeps the life going, and purpose gives it a reason. Open it when you are fed and still do not know why you are alive.

### Codon ring — Ring of Life and Death
This care keeps one life going on another, and it belongs to a family of six, the Ring of Life and Death. Each of the six is a stage that something in your life passes through. UL 3 is the stage where it begins, and UL 20 the stage where you are fully in it. UL 23 is where it falls apart, UL 24 where it comes back and UL 42 where it grows on. Open the one that matches where you are.
