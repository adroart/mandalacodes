# UL 50 — Melt Into Perfection

## CODE

_Keywords:_ What Was Handed Down · Cooking It Slowly · Finding the Middle Again · Feeding the Ones Near You · Why Bother · One Habit at a Time · Everything in Tune

Keeping what people live by is this energy, the sense in you of what is good for the ones near you and what has gone off. It came with a responsibility nobody handed you, and with faults you did not choose either, wound into you before you were born the way a family's habits are. What was given to you is not finished. It is raw, and your part is to cook it slowly until it can feed someone.

Carried well, this energy is balance, which is not a place you reach and keep but a thing you keep finding. You bring it into a room without thinking about it, and people settle around you without knowing why. Too much effort and you tense. Too little and you drift. Both hurt, which is what brings you back to the middle, and what keeps the middle honest is having nothing hidden on the table.

The way is slow, and it is not pulling the fault out by the roots, since the harder you grip it the deeper it goes. Work at one habit at a time, and let patience do the rest. There will be days when it seems that nobody else bothers, so why should you, and the giving up will feel like clear sight. It is the one thing the fault in you relies on. Take a stand instead, and a force wakes in you that wavers at first, then stays.

## ICHING

### Combination
Fire over Wind, and wind below is wood: a fire on a bed of wood, which is cooking, raw things turned by heat into what can feed. The traditional character draws a bronze pot on three legs, in which food was cooked for the ancestors and the gods. The six lines draw it too: the legs at the bottom, the full belly, the two ears, the handle on top.

### Upper trigram — Fire (Li)
Above is fire, and fire has no body of its own. It clings to what it burns, one soft line held between two firm ones, and while it holds it gives light, so that things can be told apart. In a family it is the middle daughter, the one who sees. In you it is the heat that works on what you were given, the clear eye above the work that knows raw from ready, what to keep and what to pour off. Its risk is burning, when the heat that was meant to cook a thing goes on past ready, and there is nothing left to feed anyone.

### Lower trigram — Wind (Xun)
Underneath is wind, and here wind is wood: the tree with its roots down, the thing that feeds a fire. It goes in gently and keeps going in, the way roots work into the ground. In a family it is the eldest daughter, patient, the one who keeps the house going. In you it is what you put under the fire, the patience that feeds the work a little at a time and does not stop feeding it. Its risk is being used up, since wood that feeds a fire is spent by it, and what feeds everyone else can burn away before it has been fed itself.

### Reading
The old order has been thrown down and you are standing in what comes after, which is the harder part. Something has been left in your hands to be made into the new, and it has to be made, not found. The picture is a pot on a fire, the pot that fed the gods first and then every member of the house, each one served according to their place. What is asked is that you feed what is best in the people around you first, because the whole of what comes next grows out of that.

Before you cook, clean the pot. What was left in it from before has gone stale, and a good thing cooked in a dirty vessel comes out spoiled. Then take your place, stand in it rightly, and a life whose place is right sets firm the way cast metal does. Cook gently, and your ears grow clear, your eyes sharp. Nothing here is sudden. The heat has to be watched to the end, since one slip near the finish loses the whole of it.

In the body this energy is the ears and the eyes, the part of you that hears what the people at your table need, that sees when a thing is ready. Notice where you are holding yourself now. Stand as a pot stands over the fire, on three feet, still, holding what is not yet finished.

### Judgement
- The Cauldron. Supreme good fortune. Everything goes through.
- Fire on wood is cooking: the sage cooked to make an offering to heaven, and cooked again to feed the wise.
- Through gentleness the ears grow clear and the eyes sharp. The soft rises to the centre, and the firm answers it.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ the pot tipped over on its feet, and the stale poured out. → becomes Hexagram 14, Great Possession.
Tipped over on its feet, the pot loses what had gone stale, and that is the good in the overturning. It is not the time to add anything new but to get rid of the old, so that what is set on the fire stands level. If the vessel you have will not do, take another, even a lesser one, for what it can bring. No fault in that.

**Line 2** · _image:_ food in the pot, and one who will not come to eat. → becomes Hexagram 56, The Wanderer.
There is food in the pot, and the one set against you cannot come near it. The objection they always have does not reach you now. It may be a part of yourself that will not come to the table, the head refusing what the belly knows. Be careful where you go, because the tension will keep until you can settle it. For now, the meal is good.

**Line 3** · _image:_ the handles changed, the pot stuck fast, and the pheasant's fat uneaten. → becomes Hexagram 64, Before Completion.
With its handles gone the pot cannot be lifted, so the rich meal inside it goes uneaten. Firmness with no give in it is how this happens: you filled the pot and would not empty yourself, so the work stopped. Regret comes like rain, and the rain clears it. Listen to what the others are saying, because the way to move it is in their hands, and it ends well.

**Line 4** · _image:_ the legs broken, and the prince's meal on the floor. → becomes Hexagram 18, Work on the Decayed.
The legs give way under a pot too full, and the meal meant for the prince is on the floor, his robes soaked. You took on more than your strength or your standing could carry, and leaned on a position instead. The shame is the lesson. Stop where you are, put right what was spilled, and look again at who you relied on before carrying anything else.

**Line 5** · _image:_ yellow ears on the pot, and rings of gold to carry it. → becomes Hexagram 44, Coming to Meet.
Yellow is the colour of the centre. A pot with yellow ears and golden rings can be carried anywhere. The ears are hollow, the handle solid: soft and firm carry each other. You are in the right place, bending to the ones worth feeding with a humble strength. Hold to one thing, do not divide your attention, and stay humble, since everyone answers to something above them.

**Line 6** · _image:_ a handle of jade. → becomes Hexagram 32, Duration.
The handle is jade: hard as metal, with a softness of its own, and no fire that melts metal can touch it. The cooking is finished, and what was firm in you has become one thing with what was soft. Nothing you do now fails to help. Strength with no strain in it is what lifts the pot at last, so lift it, and give what is in it away.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Corruption · _Gift:_ Equilibrium · _Siddhi:_ Harmony

### Shadow — Corruption
The fault in you was copied in before you were born, and this is what it sounds like: a string that rasps instead of holding its note. It is not your doing and you have it anyway. You meet it in the world first, the crooked dealing, the ones who take and answer to nobody. It seems to come from out there. It grows out there because it is wound around you in here, and nobody has ever cleared it from the world without first pulling the root in themselves.

What the fault relies on is your giving up. Nobody else bothers, so why should you. You tried, you prayed, and the weed is still there. Resignation wears the face of clear sight. The other mistake is to grip: one bad day and you are tight all over, pulling at the weed with tense hands, which makes it worse. A string is not fixed by force. A child learning an instrument makes a sound that is painful to hear, and then, one small tune at a time, the notes start to hold.

### Repressive nature — Overloaded
The whole weight of the world has landed on this person. Everyone close to them is theirs to protect, the house is theirs to hold up, and it is too heavy, so the dreams they had were put down. The dreams were where their life was. Only facing the fear lets it back out.

### Reactive nature — Irresponsible
Here the fear turns into anger, at the ones above or below, and into taking what can be taken. This person does not feel answerable for what their actions do to anyone. It is all gain and standing, yet they are as trapped by the order they work as the ones they work it on.

### Gift — Equilibrium
Tuned by ear, the same string holds its note, and the note is balance. There is a code for harmony in you already, a middle point that the quality of your whole life rests on, and you find it by being thrown off it. Pour everything in, you tense up. Only watch, you give up. Each extreme hurts, so it sends you back to the centre. Balance is not a place you reach and keep. It is a thing you keep finding.

Then you bring it into a room. You walk into a place where the music has gone to pieces, feel what is missing, put the parts back together so that harmony is possible again, and you do it without thinking, the way a cook tastes the pot, then adjusts. What makes it hold is honesty, everything hidden laid on the table. Nothing shapes a child more than the life a parent did not live, and what is hidden between two parents comes out in the child. It cannot be imposed on anyone. If one person in the group is off balance the group has no chance, so each one has to be trusted with their own.

### Siddhi — Harmony
The note joins a music that was already playing. Balance was the flowering. This is the fruit, and it falls when it is ripe, into a harmony that was never made, only entered. Balance can be lost. This cannot. Before you can feel it every sense of being separate has to have gone, and then you melt into it. The body becomes a chamber that rings, then the chamber itself dissolves, and the vessel turns out to be everything there is.

Every life is a note on one score, and the score grows sweeter. Under the surface of your days it was playing the whole time, whether or not you could hear it, and everything you ever did was moving you toward it. One person who arrives here turns the whole thing, since what was hidden comes to light around them. You are not the player. You are the instrument, and what plays through you is the music.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 50, Values
What the people near you need in order to be well is what presses here, and Gate 50 is its door: the sense of what is good for them, what they should not be given, held as a responsibility you never agreed to. It is the mother's pressure, whoever carries it, the drive to keep the ones you love whole, and to guard what the group lives by. Under it is a fear: that you are not taking enough care, that they will be let down. The fear makes you take on more than is yours, until you are carrying people who could walk, and calling it love.

From outside it can look like meddling, or like a rule held so hard that nobody is allowed to make their own mistakes. The cost is guilt, since care driven by guilt empties the one who gives it. People who carry this all their lives learn that taking care of themselves first is not selfish but the only way there is anything left to give, and that it is arrogant to think the ones they love could not do without them.

### Where it lives — the Spleen Center
The oldest alarm in the body is where this pressure is felt, the Spleen Centre, low on the left, whose whole work is to keep you well. It houses the fears that keep an animal alive, one to each door, and this door's fear is of letting your people down. Most of those fears are ropes taken for snakes: a thing lying across the path that stops you cold until someone else walks past it. The way through is to see it for what it is, thank it, then go on. When this centre is at ease there is an aliveness in you, the lightness of a body that feels safe, and it meets each moment fresh, which is why it keeps the time.

In about fifty-five people in a hundred this centre is switched on. For someone like that the body carries a glow that says all is well, and people who are not sure of themselves come to stand near it. The catch is that every fear they hold goes out into the room with the same strength. In the other forty-five it is not, so the body takes in the state of whoever is near: another person's poor health can be felt in their own body, mistaken for theirs, and time slips, lost or pressing on them. When they are well there is a fine sense in them of what would be good for another person.

### What completes it — the Channel of Preservation (27–50)
Knowing what would feed people is not the same as feeding them. Its other half is Gate 27, low in the belly, with the power to do the work. Together they make the Channel of Preservation, the drive to keep your people well: the values that hold them together, with an instinct for what will nourish, at the table or in a lesson. Not everyone needs your care, and part of the gift is knowing who does. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you, the keeping is only done where the two meet.

For some months before you were born, it was one of the places your blood was made, and it has kept house for that blood ever since. Rest a hand on the left side under your ribs: behind the stomach there is the vessel in which what you were given became you.
