# UL 60 — Woven Light

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Drawing the Line · Living Inside a Limit · The Frame That Holds · Waiting in the Gap · Rising Against the Pull · A Rule Kept Too Long · Letting the Frame Go

Limits are this energy, the line drawn so that what moves through you can take a shape. A river with no banks is a flood that goes nowhere; with banks it is a river, and it arrives. In you it is the part that says this far, this much, no further, so that a piece of work or a whole life can stand up and be something. It is not a brake. It is what lets force go somewhere.

Carried well, it is a balance between two pulls. There is a force in you that wants to rise, reach, fly, and a force that pulls back down, tightens, settles for less. You live between them, and neither is meant to win: too much of the first burns you, too much of the second shrivels you. You need the structures, and you need to be free of them at the same time. The one who carries this well builds a frame without living in it. Without a limit nothing in you has a shape, and without a shape you cannot say what you want; the discipline that draws the line is how you find out.

The low of it is a frame kept past its season. You built a way of thinking once, about money or about the person you live with. After long enough the walls stopped being something you made and became the edge of the world. This force does not yield to being pushed. It waits for one thing, which is time, because everything alive moves in cycles and rights itself when it is let. When a frame has done its work, let it crack, and keep what it was built to hold.

## ICHING

### Combination
Water over Lake: the stream, and under it the lake that holds it. The old character is the joint in a stalk of bamboo, the knot that divides the cane into lengths, the reason it stands so tall without snapping: a division that makes the parts and joins them at once. Above, the stream keeps coming; below, the lake takes a measured amount and no more.

### Upper trigram — Water (Kan)
Water is the stream, the force that keeps coming. One firm line is held between two that give way, so its strength is hidden inside a hollow. It is the middle son, the one who goes down into the gorge, the place where a step can be lost. Above, it is the outer face of the situation: what keeps arriving from higher up, more than can be held. In you it is whatever runs, the feeling or the money, moving and able to run away. Its risk is the fall, the flood that spreads until nothing has a shape.

### Lower trigram — Lake (Dui)
Lake is the basin, the open water that lies still because something holds it. It is the youngest daughter, the one who pleases, with a face open to whatever falls on it. Below, it is the inner side of the situation: the gladness of a thing held to its measure, the ease of water that knows its shore. In you it is contentment inside a limit, the settled feeling of enough, which is a pleasure and not a defeat. Its risk is the overflow, when the basin takes in more than it can keep and the fields around it drown.

### Reading
What you are in asks for a limit, and the limit is what will let it succeed. A lake stays a lake by holding a measured amount of water. Too little and it dries. Too much and it breaks its banks. So the level is watched, and the watching never ends. A cane of bamboo grows tall because it is jointed, each knot a small stop that lets the next length begin. What you are building will stand the same way, by the places where you say this far.

What it asks of you is to make the measures: to reckon what you will spend, how far you will go, what you will not do, then to weigh your own conduct against them. A measure made well injures nothing you own and harms no one near you. Heaven and earth keep to theirs, which is why there are four seasons rather than one long day.

There is one warning, and it is the centre of the matter. A limit that has gone bitter must not be carried on. A rule kept past its season, severe and hard to bear, stops serving the thing it was made for; the harder you hold it, the sooner its road runs out. The measure has to be measured too. In the body this is the joints, the knee and the knuckle, a limit that is the whole reason a limb can bend. Feel yours now, whether they are easy or locked. A joint held stiff does not make you stronger, only unable to move.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ not going out of the door and the courtyard. → becomes Hexagram 29, The Abysmal Water.
Staying inside your own door, when the way is blocked, is not timidity; it is knowing the difference between a passage and a wall. You have the power to move, the road is not open, and the one ahead of you holds it for now. Be careful at the beginning and there is no fault at the end. Stay in, say nothing, and wait.

**Line 2** · _image:_ not going out of the gate and the courtyard, when the gate stands open. → becomes Hexagram 3, Difficulty at the Beginning.
Still inside the gate one step later, with the gate now open: this is the same staying in gone wrong. The hour has come, you have the strength, and holding to a small rule loses a large thing. Waiting is watching for the moment; hesitating is being handed the moment and not taking it. Know your limits from your doubts, and go out.

**Line 3** · _image:_ no limit at all, and then the sigh. → becomes Hexagram 5, Waiting.
Nothing held back and every appetite let run, then the sigh comes, with nobody to blame for it. You did what you pleased without knowing that pleasure needs a measure, so the wound is your own. Nobody scolds here; the line only asks whose fault it was and leaves you to answer. Set the limit before you set out, and you will not lose your way.

**Line 4** · _image:_ peaceful limitation, the measure kept without effort. → becomes Hexagram 58, The Joyous Lake.
At peace inside the measure, this line keeps its limits the way a settled person keeps a promise, without strain. It takes them from the one above and makes them its own. The limit is no longer forced; it has become natural, helped by the strength beside it. Work within what is fixed, and inside it you are without limit.

**Line 5** · _image:_ sweet limitation, from the middle place. → becomes Hexagram 19, Approach.
Sweet limitation is the measure kept so well that it tastes good, and this line keeps it from the centre, neither too much nor too little. Content in the middle of difficulty, it needs no one to hold it to its word. Others cannot be made to keep a limit; they keep the one they see kept. Stay in your own banks, go on, and there is honour in it.

**Line 6** · _image:_ bitter limitation, held to past its use. → becomes Hexagram 61, Inner Truth.
Bitter limitation is the rule gone severe, held when it should have bent, and even where it is correct it ends badly, because its road runs out. This is the one who can hold back but cannot reach out, a discipline turned into its own suffering. It is a smaller fault than having no rule at all, so it can be mended. Let the regret turn you, and it disappears.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Limitation · _Gift:_ Realism · _Siddhi:_ Justice

### Shadow — Limitation
Frozen patterns are the low of this energy: life closing into tighter shapes over the years, until the openings that once let something in have shut. It is the counterweight to being alive, and it works through the mind, which likes to build. Thought goes round the same track it went round yesterday, about the bank account, about the government, and nothing original gets in, because the door was never left ajar. Out in the world it is red tape, the law so tangled it no longer protects anyone, the child made to sit at a desk at the age when the whole body wants to run. It cannot stand the new, and it cannot stand the young.

What it costs you is trust. Life has a way of righting itself, in its own time. This is the part of you that cannot believe that, so it holds everything still and calls the stillness safety. Push it and it does not move; it waits for one thing only, which is time. Nothing you build will last, the body included. Knowing that is the first loosening of the grip.

### Repressive nature — Unstructured
This person has no frame at all. Nothing in their life lasts, not the work, not the town, because a commitment is a structure and structure is what they fear. What they had to give never finds the people or the place it needed. It looks like freedom; it is drift.

### Reactive nature — Rigid
Nobody questions this person. What they say and do is beyond reproach in their own eyes; anyone who crosses the line is met with anger. Every new way is a threat. They hold the form so hard that whatever the form was for is forgotten, and unless the grip loosens they shrivel inside it.

### Gift — Realism
Knowing how things get made is what this energy becomes: what the world of form allows and what it does not, which is why people who have it are so sought after. An idea, however good, will not enter the world on its own. It needs a shell, as a seed does, or it never takes. So you build the shell, or you graft the new shoot onto the old root, bringing change through what already stands rather than against it. You hold the structure lightly, with something like a smile, knowing it is a means, not the point, since every structure is doomed. That is realism. It has a natural business sense, building the kind of frame that bends when the ground moves.

Then it waits. Nothing new arrives on schedule; it comes in the gap between what you meant and what you built, once the easel is up, on a day you did not choose. There are stretches when nothing moves, arriving as suddenly as they leave, and you do not interfere. The highest part of this is knowing when to stand back and let the frame go.

### Siddhi — Justice
Justice is what this energy becomes, and it is not the word as it is used. Not punishment, not reward, not a scale weighing a deed. A man once stole the silver of the one house that had taken him in, was caught, and was brought back to its door. The owner told the police the silver had been given to him, and that undid the thief further than any sentence could. Being forgiven cracks you open. From here nobody is punished, because no separate person is left to have done the thing; the balance rights itself with no one keeping the books.

What loosens at this height are the laws themselves, time first, then the pull that holds matter together. What that feels like is weight leaving. Drop the past and you are light, in both senses of the word. Every wall you ever built turns out to have been holding you up only until you could stand.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 60, Conservation
Gate 60 is the pressure to bring something new into form, within limits, and its name here is Conservation: keep what still works. Out of everything that arrives, most of it noise, it feels for the one thing worth carrying forward and holds it until it has a shape. From outside that looks like a brake, a refusal to move; what is happening is a flower coming up out of mud, which cannot be hurried and cannot be skipped. In a time of loss the same pressure keeps you looking at what is still standing rather than at what has fallen, which is what lets a person rebuild. Its cost is intense. The pressure to produce a result is there before the result is. When nothing shows you can go low and believe something is wrong with you, when the timing is simply not yet. People who carry this all their lives learn a particular patience: the seed under the ground is growing while nothing is visible, and there is nothing anyone can do to push it.

### Where it lives — the Root Center
The pressure is felt at the base of the trunk, in the Root centre, which runs on adrenaline and is the foundation everything else stands on. It is a motor, one of four, but it does not run steadily: it pulses, on, then off, like the spark that fires an engine, so its whole law is timing. Three of its paths run up to the belly and set the pattern a life moves in. This is the one for change: unpredictable bursts of it, each trying to bring something new into being.

In about six people in ten this centre is switched on. They can bear pressure and aim it, hard to push into starting what is not theirs. What they are at the mercy of is the pulse. When it is off, the rug is out from under them, and they cannot make themselves do the thing they know they should, sometimes for a month. In the other four it is not switched on. The pressure of everyone near them arrives in them doubled, and beginning anything is hard, since the spark is borrowed. For them the way to start is to sit near someone whose motor is running, a partner in the same room or a stranger in a cafe, and to learn which pressures are their own. What they get for it is a wisdom about pressure that the six will never have.

### What completes it — the Channel of Mutation (3–60)
Pressure to keep what works is only a holding. Its other half is Gate 3, low in the belly, the drive to bring order out of a mess. Together they make the Channel of Mutation: bursts of change that cannot be predicted, each one trying to birth something new inside the limits that will let it last. It is evolution, never revolution; the tension between the push to change and the pull to keep is the channel working. Whether the other half is in you or in someone near, the new thing takes form only where the two meet.

## BODY

### Physiology
In the body this energy is given to the colon, and the reason is the banks. A litre and a half of liquid comes into it every day, the leftover of the gut above. All but a cupful of the water goes back into your blood, so what arrived as a flood leaves with a shape. It holds what it has for a day or two, then moves, a few long pushes a day, most often after the first meal, since a filling stomach sets the far end going. Nothing you decide changes that, except at the very end, where one muscle, the only one along the gut, waits for you. Felt from inside, the shape comes from what is taken back, the hold has its own time, and the last word is yours.
