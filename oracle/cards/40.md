# UL 40 — Eternal Wellspring

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Tired All the Way Down · Doing Less · Saying No · Time on Your Own · Work That Fills You · Knowing When to Stop · Filled From Below

The will is the energy here, the part of you that carries a life through. It lives in you as a spring, filling from underneath in its own time, giving what it has for as long as it is left to fill. In a life it is what you put into whatever you have set your hand to, and what returns when you stop. The water rises on its own when what you are doing is what your life was for. When it is not, you haul it up by the bucket, and hauling empties a spring faster than it fills.

The spring that is left to fill gives a strength that does not wear out, because nothing in it is forced. There is one thing you are here to do, and it is probably in front of you now. Give yourself to that and the tiredness goes, because work that fits you is not work; the strength for it arrives as you spend it. You still work hard, harder than most, and you stop when it is time to stop. Help comes on its own once you are doing what is yours, and goes when you push against your own life or give in to somebody else's. Time alone is where this strength refills, so you do not need company to be full.

Do less. Where you spend too much only you can see: too much at work with nothing left for home, or everything for other people. Cut those one at a time, without hurry, because the hurry was most of the trouble; what is left settles the way the body does when it is given less to carry. There is a tiredness that a night's sleep does not touch, and it comes from being cut off from your own source for too long. Nothing you were made for was meant to be this hard. Stop, be quiet, be on your own a while, and the water comes up.

## ICHING

### Combination
Thunder over Water: a storm breaking over a deep pool, the first roll gone through and the rain coming down. What was tied all through the waiting comes undone. The traditional character is a knife taking the horn from an ox, a thing joined being taken apart, which is the old word for untying a knot and for setting free.

### Upper trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder comes up out of the ground before it is heard in the sky. In the old picture the first stir of spring is under the earth, where the seed splits and the shoot pushes; the thunder is that same stir let loose overhead, big enough to move heavy things. In a family it is the eldest son. In you it is the moment of moving: after a long time held still, something in you gets up. Here it sits on top, so the moving is what shows. Its risk is that a shock which starts things can also only start them: all stir, nothing carried through.

### Lower trigram — Water (Kan)
Water underneath is the gorge, the deep place a stream has cut, where the ground gives way and the water goes on moving in the dark. In a family it is the middle son, the one caught between. In you it is the danger you have been living inside, the hard stretch with no bottom you could feel. The strength in it was real, held in the middle, which is why you lasted. Here it lies under the thunder, so the trouble is the ground the storm rises out of. Its risk is the pit: a danger not moved out of, only sunk further into.

### Reading
The hard stretch is ending, and knowing that is the first thing asked of you. The storm that stood over you so long has broken, and the rain after thunder is the kind everything grows under: a hundred kinds of seed split open at once. Danger is left behind by moving, and you have moved. Now the way asks two things. If there is nothing more to do, go back: to the old ways, to what your days were before the trouble, without the urge to change everything because you came through. If something is still to do, do it early, in the grey before the day starts, while the strength of the storm is in you and before the trouble can set again. Do not stand in the afterglow; whoever came through this before you went back to work the next morning, and you can walk in their steps.

Then forgive. After a storm the wise let the rain wash the offence off, their own mistakes with everyone else's, because a person still holding on to who was to blame is still tied. Give it time; a knot comes undone at the pace it was tied. In the body this is the foot. After a long time sitting still, the foot is the first thing to move, and it moves before you have decided to walk. Stand up the way a field looks after rain, clear with nothing left clenched, and take the first step early.

- When there is still somewhere to go, going early brings good fortune.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Exhaustion · _Gift:_ Resolve · _Siddhi:_ Divine Will

### Shadow — Exhaustion
Tired is the low here, the kind of tired that has stopped being about sleep, and the hand is where to see it. Everything you have taken on is in that hand, and it will not open: the job, the people who lean on you, more than any one hand was made to hold. You live in a time that calls this normal. Doing more, holding on harder: that is what you were told a strong person does, and each is what drains you. The cost is felt as thirst. You are thirsty for something you cannot name, and most of the time you do not know how thirsty you are, because the hand is too busy to notice. Underneath, something is being denied, usually a feeling.

The way it runs you is excess: too much in one corner of your life, nothing in another, so that you are many things to many people and yourself to none. Cut off from what feeds you, you begin to run down, the way anything does that is taken from and not fed. The hand does not need to be forced open, only noticed, and that is where the low starts to end.

### Repressive nature — Acquiescent
This person's hand is open the wrong way, palm up, for anyone to take from. They never learned that their own needs count, so they give without end, mostly to people who do not care about them, and cannot find the word no. Their life turns the day they make a stand for themselves alone.

### Reactive nature — Contemptuous
Here the hand is a fist. Underneath is an old rage that was never allowed, turned into scorn for everyone, the weakest first. They look strong, because rage burns like fuel, and they let nobody near enough to help. What they cut away was holding them up, and in the end it tells.

### Gift — Resolve
Deciding is where this strength comes in, and the deciding is done with an open hand. Take hold of the one thing that is yours and the rest drops from your fingers: the commitments that were never yours, the things you did because someone asked. What stays is what feeds you, and you can work harder than before without wearing out, since the willing is built into the work. You know when to say no, and you say it without a fight, because whoever you turn away belongs somewhere else. When life calls you off in the middle, a child or a friend at the door, you put the work down for the hour; that is not the strength failing, it is the strength.

Most of this is knowing how to rest. Rest is everywhere once you stop giving in to too much: a walk with nowhere to get to, the work you love with the people you love. None of this is going without; you take what life offers, with measure, and sometimes more than you need. A worn-out person who meets you like this goes away changed.

### Siddhi — Divine Will
Both hands are empty now, and nothing is missing. What was decided with an open hand is not decided at all any more, because there is no one left to do the choosing. You are free to do exactly as you please, and none of it is in your hands. That is not a defeat. It is the deepest rest a body can have, every part of it at ease with nothing pushing against anything. People who come near you can see it, because it shows in the skin like light. Nobody can pretend their way here; the ones worn out by searching who take up the idea of surrender as a way off the hook have only gone quiet. It comes at the end of the searching, when every hope has been dropped, and it comes alone. There is nothing to reach for outside your own aloneness.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 40, Restoration
Stepping back is the pressure here. Gate 40 is the door it comes through: the pull, after work or after people, to go off on your own and gather yourself again. In the body it is felt as a need to be alone that arrives before you can explain it, sometimes in the middle of a room with nothing wrong in it. Something in you has to go away to come back, and it goes whether or not you let it, so the choice is only whether you go with it.

From outside it looks like withdrawal, and a partner can read it as rejection unless you say what it is. From inside the danger runs the other way, toward staying: giving to everyone who asks, earning your place by doing more than was asked, blaming others for a tiredness you never named. The loneliness that comes with this is a signal, meant to be read as an invitation inward, not as proof of anything about your worth. People who carry it all their lives learn the rhythm of going away then coming back, and find they return with more to give than they left with, because they went.

### Where it lives — the Will Center
Off to the right of the heart is where this pressure rises: the Will Centre, the smallest place on the figure, with the most say in the material world. It is a motor, one of four, and what it makes is the strength to say you will do a thing, then do it. Its work is value: what you can do, and what you are worth. It works in bursts, then it must rest, and the rest is not a pause in the work; it is how the next burst is made. Pushed on without rest it burns out.

In about four people in ten this place is switched on, so the will is there every day: they decide, they follow through, and everyone near them borrows it while it lasts. Their trap is that they can push, so they do, until the body stops them. In the other six it is not, so they take in whatever will is in the room and make it louder, the doubt as much as the strength. Someone like that can spend a whole life proving a worth that never needed proving, and wear out doing it. What they have instead is a taste for what is worth anything, tested over the years, with nothing at all to prove. For them there is an old line about a garden: an orchid has no more worth than a daffodil.

### What completes it — the Channel of Community (40–37)
Stepping back on its own is only a person going away. Its other half is Gate 37, under the ribs, the pull to belong to a few people and see them provided for. Together they make the Channel of Community, which is a bargain: fair terms kept on both sides, then the strength to work without tiring on what you agreed to. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you, the going away only counts where the coming back is expected.


## BODY

### Physiology
The stomach is where this energy lives in you, and the reason is rest. It is the part of you that turns what you eat into what you run on, and does that only while you are at rest. It makes room for your meal before the meal arrives, then works on it for hours, unasked. When you are afraid, angry or straining, it stops; the blood goes to your muscles and the meal waits. When it is full, the will in you is content; empty, the will in you goes out to find food. What you force costs you what you had, and what you take in at rest is what you have to give.

It lies high on your left, under the ribs, below the tip of the breastbone. After a meal, rest a hand there and you can feel the fullness; on an empty morning the growl under your hand is its wall working on nothing. You are resting your hand on the part of you that only works when you leave it alone.

### Amino acid
The will in you has a substance, and the substance is glycine. It is the smallest of the twenty pieces you are built from, and your body makes most of what it needs, then takes the rest from the skin or bones of meat or fish. A third of the frame that holds you up is made of it, skin or bone, and in the cord of your back it is the signal that tells a nerve to stop firing.

This will is a chemistry, one you share with your kin, a family in your body whose path is not wasting strength. What that family releases is everything your body still holds against itself, until every part of you works as it was made to. That is the rest this energy ends in: not a mood, your body letting go of everything it held.

Storm overhead and still water underneath, that is the Tarot's picture of this card. The codon ring gives the Magician, the maker who turns will into form. The two trigrams each give their own faces: Thunder above as the Tower and Judgement, the shock that breaks the structure and the call that wakes the dead ground; Water below as the High Priestess and the Hanged Man, the one who perceives in the dark and the one who hangs still until the view turns right. All five together say one thing, a breaking overhead met by a deep patience beneath.

- **Ring Arcana — I · The Magician** (Manifestation): Through the Ring of Alchemy, this code carries the Magician, the adept who gathers the scattered forces and turns will into a made thing. The resolve at the heart of this code is his table laid with tools, the one mark fixed and brought into being.
- **Thunder (upper) — XVI · The Tower** (Shock, Upheaval): The Thunder breaking overhead wears the Tower, the sudden stroke that cracks the old structure open. The release of long tension is exactly this, a lightning that frees what was walled in by collapsing the wall.
- **Thunder (upper) — XX · Judgement** (Salvation, Renewal): Thunder also carries Judgement, the trumpet that wakes the buried life. Under the breaking rain the hundred seeds split at once, the whole ground answering a sound it cannot refuse.
- **Water (lower) — II · The High Priestess** (Perceive, Listen): The Water beneath wears the High Priestess, who does not act but reads the dark, who sits at the threshold and listens to what the danger is actually saying. The pit you were in was also a depth to be understood.
- **Water (lower) — XII · The Hanged Man** (Pause for Perspective): Water also carries the Hanged Man, suspended, willing to hang still and see the bind from upside down because forcing it will not free it. The patience inside the difficult release is his teaching.

### Immortals
Cao Guo Jiu stands above, the one of imperial blood who laid down his court tablet and walked away from inherited power, and Li Tie Guai below, the healer who came back through his own death carrying a gourd of medicine. Cao Guo Jiu walked away from what his birth owed him, and Li Tie Guai came back from death with the cure. Deliverance asks for both moves.

### Deeper correlation
- **Sky — Mercury:** the quick messenger force, the planet of nimble making and untying, that loosens what was knotted and carries the released thing across.
- **Hebrew letter — Bet, the House:** the dwelling, the enclosure that gives shelter, as the well that refills is the house a person returns to. The Magician's path stands on this letter, the maker who builds a place to rest.
