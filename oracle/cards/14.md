# UL 14 — Ancestors Bloom

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Plenty · Harvest · Drive · Working From Love · Somewhere To Go · Gratitude · Giving It Away

Some weeks you have more than you can carry and none of it weighs anything. The work you love is in front of you, the sun is high on all of it, and your hands know the next move before you have thought it. People turn up to help. Money turns up, or the one person you needed, and you did not go looking. It feels less like pushing and more like being drawn, low in the belly, a plain yes you could follow with your eyes shut, and the more you hand out of the load the less it weighs.

Then the yard goes quiet. The cart is still there, still full, and nobody has robbed it. It has stood since the day you took the job that paid over the one that pulled, and a standing cart leaks from the bottom where you cannot see. You keep working. Harder, at something you would not choose, and the harder you push the flatter it gets, until you wonder where the plenty went. It went nowhere. It is soaking into the ground under a wheel that has not turned.

Get it rolling. Toward anything you would do for the love of it, however short the first stretch, because what you have keeps only while it is going somewhere. Pick the one thing today the belly says yes to and do it all the way; let the rest come second. Then pour something off the back before you are sure you can spare it. Watch the level come back up.

## ICHING

### Combination
Fire sits over Heaven, the sun at the top of the sky over all of heaven's strength. In the name, great is a figure standing with arms and legs thrown wide, and having is a hand reaching for the moon, held the way the moon is held, for a night and gone. Everything is lit, everything is supplied, none of it is fastened to you. One open line holds the high seat, five firm lines answer upward, and down among them the big wagon stands loaded, with somewhere to go.

### Upper trigram — Fire (Li)
Fire, Li. One open line between two firm, a flame with no body of its own that lives on what it burns and tells one thing from another. On top, this trigram is the sun at the height of the day, showing the whole load with nothing left in shadow.

### Lower trigram — Heaven (Qian)
Heaven, Qian. Three firm lines, the forward motion that never tires, steady as the turning of the stars, the father. Below, this trigram is the pull under the wagon, the strength that keeps the load moving while the light above shows it where.

### Reading
You have a great deal, and the season is good, and that is harder than it sounds. The sun is high, nothing is hidden, and the strength underneath has not tired. What you have was not stolen and did not arrive by luck, and now you are standing next to a full wagon wondering what a full wagon is for.

The danger is not loss. It is fullness. A cup filled to the brim spills at the first step, and once you are full of what you have you begin to believe you made it, and from there it is a short road to the one who thinks he has what he has not and calls himself great. The hexagram answers this with its one open line, sitting in the ruler's seat above five strong ones. All that strength is held by an opening at the top, something soft and reachable that the strong lines answer to without being made to. Keep that opening. It is what lets the having stay in the middle, where what is stored does not spoil.

The counsel runs one way through all six lines. Stop what is harmful in yourself before it gets on the cart. Let the good show. Offer the harvest upward, to whatever is larger than your own house, because the one who cannot do that is owned by the load. Restrain the strength you have rather than lean on it. And keep the wheels turning, because the wagon is for going somewhere, and a lower layer hears the quieter danger under all this plenty, the small giving-way at the hub, which has a name of its own, compromise.

### Judgement
- Great possession. From the source, it goes through and grows.
- The open line holds the high seat, and the strong lines above and below answer it.
- Firm inside, bright outside, in step with heaven and the season.

### Image
- Fire high above heaven. Great possession.
- The one who has much stops what is harmful, lets the good show, and bends to what is above.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ no mingling with harm, the hard ground kept in mind. → becomes Hexagram 50, The Cauldron.
At the bottom, what you have is not much yet, and nobody is looking. Keep the wrong company off the cart, outside and in. A thought you feed is company too. Nothing here is a fault as long as you remember the ground is hard and the work repetitive. Struggle with it quietly and you come through clean, into the cauldron where raw things are cooked into use.

**Line 2** · _image:_ the big wagon loaded, with somewhere to go. → becomes Hexagram 30, The Clinging Fire.
The cart is big enough for the whole load, which sits in the middle where it will not spoil. What you have is real now and needs carrying out to be seen. Choose the direction and go. A full wagon parked is the one thing this line does not allow. Carry it lightly through whatever the road throws up and it becomes the fire that clings to what it burns.

**Line 3** · _image:_ a prince laying his harvest before the son of heaven. → becomes Hexagram 38, Opposition.
You have standing now, and a harvest, and the move is to give it upward, into the service of something bigger than your own house. The prince does this and is not made poorer. The small man cannot, because what he owns owns him. Notice which of the two is speaking in you when the offering comes due, and give, and the strain of being seen to have eases.

**Line 4** · _image:_ great means held in check, the drum unanswered. → becomes Hexagram 26, Great Taming.
The strength is here and the brightness is here, and the counsel is to lean on neither. Somewhere a drum is beating for you to show what you have. Do not answer it. Power that does not presume on itself looks, from outside, like less than it is, and inside it keeps growing. Keep it in check and the great taming begins, the holding that lets a herd fatten.

**Line 5** · _image:_ trust going out and coming back, a dignity that needs no preparing. → becomes Hexagram 1, The Creative.
The open line in the high seat, the one all the others answer to. You are trusted because you are reachable, and the trust comes back the same way. Sincere for sincere. Let it carry a plain dignity, the kind not assembled beforehand; preparing to be impressive proves you doubt you are. Stay open at the top and every strong line beneath you moves, back toward the pure creative.

**Line 6** · _image:_ help coming down from heaven itself. → becomes Hexagram 34, Great Power.
At the top of great possession, firm and open have come into match, and strength and clear sight are one thing. Nothing here fails to benefit. This is the late fruit of keeping the load humble all the way up, trusting what is above, so that heaven finally lends its hand. From here the power is great and plain, and the question is what you will do with it.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Compromise · _Gift:_ Competence · _Siddhi:_ Bounteousness

### Shadow — Compromise
You were born with a wagon already loaded. A particular way of doing things that nobody else has, an appetite to work at it, and a plenty that was meant to be spent on that work. Almost nobody drives off with it. The load is let go one small surrender at a time, each too small to look like a decision.

The kind that costs you is the inner kind. Staying home because someone you love is unwell costs nothing; love made that choice. Taking the job that pays over the one that pulls, or letting the thing you wanted at nine slide a little further off each year until by forty it is not mentioned, those cost you every time. The tell is always the same. You do not feel good. Something low down contracts, and the wagon, still standing in the yard, still full to look at, starts to leak from underneath.

Compromise is contagious and it hides in sensible words. You call it being realistic, being a team player, and you try to fix the flatness by pushing harder at the very work that is draining you. The contraction is a better guide than the words. It only happens because the load is still there to lose, and one act a day that does not sell it is enough to feel it stop leaking.

### Repressive nature — Impotent
One answer to the leak is to go slack. You stop reaching, go along with whatever the day brings, and call it easygoing. Nobody sees weakness; agreeing looks like peace. But the power meant to be spent sits choked, and even the body's appetite for making, in bed and at the bench, goes dim.

### Reactive nature — Enslaved
Proving is the other way out. You work hard at something you did not choose, to show you count, and the recognition, when it comes, does not land, because what was in the wagon never got out. Push, and the rage is right there. Power that must prove itself is owned by its audience.

### Gift — Competence
Stop making the inner compromises, one small act a day, and the wagon starts to roll. Slowly at first. It moves at its own pace and you cannot hurry it, but every day it is not standing it picks up speed, and what shows up once it is moving is competence.

Competence is what working looks like on someone who loves the work, and it looks like a saw drawn toward the belly rather than shoved away from it, a finer cut for less effort, in rhythm with the grain. The work goes low, into the belly, and from there it finds the simplest way through anything. It has a way of doing things that is only yours, and it is loose enough to take on a job it has never done and learn it.

You cannot do it in hiding. The enthusiasm leaks out of you the way the compromise used to, only this time it spreads, and it turns a few people into a team and gets an idea off the page; if you would rather work alone, dig a little, and you usually find fear of your own size dressed up as modesty. And it draws. The right help and the right money walk toward a wagon that is moving. Get clear on what you want to serve, and then give the wheels a shove.

### Siddhi — Bounteousness
The wagon at the far end is never full and never empty, because it is always being unloaded. Whoever was driving it is gone. There is just the giving, in every direction, and a strange arithmetic under it: the more goes out, the more arrives, and the more arrives the more there is to be grateful for. Gratitude is the ember it all started from, a spark the size of a seed that you cupped in your hands and breathed on, and now it is a sun.

The sun gives because giving is what it is, and the giving is what feeds it. Nothing in this takes. Receiving is still here, and receiving is a different thing; it feeds the thanks. Possession in great measure turns out to mean fertility, the overflowing kind, prosperity moving through one body into other lives without anyone managing it. Think of someone like that and something in your own week loosens and grows. One life lived like this pulls the whole species a little way toward plenty, and that is what one cell in a great body is for.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 14, Power Skills
It comes up from low in the body as a surge: get up, get working, make more than you need, and turn it into something others can live on. It pulses. High for a stretch and then gone, and on the high days you can outwork anyone in the room while on the gone days you cannot understand where it went. What it wants is resources, in the plainest sense, money and material and the means to keep going, and what it wants them for is only partly you. The surge is built to generate more than one person can use.

People who carry this get called things. Greedy. A workaholic who cannot switch off, materialistic, too much. From inside it is a pressure to produce that will not leave you alone, and it turns sour only when it has nothing worth producing for. Aimed at something you love, it is stamina. Aimed at nothing, it is restlessness, and restlessness is the same power with no road.

### Where it lives — the Sacral Center
You feel it under the navel, as a sound before it is a thought. Something crosses your path and the belly says uh-huh or uh-uh before your mind has arranged an opinion, and that sound is the whole instrument for knowing where the wagon should go. The energy here comes from responding, and it renews itself only when it is spent on a yes. Spend it on a no, on the thing you agreed to because someone asked nicely, and it drains and does not come back, which is how you, with more stamina than anyone you know, end up burnt out. Leave it unspent and it goes the way of a battery in a car nobody drives.

You wait, then. You let things come toward you and you listen for the sound. Other people will see the engine and want to borrow it, and the only guard you have is the plain yes and the plain no, honoured every time. When you are producing nothing, look there first.

### What completes it — the Channel of the Beat (2–14)
On its own the surge has no idea where to go. It can generate for a lifetime and still leave you dissatisfied, because producing is not the same as heading somewhere, and a full wagon with no destination is just a heavier thing to stand next to. What this drive reaches for is the other gate on the channel, the direction of the self, the part of you that quietly knows which way your life faces, sitting in the seat of identity higher up the body. That part cannot move a thing by itself. It can only point.

When the two meet you get a beat. Your own, and audibly not anyone else's. The knowing of the way gives the engine a road and the engine gives the knowing its legs, and the whole life starts to move at a tempo that people around you may find odd and cannot argue with. The rhythm is the sign it is working. Grinding at someone else's tempo is the surge with the pointing part unplugged, and the fix is never more grinding.

## BODY

### Physiology
Under the warmth you feel when the work is right there is a long coiled tube, the small intestine, folded back on itself many times beneath the navel, and it is doing the plainest version of what the whole code does. The meal arrives broken down from above. The wall of the tube is lined with millions of tiny fingers that reach into it and draw the goodness across into the blood, and what cannot feed you is passed on down and let go. It draws, slowly, and it works best when the belly is soft and unhurried.

That is why the gut is the first thing to tell you about a compromise. Take the job you did not want and the belly tightens around the meal, appetite goes strange, and the tube that was meant to draw starts to clench and hurry. What comes in stops becoming you. The return is felt there too. The day you do one thing for the love of it, the warmth is back under the navel, you are hungry at the right hour, and the load coming in is being turned into a person again.

### Amino acid
The wagon goes down to a molecule, and the molecule's job is unloading. Out of lysine, one of the nine letters your body cannot make and has to be fed, the body builds the carrier that takes stored fat into the furnace of the cell to be burned. Without that carrier the fat stays where it is, in the store, and you can be carrying weeks of fuel and still have nothing to run on. Having is one thing. Being able to spend it is another chemistry entirely, and this code runs on the second.

You were born with the plenty. Whether it could ever be moved into use was the open question, and the body answers it with something it had to be given first, a letter that came in from outside and turned the store into a road. What you have keeps only while it is going somewhere.

## RELATIONS

_Read alongside UL 13, these two are the harvest and the people it feeds, one wagon and the crowd it was loaded for._

### Pair — UL 13, Universal Crest (Fellowship with Men)
Turn the hexagram over and the sun that stood above heaven is now the flame under it, out in the open where people gather round. That is the pair, and the two are one picture read from both ends, what one person has in great measure and the company that having was always for. A full wagon with no one to share it is only storage. Fellowship with no one bringing anything is only a meeting. The old books put them side by side and say the harvest is many, and the gathering is close. Read together, one is the food and the other is the table.

### Inverse — UL 13, Universal Crest
The inverse here is the same card as the pair, so there is one bond and it is written above. What the turning itself shows is worth one look. The single open line that sat in the ruler's seat, the reachable softness that held all the strength, drops to the second place, down among the people in the field. The openness that kept your plenty humble is, seen from the other side, the openness that lets strangers in. Same line, other end of the wagon.

### Programming partner — UL 8, Odyssey of Freedom
Across the Gene Keys this code is wired to UL 8, Odyssey of Freedom, and the wiring runs through style. Both ends have a low season, compromise on this side and a settling for the ordinary on that, and each keeps the other stuck: plenty with no style of giving stays a hoard, and style with no plenty behind it stays a pose. Rise together and the wagon is full and carried in a way that could only be yours. Flip every line of this hexagram and you arrive at the same card, so the old books and the new agree on the partner.

### Codon ring — Ring of Fire
Here the spark from UL 1, Earth's Breath, has been fed and has something to burn, and what it throws off is a harvest. Two cards only in this ring, one fire at two moments. Over there is the wanting to make, the flame that starts from nothing and asks for nothing. Here is the making carried all the way out to a loaded wagon and handed round. That card starts things. This one is what starting was for.

### Tarot
The loaded wagon under the noon sun is dealt five faces here, one from the ring and two from each trigram. The ring gives Temperance, pouring from one cup into another without spilling a drop. A full load has to learn that. Fire above gives the Sun and the Emperor, the plain radiance and the seat it rules from. Heaven below gives the Fool and the Empress, the open first step and the ground that makes it fruitful. Read in order, that is trust, fruit, rule, and the pouring out.

- **Ring Arcana — XIV · Temperance** (Flow, Alignment): Through the Ring of Fire this code carries Temperance, who pours between two cups and loses nothing in the passing. Force and patience blended until they are one drinkable thing. Temperance is the load in motion, the plenty kept fresh by being poured.
- **Fire (upper) — XIX · The Sun** (Glory, Clarity): The Fire above wears the Sun, the noon glare that shows everything and asks nothing for it. Giving at full strength, uncounted, refilled by the giving.
- **Fire (upper) — IV · The Emperor** (Dominion, Lead): Fire also carries the Emperor, who has found the seat and governs from it without getting up. The lines keep asking for strength that is held in check rather than leaned on, and here it is, a ruler set over the plenty instead of a mob running off with it.
- **Heaven (lower) — 0 · The Fool** (New Beginning): The Heaven at the root wears the Fool, the step off the edge before the outcome is known. Under all this having is a willingness to begin without proof, which is where every load was first picked up.
- **Heaven (lower) — III · The Empress** (Abundance, Nurture): Heaven also carries the Empress, the field that turns seed into harvest. The force below becomes the plenty above by way of her, force made fruitful rather than stored, which is why the old name for this hexagram is also the word for harvest.

### Immortals
Two of the eight are here, one for each trigram. Over the Fire sits Lu Dong Bin, the scholar who carries a sword for cutting through what is false and who is said to have refused to ascend until he had helped the people below him. Under the Heaven is Han Xiangzi, whose flute was said to make flowers open out of season and things grow where they should not yet. A blade to prune the load, and a tune to bring it into bloom.

### Deeper correlation
- **Sky — Sagittarius:** the mutable fire that aims past what it can see, the arrow loosed at a far mark. Plenty with a direction, which is the one thing a full wagon needs.
- **Hebrew letter — Samech, the Prop:** the peg that holds the tent up, the staff a climber leans on. On the Tree the path runs from the heart down to the foundation, Tiphareth to Yesod, and the letter says that what rises high needs something under it to lean on, or it comes down.
