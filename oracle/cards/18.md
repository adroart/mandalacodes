# UL 18 — Liberation of the Greater

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Seeing the Flaw First · Mending What You Inherit · Strong Without Being Hard · Waiting to Be Asked · Needing to Be Right · Judging from the Heart · Seeing the Best in People

An instinct for what has gone wrong lives in you, and so does the wish to put it right. When something comes to you already spoiled, in a family or in a piece of work, you are the one who sees it. Once you start on it, you stay, and you keep making it a little better. Held well, this is integrity. You keep a high standard, and it shows in how you stand.

At times the faults are all you can see, in the people nearest you and in yourself. Ask whether you would rather be right or happy. What bothers you most in someone else usually points to something in you that wants settling, so start there.

Treat the life around you as your own body, only greater. The more of it you care for as yours, the less personally you take it, and the lighter your days become. Mend what you can, one thing at a time. What was spoiled becomes sound again, steadier than it was before.

## ICHING

### Combination
Mountain over Wind is the hexagram of setting right what was spoiled. Wind is the gentleness in you that finds its way in. Mountain is the part of you that holds still. The stillness sits above because that is how a family or a workplace spoils, when those in charge stop and everyone else goes along. This hexagram has long been read as wind blocked at a mountain's foot, where the air goes stale. A bowl left standing too long spoils, and the cure is to clean it and use it. The same spoiling, watched and stopped in time, makes rice wine. You can make good what went bad.

### Upper trigram — Mountain (Gen)
Mountain is the part of you that stands firm and will not be moved off what is right. It sits on top because it keeps you from being dragged down by what has gone bad around you. Mountain has long been read as a limit, the place past which you cannot see. At a limit you stop, and stopping lets you see clearly what is in front of you. On its own, it turns hard and stubborn, and nothing near it moves.

### Lower trigram — Wind (Xun)
Wind is the patience in you that goes in quietly where force would break what is left. It lies underneath because every repair rests on that patience. Wind has long been read as a tree, rooted below and spreading above. You work the same way, slowly and from the roots up. On its own, it only follows along with what is there, and the spoiling goes on.

### Reading
Something has come to you already going bad. It went wrong in the hands of the people before you, and now it is in yours. The spoiling is not your fault. Setting it right is your work, and this is a good time for it, because what has gone bad can still be mended.

This time asks you to act, not to wait for things to sort themselves out. Before you start, look hard at how it went wrong. When you finish, look again at what your work has changed. Take up what the ones before you meant, and leave what they did. Stir up whatever has gone still, in yourself and in the people around you, and give them heart.

Bend your back to it. No one is ever fully ready to carry the work a family or a place leaves undone. Carrying it is how you grow into it.

### Moving lines

**Line 1** · _image:_ setting right what the father spoiled, and a son to do it. → becomes Hexagram 26, Great Taming.
You take up the work your father left spoiled, while the damage is still small. Take up what he meant and leave what he did, and no blame stays on him. There is danger here, because what is let slide now grows. Be careful at the start, and it ends well.

**Line 2** · _image:_ setting right what the mother spoiled. → becomes Hexagram 52, Keeping Still.
What was spoiled this time came from your mother, and it lives inside love. Correct it and you hurt the love. Leave it and you hurt what is right. Only someone strong who is not hard can do this. Bend where you must, keep to the middle, and do not push your rightness all the way.

**Line 3** · _image:_ setting right what the father spoiled, with too strong a hand. → becomes Hexagram 4, Youthful Folly.
You set right what your father spoiled, with a heavier hand than it needed. There is a little regret in that, and no great fault. You were right to act. Keep going, and let the regret teach your hand.

**Line 4** · _image:_ going easy on what the father spoiled. → becomes Hexagram 50, The Cauldron.
You see what your father spoiled and let it be, telling yourself it can wait. It grows while you wait, and going on this way ends in shame. The fault lies with the one who will not look. Turn and face what you have been choosing not to see.

**Line 5** · _image:_ setting right what the father spoiled, with praise. → becomes Hexagram 57, The Gentle Wind.
You set right what your father spoiled, and people praise you for it. You come to it with an open heart, admit what you do not know and praise what others do well. Their clear sight helps you past what you cannot see yourself. Be honest with people, and the goodwill it brings undoes what force could not.

**Line 6** · _image:_ serving neither king nor lord, and looking higher. → becomes Hexagram 46, Pushing Upward.
The work is done, and you no longer work to please anyone above you. You work for the purpose rather than a person, and not for pay or fame. Your footing is firm now, and nothing needs correcting. Keep your eyes on what is highest, and what is true in you will outlast you.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Judgement · _Gift:_ Integrity · _Siddhi:_ Perfection

### Shadow — Judgement
The flaw is the first thing you see, and the mind that sees it was built that way. What goes wrong at this height is that you become your verdicts. Each one feels like who you are and makes you feel safe, so you defend it. Holding them that tightly makes you tense, and people feel the tension before they hear a single opinion. What you think about a thing hardly matters. How tightly you hold it does.

It usually sets in between fourteen and twenty-one. A young mind sees faults everywhere, in parents and teachers. It escapes them by freezing into one fixed view, usually borrowed from its friends. It feels like safety, and it stops the mind from growing. Much of a day's thinking stays there, in quiet complaints about the people nearest you and worry over money and health. Every complaint harms twice, the one it is about and the one who makes it.

### Repressive nature — Inferiority
Here the judging turns inward and stays there. Judged too hard in their teens, they compare themselves with whoever seems above them and always come up short. So they go along with the majority instead of standing up for what they believe, and quietly undermine themselves.

### Reactive nature — Superiority
Aimed outward, the judging challenges every authority, starting with the parents. When the adults around them hold no firm line, an old anger sets in, and every rule becomes theirs to break. They are volatile, and their whole sense of who they are rests on putting other people down.

### Gift — Integrity
Integrity is a word builders use for how much load a structure can bear before it fails. In you it is physical, a steady core that shows in how you stand and move before you have said anything. Every animal has it without trying, which is why nothing in a forest judges. You were born with it too, and still you have to earn it. Once earned it stays, because it was your true nature all along.

The eye that found every flaw turns first on your own thinking, and clearing out old verdicts gives you back a clear head. From there you hold a high standard in all you do and ask it of others, from the heart, which is never cruel. You go against the grain calmly, leading by example. Part of you is in the game and part of you rests outside it, which is where the calm comes from. Having seen how a person is shaped young, you can help others finish growing up.

### Siddhi — Perfection
The world is perfect as it is, and now you can see it, the flaws included. The mind that judged goes quiet. The clearer it is that nothing needs mending, the more you mend until the day you die. The more good you do, the more you see could be done, and that restlessness is the work itself.

In anyone, even someone who seems wholly bad, you see only what is highest. Holding that sight lifts them. You could leave the world behind, and you stay. Your body belongs to the whole body of people, and no one is fully well until everyone is.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 18, Correction
Gate 18 gives you an eye for what has gone out of line, the detail everyone else has stepped over. It arrives as an instinct, before any thought, and it is usually right. It is less about getting a thing perfect than about making it a little better, again and again. The hard part is timing. Offered before anyone asks, a correction is heard as criticism, and people push away the very thing that would help them. Then you are the one who feels judged. So wait until you are asked, and give it to bring more joy between people, never to prove you were right. It is worth knowing how it feels to be right and keep it to yourself.

### Where it lives — the Spleen Center
The Spleen Center sits low on the left side of the body, and it is your oldest alarm. It works through the senses, in the present, and tells you at once whether a thing is safe. It looks after your health, and it keeps time. It speaks once and softly, so a busy mind can talk straight over it. It holds the fear of being judged and the fear of things not being right. Seen for what they are, those fears ease into a relaxed alertness, like a cat asleep in the sun with one ear still turning.

A little more than half of people have this centre defined, and for them the knowing is there every day. Their bodies tend to glow with health, and the risk is taking it for granted, pushing on past feeling unwell. In the rest it is open, and the body is more sensitive. It knows it is getting ill before anything shows, and it takes in the fears of the people nearby and makes them louder. The work there is telling which fears are your own, and the reward is a fine sense of what is healthy for other people.

### What completes it — the Channel of Judgment (18–58)
Gate 58, at the base of the body, is the pressure to be alive and to enjoy it. Joined to Gate 18 it makes the Channel of Judgment, which sees what needs putting right so there can be more joy. A body stays alive on its vitality, and this is what the correcting is for. Gate 58 may be in you or in someone close by, and with both present the seeing becomes service.

## BODY

### Physiology
The lymph carries this energy in the body, and the reason is washing. All day your blood leaves water behind in the spaces between your cells, along with whatever the cells are done with. None of it is left there. A second set of vessels gathers it, two or three litres a day, and carries it through several hundred small filters. Anything wrong in it is met there, before the water goes back into your blood. Your body has no pump for it. It moves when you move, lifted by each step and each breath, and it pools while you sit.

The clear drop that beads on a graze before any blood comes is that same water. The word lymph means clear water, so you have already seen what it washes.

### Amino acid
The energy that puts things right has a substance in you, and the substance is alanine. It is one of the twenty small pieces every part of you is built from. Your body makes this one itself, from what the sugar in your blood becomes as it burns. There is more of it in you than of almost any other piece. The fears this energy carries lie deep in your body, in the defences that fight off what does not belong in you. Going through them, never around them, is what opens what is higher in you.

This substance is made from what already runs in your blood. You share it with three other energies, each carried by a different part of your growing up.

## RELATIONS

_UL 17 is this card's pair, its inverse and its partner at once: going gladly along, where this one sets right what going along has spoiled._

### Pair — UL 17, Pearl of Christos (Following)
This energy mends what went wrong, and it runs out when the thing works again. Pearl of Christos is where it goes next: the sense of where things are heading, which people gladly follow. Open it when the repair is done and you need to know what it was for. Whatever is followed long enough will one day need mending again.

### Inverse — UL 17, Pearl of Christos (Following)
Turn this energy end for end and setting things right becomes going along with them. Pearl of Christos is the same road walked gladly, before anything has gone wrong. Open it when you have mended enough and it is time to follow. Going along too easily is how things start to spoil.

### Programming partner — UL 17, Pearl of Christos
This energy looks at what is wrong right in front of it. Pearl of Christos holds what it lacks: the far sight that knows where things are going. A repair means little until you know what the thing was for. Open it when you can see every flaw and none of the purpose.

### Codon ring — Codon Ring of Matter
This energy shapes the mind between fourteen and twenty-one, when your views are formed. It belongs to a family of four called the Ring of Matter. Together they shape how soundly you grew in body, feeling and mind. UL 57, Flight of the Tao, carries the months before you were born. UL 46, Fountain of Light, carries your body in your first seven years. UL 48, Doorways of the Unknown, carries your feelings in the seven after. This one carries your mind.

### Tarot
Five of the tarot's major cards, its Arcana, come to this energy through the ring and the two trigrams.

- **Ring Arcana — IV · The Emperor** (Dominion, Lead): The Emperor sets the standard a thing is measured against, and your mending needs one to aim at.
- **Mountain (upper) — V · The Hierophant** (Tradition, Teach): The Hierophant hands down the ways that came before, which are what you are asked to mend.
- **Mountain (upper) — IX · The Hermit** (Wisdom, Right Path): The Hermit steps away to find the right path, as you look higher once the work is done.
- **Wind (lower) — X · Wheel of Fortune** (Turning Point): The Wheel of Fortune turns, and going bad is one turn of it, with a fresh start on the other side.
- **Wind (lower) — XIII · Death** (Metamorphosis): Death clears away an old form so a new one can grow, as a spoiled thing has to end before it is made new.

### Immortals
Mountain above carries Zhang Guo Lao, who rides his donkey facing backwards. You look back the same way, to see where a thing went wrong. Wind below brings He Xian Gu, the one woman among the eight, who holds a lotus. The lotus comes up clean out of mud, and so can what you mend.

### Deeper correlation
- **Sky — Aries:** the first sign of spring, the start of the year, the fresh start your mending gives a spoiled thing.
- **Hebrew letter — Heh, the Window:** a window lets in air and light, which a shut, stale place needs before it can be put right.
