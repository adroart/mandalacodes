# UL 32 — Art of Living

## CODE

_Keywords:_ A Feeling for What Lasts · Knowing What to Keep · Taking Your Time · Knowing When to Let Go · Afraid of Failing · Rushing Through the Day · Living Well

When the newness of something has worn off, the long middle begins. A feeling for what will last comes alive in you here. You can tell which plans and friendships have a future in them before you could say why. You know what to keep and what to let go.

Some days a quiet panic runs under everything. You rush from one task to the next toward a rest that never comes, and underneath it is the fear of failing. Slow down inside the rush, and it loosens its grip. An ending met with grace is never a failure.

This is the art of living: to take your time with things and enjoy more of your days. Let people in, even when it hurts, because being opened is what makes you rich. Ask what you want to leave behind, and give your days to that. Lived this way, an ordinary life becomes something worth handing on.

## ICHING

### Combination
Thunder over Wind is the hexagram of lasting, of how a thing goes on once it has begun. Thunder is the part of you that stirs things up and acts where others can see. Wind is the part of you that works quietly underneath and never stops. The stirring sits on top because what lasts needs action on the outside and patience within. Thunder and wind have long been read as a couple married for years. One of the two makes the moves the world sees, while the other keeps the home going. Your life lasts the way the seasons do, by changing as it goes. What lasts in you is one bold part and one patient part working together.

### Upper trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder is what gets you up in the morning and sets you to work on the heavy thing. It sits on the outside because this is the part of lasting other people can see: the effort, the start made again each day. Thunder has long been read as a power rising from deep in the ground, the way a shoot breaks out of its seed. Each fresh start on an old task comes up from that same depth. On its own, thunder keeps shaking things up after there is nothing left to move.

### Lower trigram — Wind (Xun)
Wind is the steady care you give a thing when nobody is watching. It sits underneath because it is the ground the whole effort rests on, the part that feeds it. Wind has long been read as a breeze and as a tree, and both get in everywhere a little at a time. Roots go down a little further each year, and a habit you keep up does the same in you. On its own, wind goes on entering with nowhere in particular to go.

### Reading
You are in something for the long haul now. The start is behind you, and what matters is how you keep it going. You may be carrying more than was ever fairly yours. The way to bear it is to keep changing inside while you stand firm on the outside.

Bear the hard days with dignity, and the good ones with humility. Change yourself, and leave others to change in their own time, since forcing it on someone is oppressive. Look for allies who are least like you, because a bond between different people holds the longest. Keep a direction, since it helps to have somewhere to go.

Your legs do the work now: the feet that carry you forward and the thighs that bear the weight. Stand the way someone stands through a long day, with feet planted and knees soft.

### Judgement
- Lasting. It goes well, and there is no blame.
- It pays to hold steady. It helps to have somewhere to go.

You dig for depth on the first day, wanting the settled form of a thing before it has grown one. Forcing yourself to last is not lasting. Push now and nothing you try will help. Let the thing find its own pace, even when that feels like losing control.

**Line 3** · _image:_ a start that never reaches its end. → becomes Hexagram 40, Deliverance.

**Line 4** · _image:_ a field with no game in it. → becomes Hexagram 46, Pushing Upward.

**Line 5** · _image:_ one way kept from start to finish. → becomes Hexagram 28, Great Preponderance.

**Line 6** · _image:_ restlessness kept up at the top. → becomes Hexagram 50, The Cauldron.
You keep yourself stirred up long after the force has run out. You feel full while you are empty, raised high and close to a fall. Restlessness kept up for years achieves nothing. Choose one long road and commit to it, then stop wearing yourself out on motion.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Failure · _Gift:_ Preservation · _Siddhi:_ Veneration

### Shadow — Failure
Failing, deep in the body, means being cut off. Long before you, the people you come from learned that to be pushed out of the group was to die. That lesson is still in you. Today the fear has moved into money. The bigger the number, the safer you feel. Yet it never settles, because fear always needs something to feed on. Take the money away and it would find the next thing. Close beside it is the fear that your family line will end, with no one to hand anything to.

The fear made people stingy in spirit, keeping to their own small circle, and it kept the species alive while it did. It lives deep in the body, and the mind feeds it by telling its story again. Let go into the way life moves, and you find the support was there all along.

### Repressive nature — Fundamentalist
This person pulls in tight. The rules are held so hard they cut, and everyone outside the circle is suspect. They are choking themselves in body, in feeling and in money, starved of breath and of help. Groups made this way stop meeting the wider world, and in time they die out.

### Reactive nature — Disjointed
Here the thread of a life has been dropped. Underneath is anger that nothing holds them up but themselves, and it drives one bad decision after another. Each decision cuts them off a little further from what could support them. Their days have no rhythm, and nothing they start lasts.

### Gift — Preservation
Keeping things alive is what this energy becomes, and its whole art is choosing which things deserve to go on. You sense the long run of a situation. You can hold your energy or your money back from what tempts you but would not serve. You trust a feeling about someone that no reason supports, and it proves right. The balance is between holding back and taking a chance. The one who buries his coin for fear of losing it loses it.

The craft is grafting. You keep the old rootstock, the part that has proved strong, and set the young shoot onto it. Then one day you see that what you fought to preserve was never the real thing, and its shell cracks. You can plant a few trees every day around your home, lose most of them, and keep planting through wars and changes of government. In time you stand in a forest where the ground was bare, with villages living off it. Whether or not anyone learns your name, you will know.

### Siddhi — Veneration
Reverence is what remains once the fear has burned away. You see your place in the long chain of living things, with lives behind you and lives still to come. You look at it with no self in the way, so all that is left is wonder. You stand on the shoulders of those who came before, and you offer your shoulders to whoever comes next. Even the smallest insect is holding you up. You feel tiny and huge in the same breath. Wisdom lives in one person for a while, then moves on to the next who can hold it. The one discipline it asks is to pause and listen inward.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 32, Endurance
Gate 32 is a sense of what will last, and it sits in the Spleen Center. It reads a plan, a job or a friendship for whether it has a future, and it usually knows before you could explain. Growth under it comes in cycles rather than leaps, because real change takes time. The drive is to prepare, to set the stage so you are ready when the moment comes, without dragging it closer.

The fear of failing presses from the same place. It can stop you from trying at all, or from preparing, so you are not ready when the time is right. It can also push you too hard, too fast, for too long. The line to find is between holding on and clinging.

### Where it lives — the Spleen Center
The Spleen Center is the body's oldest awareness, the alertness that keeps you safe and well without a word. It works through the senses. It is the centre of time, telling you in each moment whether to move. It also holds the survival fears. Gate 32's fear is the one that says: I might fail, so I will not try.

About 55 in 100 people have this centre defined. For them the hunch comes steadily, speaks once and softly, and does not repeat. The risk is letting the mind talk over it. Followed, it keeps the body well. The other 45 in 100 have it open. Time tends to slip for them, and they take in the fears of the people around them as if they were their own. They also tend to hold on to jobs and relationships longer than is good for them. What they have instead is a fine sense of what is healthy for someone else, and their best decisions are slow ones.

### What completes it — the Channel of Transformation (32–54)
Gate 32 can tell which big dreams to follow, but it has no push of its own. Gate 54 sits in the Root Center at the base of the body. It is ambition, the drive to rise from where you started. Together they make the Channel of Transformation, which picks the right dream and sees it through, in work or in waking up. Gate 54 may be in you or in someone close to you, and the dream moves only when both are present.

## BODY

### Physiology
Keeping is the reason this energy is given to the spleen. Your blood passes through it many times a day, and it sorts what it finds. It retires what is worn out and keeps what still serves. A red cell lives about four months, and most of them end here, taken apart. Their iron is kept and sent back to be built into new cells, so your body loses almost none of it. The red in your blood today was carried by cells long gone. In the old medicine the spleen holds the blood inside its vessels and moves the water through you. It also turns what you eat into what you live on.

It sits high under your left ribs, behind the stomach, about the size of a fist. Look at the pink under your fingernails. That colour is iron your spleen has kept for you, cell after cell, for years.

### Amino acid
Keeping what matters has a chemistry in you, and one substance in it is asparaginic acid. The letters your body is written in are partly built from it. Joined to one other piece, it is the sweetener printed on a can of diet drink. In a body that keeps and counts, the fear of death and the worry about money run together, older than any bank account.

What is yours in it is the fear of dying with your life unlived. The same chemistry holds the old root and the young shoot, what has lasted for generations with the new set onto it. You share this chemistry with one other energy, the one that fears the ending itself.

## RELATIONS

_UL 31 is this card's pair and its inverse: the pull that begins a bond, where this one keeps it going through the years._

### Pair — UL 31, Theater of Truth (Influence)
This energy keeps a bond going through the years, and it runs thin when the pull that started it is forgotten. Theater of Truth is where it goes next: the attraction that draws two people together in the first place. Open it when something long-lasting has gone flat and needs its spark again. Anything that lasts starts with a pull.

### Inverse — UL 31, Theater of Truth (Influence)
Turn this energy over and the long marriage becomes the first meeting. Theater of Truth is the same bond seen from its start, when one person moved another. Open it when you have forgotten why you began.

### Programming partner — UL 42, Moving to Perfection
This energy knows what to keep, and it can hold on too long. Moving to Perfection holds what it lacks: the ease of letting a thing end so the next has room. Between the two is the whole breath of a life, keeping and releasing. Open it when holding on has started to cost more than it gives.

### Codon ring — Ring of Illusion
This energy keeps a life going, and under it lies an old fear of dying before that life has been lived. It belongs with one other card in the Ring of Illusion, whose theme is the fears that look solid until you face them. UL 28, Becoming the Mystery, carries the fear of death itself. This one carries the fear of failing to live well.

### Tarot
Five of the tarot's major cards arrive through the ring and the two trigrams, each a turn your life can take.

- **Ring Arcana — XVIII · The Moon** (Confusion, Flux): The Moon lights your path only halfway, where old fears look larger than they are.
- **Thunder (upper) — XVI · The Tower** (Shock, Upheaval): The Tower falls in one stroke and frees you from what you held too tightly.
- **Thunder (upper) — XX · Judgement** (Salvation, Renewal): Judgement calls you up to begin again, changed.
- **Wind (lower) — X · Wheel of Fortune** (Turning Point): The Wheel turns through good years and lean ones, and you keep your place through both.
- **Wind (lower) — XIII · Death** (Metamorphosis): Death ends one shape of your life so the next can grow.

### Immortals
Thunder above carries Cao Guojiu, the nobleman who left the court and its riches. He is the part of you that can walk away from comfort to live truer. Wind below carries He Xian Gu, who chose a simple life in the mountains. She is the part of you that needs little to keep going. Between them, what lasts in you is a way of living, not the things you own.

### Deeper correlation
- **Sky — Pisces:** the last sign of the year, where one cycle dissolves into the next and your old patterns loosen.
- **Hebrew letter — Qoph, the Back of the Head:** the part of you below waking thought, where the old patterns of a life are kept.
