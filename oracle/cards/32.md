# UL 32 — Art of Living

## CODE

_Keywords:_ Staying With It · Afraid of Failing · Counting What You Have · Knowing What to Keep · Letting Go of the Rest · Living Well

This energy is the art of lasting, the way a thing goes on after the newness has worn off. It lives in the long middle of things: the marriage years past the wedding, the work that no longer announces itself, the practice kept on a day when it gives nothing back. What lasts does not hold still.

Carried well, it comes with one knowledge: the only failure is the failure to live well. Under it sits a fear older than you, the body's memory that to be cut off from the others was once to die, and in a modern life that fear hides inside money, so that no amount arriving ever quite settles it. What it wants is not the number. It wants to belong to something that goes on, and it is fed by every choice that ties you to a larger life rather than cutting you off from one. An ending is not a failure when it ends with grace. Letting life in, even where it hurts, is what makes you stronger.

Keep the strong old root and put the new growth onto it. Some of what you have is worth carrying to the next generation, and some is dead wood. Knowing which is the whole craft, and letting the dead wood go takes the same nerve as saving the rest. There will be days of a low hum of panic, rushing from one thing to the next to reach some rest that never comes, and that hum is the fear of failing with the words taken out. Slow down inside it, and the things worth keeping will still be there.

## ICHING

### Combination
Thunder over Wind: the shock that moves above, the breeze that works below, and the two go on together without wearing out. Both are wood, so the picture is a tree, stirring at its crown while its roots hold. The name means to continue in the same spirit: constant, habitual, renewing itself as it goes.

### Upper trigram — Thunder (Zhen)
Thunder is the rousing, the force that starts things moving, and what thunder does is wake. It comes up from below the ground, sudden, loud, over in a moment, then comes again. In a family it is the eldest son, the first to move, the one who goes out ahead. In you it is the stir at the top of a long thing: the morning you begin again, the rousing that keeps a lasting thing from settling into sleep. Its risk is agitation. A stir with nothing under it is only noise, and thunder that never stops is a storm, not a season.

### Upper and lower share Wood
Both halves of this figure are wood. Thunder is the wood that moves, the branch in the wind, and Wind is the wood that roots, the trunk going down into the ground. A tree is the two at once: it sways at the top while the bottom does not move, and the swaying is how it survives the weather. That is what lasting looks like from outside.

### Lower trigram — Wind (Xun)
Below the thunder is Wind, which is also wood: the breeze that enters everywhere without being asked, the root that goes down a little further every year. What wind does is keep going. It is never strong, it never stops, and given time it wears a stone smooth. In a family it is the eldest daughter, the steady one, the one still there when the others have come and gone. In you it is the patience that goes into a thing slowly and stays. Its risk is that going in is all it does, until the entering has no aim and the root spreads with nothing above it to feed.

### Reading
You are in the long middle of something, past its beginning and well short of its end. This is the sequel to being drawn together: the two who met are a household now, and the question has changed from how to begin to how to go on. The sky over the earth goes on, the sun goes on, the seasons turn and go on, with none of them standing still to do it. They last by changing inside a shape that does not change.

What it asks is that you stand firm and do not change your direction, while everything inside you keeps moving. Outwardly, conviction. Inwardly, a change that never stops. The way to endure is to keep changing, and the way to fail is to grip. You may be carrying more than you should have been asked to carry; endure it with dignity, and if good fortune comes, carry that with humility. You cannot force anyone else to change with you, only yourself. Keep near the ones least like you, because a thing that lasts is made of parts that are not the same.

In the body this energy is the legs, the feet that move you and the thighs that carry the weight. Notice how you are standing now: rooted, or shifting, or braced against the ground. Stand as a tree stands, and let the top of you move. It furthers to have somewhere to go.

### Judgement
- Lasting goes through, and there is no fault in it.

## KEYS

_Shadow:_ Failure · _Gift:_ Preservation · _Siddhi:_ Veneration

### Shadow — Failure
Failing has one meaning in the body, and it is being cut off. Long before you, the ones you come from learned that to be put out of the group was to die, and that lesson is still in you, older than any exam or business you ever failed at. In a modern life it has moved into money: the larger the number, the further from failure you feel, yet it never settles, because fear always needs something to feed on, and if money went it would find the next thing. Its close cousin is the fear that your line dies out, with no one to hand anything to.

The fear made people stingy in spirit, keeping to their own small circle, and it kept the species alive while it did. It is rooted deep in the body, and it is the mind that feeds on it, telling the fear's story over again. When you let go into the pattern life moves in, the support was there all along, and what you truly need comes when it is needed.

### Repressive nature — Fundamentalist
This person contracts. The circle is drawn tight, the rules held so hard they cut, and everything outside the circle is suspect. They are choking themselves, in body as in money, starved of breath and of help. Groups made this way stop meeting the wider world, and in time they die out.

### Reactive nature — Disjointed
Here the thread has been dropped. Underneath is anger, the anger that nothing holds you up but yourself, and it drives one bad decision after another, each cutting a little further from the source. The days stop belonging to each other. It can look like freedom next to the tight ones. Nothing in it lasts.

### Gift — Preservation
Keeping things alive is what this energy becomes, and its whole art is knowing what to keep. You begin to sense the long arc of a thing, whether it has a future in it, before you could say why. You can hold your energy back from what tempts but will not serve, and trust a feeling about someone that no reason supports. The balance is between restraint and risk: what to keep as it is, what to change. The one who buried what he was given, for fear of losing it, lost it.

The craft is grafting. You keep the old root, the part that has proved itself strong over generations, and you set the young shoot onto it. Nature is the old root, you the young shoot, which is why the old ways draw you. Then you see that what you were fighting to preserve was never the real thing, and the shell of it cracks. A man once planted trees every day in widening circles around his home, through wars and governments, most of them dying, until a forest stood where there had been bare ground, with villages living off it. Nobody knew who he was. He knew.

### Siddhi — Veneration
Reverence is the state of seeing your place in the chain of living things, and it is what remains when the fear has burned all the way out. Below you are lives less far along, above you lives you cannot follow yet, and you look at your own place with no self in the looking, so all that is left is wonder. You stand on the shoulders of the ones who came before you, and you give your shoulders to whoever comes next. Even the smallest insect is holding you up. You feel tiny and immense in one breath, a single thread from the past to what is still to come. It gives off something, a scent caught on someone in whom wisdom has taken up residence. Wisdom lives in a host, passes through, and moves on to the next one. The one discipline it asks is to pause, and listen inward.

## DESIGN

### The drive — Gate 32, Endurance
A sense of what will last is the pressure in you, and Gate 32 is where it works. It reads a thing, a plan or a bond, for whether it has a future, before you could say what you saw, and it tells you which chances to take for the long run. Growth under this pressure comes in cycles rather than leaps; real change takes time, and you know it in your body. The drive is to prepare, to set the stage, to be ready when the time comes without dragging it forward.

From outside it can look like caution, or like clinging. The cost is the fear of failing, which can stop you from even trying, or from preparing, so that when the time finally comes you are not ready for it, or it can push you the other way, too hard for too long, against a timing you cannot command. People who carry this all their lives learn the difference between holding on and clinging, learn when to let go, learn that patience was their power all along.

### Where it lives — the Spleen Center
Under the ribs on the left side is the place this pressure works from, the Spleen Centre, the body's awareness of what is safe and what is not, older than thought. It is the centre of time: it meets each moment fresh, adjusting in the instant to keep you well. It is also the centre of the survival fears, and the one that belongs to Gate 32 is the fear of failing, which says I am not going to try. The sense of what will last works from the same place that fears loss, which is the whole teaching: the instinct and the fear come up one channel.

For the ones in whom this centre is switched on, a little over half, the sense of timing is their own; they can act in the moment, on a hunch, and be right, with a body that stays well while they trust it. The risk is the mind talking over the hunch, asking for reasons, and the hunch does not repeat itself. For the ones in whom it is not, time slips or presses, so they are always late or always early, while the fears in a room feel like their own. What they have instead is a fine sense of what is healthy for someone else. For them the moment to act is not to be trusted; the slow decision is.

### What completes it — the Channel of Transformation (32–54)
The sense of what will last is a reading with no force behind it: it can see the long arc and cannot climb it. Its other half is Gate 54, at the base of the body, the ambition that pushes a life upward from where it started. The two make the Channel of Transformation, which knows which big dreams to follow and has the drive to bring them to fruition, whether in work or in waking up. Whether the other half sits in you or in someone near you, the dream only moves where the two meet.

## BODY

### Physiology
The spleen is where this energy is kept, and the reason is keeping. What passes through it is sorted: the worn out retired, what still serves kept for use. Every red cell in your blood lives about four months, and this is where most of them end, taken apart; the iron that reddened them is kept, then built into the next cell. Your body loses almost none of its iron. What reddens your blood today was carried by cells that are gone, and by cells before them, so the red in you has been yours for years. In the old medicine the spleen keeps from the other side too: it holds the blood inside its channels, moves the water through you, and turns what you eat into what you live on.

It sits behind the stomach, high under the left ribs, about the size of your fist. Look at the pink under your fingernails: that colour is blood, and the iron that reddens it has been kept for you by what sits under your ribs, cell after cell, for years.

### Amino acid
Lasting has a chemistry in you, and the substance is asparaginic acid. It is built into the letters your body is written in, part of what each of them is made from. Joined to one other piece it is the sweetener in a diet drink, printed on the can. What is yours in it is the fear of dying with your life unfinished, the fear that ties death to money in a body that keeps and counts; that fear is chemistry too, older than any account.

The same substance is the old root and the young shoot: what has held for generations, with the new set onto it so it grows. In your body the keeping with the grafting is one chemistry, shared with one other energy, the one that fears the ending itself.
