# THINKING — Mandala Codes

The durable log of the long direction conversations on this project: what was discussed, what
was decided, what was rejected and why, and what was left open. Loaded by every chat in this
project so a later session cannot re-propose a path that was already argued down. Append only;
a later conversation that changes an answer writes a new entry referencing the old one.

---

## 2026-09-22 — How the oracle cards should be written, settled over two days and one card

**Topic:** Adrian was about to print the whole deck as a book and stopped: the summary line at
the top of each card did not make sense to him. What began as a fix to one sentence became two
days of correction, first on that summary, then on the card prose underneath it, then on the
seven phrases that open every card. The question at stake was not the wording of any one line.
It was whether a deck written mostly by agents could be made to sound like a person speaking,
and whether the rules for that could live somewhere other than Adrian's head.

**Convergent answer:** The first rule, above every other, is that it sounds like someone
speaking to you: every sentence is one a person would say aloud across a table, and a word is
never banned, it is used the way a person would use it or not at all. Under that sit three
directives: embodied (the energy lived well, never the lack or the struggle as the subject),
relatable, and proper English. The deeper realisation came late, from Fable, and it explains
every correction Adrian made: the writer kept describing the thing and then attaching the
reader to it, so the depth lived in the description and the relation lived in the attachment,
and every cut to one side took the other with it. There is no seam when the force is the
reader from the first word. Three habits produce the fault in practice, and they are now the
things a writer checks for: an abstract noun doing the verb, a mirrored second half, and a
picture leaned on after it has left the room. The rules are no longer in Adrian's head; they
are in the writers' brief, and one card was written from the brief alone to prove it.

**Rejected paths, with reasons:**

- **The summary as a story or a scene** ("Look back and your life is a row of first days", "A
  hen on her eggs"). Adrian: "You shouldn't be telling a story, this is talking specifically
  about the energy." Forty-four of sixty-four came back this way when the rule told writers to
  come in through a moment; the rule caused it.
- **The summary as the energy's law, compressed** (the energy as the subject of both sentences,
  its behaviour chained in clauses). Produced prose that had to be decoded, not felt, and a new
  formula: "X is yours, Y is not" closing fourteen cards.
- **The summary as an identity claim** ("You are the one who starts things"). Adrian: most
  people carry several energies, and the card describes what is moving now, not who you are.
- **The summary as a dictionary definition** ("Beginning is the moment a new thing starts").
  Correct and unreadable: the reader has to attach it to themselves.
- **Explaining the systems on every card.** Adrian: that belongs in an introduction or a note
  the reader can close, not on all sixty-four.
- **Describing how a hexagram is drawn.** The picture is printed beside the text; the words
  interpret it or say nothing.
- **Any origin story or named authority** ("King Wen named it", "the old readers saw a mouth").
  The signs are older than anyone who read them and have been read many ways. Adrian: "Don't
  make shit up. It will make me lose credibility."
- **Stating a translated word as fact.** The sign's name has been rendered as the jaws, the
  corners of the mouth and nourishment; the card gives readings as readings or leaves the word
  out.
- **A translator's vocabulary carrying a sentence.** "Gladness" ran through one card eleven
  times because a translator used it; nobody says it at a table.
- **Writing up to a word budget.** Every size in the brief is now a ceiling with no floor:
  "If there is only enough for a certain amount, do not draw it out with extra words."
- **Turning the reviewer's judgement into machine checks.** Tried and measured: the habit
  patterns fire twice on sixty-four cards, so they were not adopted as gates. The judgement
  did not survive automation; the cheaper reader did.

**The honest tensions left unresolved:**

- Three of Adrian's approved phrases on one card already appear on four other cards. Whether a
  phrase may belong to two cards is undecided; the answer changes how the remaining cards are
  written.
- The hidden notes sheet on every rewritten card still describes the page as it was. The record
  that exists to stop re-argument now misleads.
- Fable was cut from the per-card loop to save tokens, and replaced by a cheap reader plus a
  fact diff. One card has been written that way and it held, which is one card of evidence, not
  a proven claim.
- The cold reader must be given a primer or it fails every system word; one card's reader ran
  without it and returned sixteen failures of which fourteen were false. The instrument is only
  as good as its briefing, and that briefing is now a step someone could forget again.
- Nobody has read the deck end to end in the new voice. Eight cards is not a book.

**Concrete next moves, in order:**

1. ~~Write the rules down where the writers read them, not in the chat~~
2. ~~Prove the brief by writing one card from it alone, with no line from Adrian~~
3. ~~Cut the expensive reviewer from the loop and replace it with a fact diff~~
4. ~~Lock the seven opening phrases: three words, understandable alone, at least four positive~~
5. Carry the rewrite through the other fifty-six cards, six at a time
6. Decide whether an opening phrase may appear on two cards
7. Correct the hidden notes sheet on every rewritten card
8. Re-render the printed book and read it as a book, not as cards

**What this entry exists to prevent:** Future Claude reading this file: do not re-propose the
summary as a scene, a law, an identity claim or a definition, do not explain the systems on a
card, do not describe how a hexagram is drawn, and never write an origin story or name a
translator as an authority. All five were tried here and rejected with reasons. Read the
writers' brief before writing a word of card prose.

## 2026-09-24 — The deck written end to end, and what the reading is for

**Topic:** Move 5 of the 2026-09-22 entry, carried out. Cards 63 and 64 were rebuilt by hand
under the six-step pass; the method was then run on all 64 by one headless writer per card,
seven at a time, and every card went live on 2026-09-24 with status still `scaffold`. On the
way Adrian corrected four things that change how any card is written.

**Convergent answer:**
- **The card's name is Adrian's artwork title, never the hexagram's.** The I Ching feeds a
  card like the Gene Keys, Human Design and the body; it never frames it. Adrian: "The name of
  the card is what I call the universal language. The title of the artwork. This is my
  system." The title goes in only where it fits.
- **The reading is the gift lived, not the problem.** Order: the gift alive in you; the hard
  stretch, briefly; how to step into the gift, ending on it. Written fresh from the source
  files, never by reworking earlier wording. Card 6 is the model.
- **Every sentence on the card is read cold**, not only the reading
  (`scripts/cold-read.mjs`). The mechanical sentence check cannot see a paragraph that says
  nothing; the cold read caught card 1's dragon-and-character Combination at once.
- **A change to how cards are written runs on one card first.** "We just run one. If it's good
  we run it on the rest."

**Rejected paths, with reasons:**
- **Opening on the hexagram's name** ("Small taming is a time when"). "Specific to I Ching and
  it's not relatable at all." CARD-PASS had said "in the hexagram's own plain words", which
  worked by accident on 63 and 64, whose names are everyday English.
- **Readings led by a lack or conflict** ("already on guard", "has not arrived yet"): six of
  eight openings did it once the rule said "a situation from your day". It breaks the first
  directive, embodied.
- **Fixing a reading by reworking its sentences.** "Don't just try to rework the words that
  are already in the three paragraphs. What about the source files."
- **Running a change on every card at once.** "If they're off we have to keep on running all
  of them."
- **Writing the whole card from a hard artwork title.** "Otherwise we're gonna get some weird
  results."

**The honest tensions left unresolved:**
- All 64 are live and none carries Adrian's `final`; he chose to read them on the live site.
- The cold read over-flags a little and a second run flags different lines; it is a guide
  the writer triages, not a verdict.
- The tools that wrote the deck lived in a temp folder and were lost once; they are now in
  `scripts/cold-read.mjs`, `scripts/card-queue.mjs` and
  `todo/plans/writing-guideline/card-writer-brief.md`.

**Concrete next moves, in order** (moves 1 to 5 of the 2026-09-22 entry are done):
1. Adrian reads the live cards and marks the ones that hold `final`
2. Decide whether an opening phrase may appear on two cards
3. Check each card's hidden notes sheet against its page
4. Re-render the printed book and read it as a book, not as cards

**What this entry exists to prevent:** do not open a reading on a hexagram name, do not lead
a reading with a lack, do not rewrite a reading from its own old sentences, and do not run a
writing change on all 64 before Adrian has read one.

## 2026-09-26 — The deck finished, and the book made ready for the printer

**Topic:** The loose ends of the 2026-09-24 entry, closed in one day: the deck's status, the
hidden notes, the shared keynote question, and the printed book.

**Convergent answer:**
- **All 64 cards are `final`.** Adrian, after the printed book was built: "mark them all
  final". Every section of every card; the progress file reads 64 finished. Two unit tests
  that assumed an unfinished card existed now read `final` and check the same things.
- **The descriptive hidden notes are gone.** meta.scene, meta.mechanism and
  meta.side_by_side went stale with every rewrite, repeated the worksheet, and nothing read
  them; 177 were removed. The entrance (meta.centre), sources, lore and fact check stay,
  because the page needs the first and the others back the "don't make things up" rule.
  The card data regenerated byte-identical.
- **A keynote phrase may appear on two cards, not three.** "Allow the phrase on two cards."
  Recorded in WRITERS-BRIEF. The one phrase on three, "Knowing When to Stop" (27, 40, 56),
  left card 56, whose keynote is now "Letting It Settle", taken from its own reading.
- **The printed book.** Each card's pen box is the tallest that keeps the card on its
  pages, so the writing spreads evenly and the last page is ruled from the end of the text
  ("a lot of white space on the last one and not as much room on the first and second").
  A cover and a back page, each with a blank inside so card 1 stays on a right-hand page;
  no contents page. `npm run workbook -- --book` writes a print pack to `workbook/print/`:
  the inside pages, front and back cover as separate files, a complete file for proofing,
  and one page of printing instructions. Cover words live in `oracle/book/cover.json`;
  Adrian's own designs dropped in as `oracle/book/front.pdf` or `back.pdf` replace them.

**Rejected paths, with reasons:**
- **Checking every hidden notes sheet against its page.** It would have to be redone after
  every rewrite; deleting the notes that go stale removes the work for good.
- **A contents page.** "I don't need a contents page."
- **Unlimited sharing of a keynote phrase.** Two cards, not three.

**The honest tensions left unresolved:**
- `final` was given without anyone reading the 64 end to end; the printed book is where
  that reading happens.
- The book's ruled lines are 0.3 pt light grey and some printers drop them; a proof sheet
  first is in the printer's instructions.
- The 18 mm bound margin suits wire, spiral or ring binding; a glued spine at 96 sheets is
  unconfirmed.

**Concrete next moves, in order:**
1. A proof sheet from the printer
2. Adrian's own cover and back designs, if he makes them, then a rebuild of the pack
3. Adrian's pen pass on the printed book, and any corrections carried back to the cards

**What this entry exists to prevent:** do not restore the descriptive hidden notes, do not
let a keynote phrase onto a third card, and do not change a `final` card's words without
Adrian's say.

## 2026-09-26 — The home page is an invocation, and the grid stays the grid

**Topic:** A design critique of the whole site set three priorities: readable text on the
card page, consistency across pages, and a home page whose three actions (birth time, today
and this year, Draw a card) competed at one weight. The first two shipped as mechanical
passes. The home page took three rounds, and the corrections in them say what the page is.

**Convergent answer:** The home page is an invocation with intention. Its three verses are
the heart of the project, the first thing a stranger meets, and the reason the rest exists;
restraint is applied around them, never to them. The hexagram grid is the deck's face: the
64 symbols read at any size and together look ancient and connected, which the artwork
cannot do small. What shipped (option B): the verses and the three ways in share one column,
the deck fills the field beside it eight across, today and this year are tiles you can open
and are lit where they sit in the deck, and birth time is a button of its own. The deeper
point: "clean it up" means clearing the clutter around the heart, not distilling the page
to one click.

**Rejected paths, with reasons:**
- **Artwork thumbnails on the deck grid.** "Art small doesn't look good and you can't see
  anything. I Ching symbols read perfectly that small and then it looks like something
  ancient and connected."
- **Reducing the page to one action** (the first round: grid-first, threshold-then-grid,
  today's card leading). It cut the invocation to satisfy a hierarchy rule. "You distilled
  things down in a way where people don't understand the heart of this project."
- **The verses below the grid or in a footer.** The heart is not a closing line.
- **Six across in the deck.** Rows must end even: four on a phone, eight on desktop.
- **Today, this year and birth time as quiet text lines.** "They don't really feel like
  something I wanna click." Every way in must look like one.
- **Regenerating the page from its design source.** The source has drifted 224 lines from
  the live file; the layout lives in the page's own stylesheet instead.

**The honest tensions left unresolved:**
- Option C's pause, a line inviting the visitor to hold a question before drawing, was
  proposed and not answered. The words would have to be Adrian's.
- The column is not pinned while the deck scrolls; at desktop sizes both are about one
  screen tall, so it was left unpinned. On taller decks or shorter screens that may matter.
- Deck rows are taller than in the approved mock because each tile flips to show its art.
- The sign-in button stays lowercase ("this is a gallery"), and the phone's Relations tab
  keeps its symbol; both were left as recorded choices, not revisited.

**Concrete next moves, in order:**
1. ~~Card page text held to 12px and readable contrast~~
2. ~~One menu label, one title pattern, no bar icons, no italics on Learn~~
3. ~~The invocation column beside the sixty-four~~
4. Decide whether the hold-a-question pause belongs on the page, in Adrian's words
5. Reconcile the home page's design source with the live file before any re-import

**What this entry exists to prevent:** Future Claude reading this file: do not put artwork
on the deck grid, do not move or shrink the verses to simplify the page, and do not re-run
the design converter on the home page. Name what a stranger should feel first, then tidy.
